Thank you, Dan. "Welcome again everyone! In this section, we’ll explore the key elements that make a strong social enterprise, and how they relate to UnLtd’s support and funding opportunities.
We’ll cover three core areas: Social Impact, Financial Sustainability, and Inclusion. Along the way, we’ll share real examples from ventures we’re supporting.

### Social Impact

Financial Sustainability

Inclusion

To be considered a social enterprise, there are two essential elements:
Social Impact – making a positive difference in people’s lives or communities.
Financial Sustainability – generating income through the sale of products or services to support and grow your work.
For UnLtd funding, we also look for a third key element:
Inclusion – ensuring your venture is shaped by lived experience and focused on communities with the greatest need.

### Social Impact

The ‘social’ element of a social enterprise – is the driving force of the venture. It describes a business that is tackling a social issue

Social impact can be defined as the effect on people and communities that happens as a result of an action, an activity, project, programme or policy.

I’m going to start by talking about Social Impact.
It’s defined as the effect on people and communities that happens as a result of an action or policy.
In other words, it’s the real-world change your venture creates.
Whether it’s improving health outcomes, reducing barriers to employment, or systemic change — your impact is what makes your work meaningful.

### How

Theory of Change

Measure Impact

When defining your social impact, focus on five key questions:
What social issue are you addressing?
Who benefits from your work?
How do they benefit?
How many people benefit — this is scale of impact.
And how much they benefit — this is depth of impact.
For example, a wellness app might reach thousands — high scale and lighter impact. In contrast, working closely with a few families over time may offer deeper change.
Think of ways you can measure your impact through outcomes and by gathering feedback and testimonials. They are powerful tools for learning, improving, and showing funders the real difference you’re making.“
Finally, a Theory of Change is a structured framework that explains how and why a social enterprise expects to create impact. It maps the logical pathway from activities to long-term social outcomes.

Let’s take a deeper look at what a Theory of Change can include. 
The Theory of Change is important for you as an applicant because our application questions are based on its principles. We’ll ask about the social issue you aim to address, your approach to tackling it, the impact you hope to achieve, and how you plan to generate income to sustain your work. This doesn’t mean you need to have a fully developed Theory of Change to apply, but it’s useful to be aware of the main principles.

The example we have used is a CIC improving employment outcomes for young people facing barriers. 
No 1 your mission - state the ultimate social or environmental change you aim to achieve.Example: “Our goal is to reduce youth unemployment in disadvantaged communities.”
No 2 – your Target population - Who will benefit from your activities?Example: “Young people aged 16–24 facing barriers to employment.”
No 3 – the Social Issue and need- What issue are you addressing and why does it matter?Example: High unemployment rates lead to poverty and social exclusion.
No 4 – your pathway of change - How will your activities lead to impact?Example: Deliver training, mentorship, and job placement leading to improved skills and sustainable employment.
No 5 – the Assumptions - What conditions must hold true for success?Example: Employers are willing to hire trained individuals; participants remain engaged.
No 6 – your indicators of success - How will you measure progress?Example: a % of participants employed within 6 months; retention rates after 12 months.
And lastly No 7 – the mission alignment - How does this support your social enterprise model?Example: We generate revenue by charging employers a recruitment fee, which funds our training programs, ensuring sustainability & impact.

### Financial Sustainability

How does my venture continue helping people and grow?

Now let’s look at another key element — Financial Sustainability.
This means your venture can keep delivering social impact without relying only on grants or donations.
To be eligible for UnLtd funding, your idea needs a revenue-generating business model, not a traditional charity model.
Charities often depend heavily on external funding, while social enterprises aim to generate income through their activities — helping them grow and sustain their impact over time.

### What should I consider before starting a business? (Business Plan)

What should I consider before starting a business? (Business Plan)

Whether you're just starting out or already trading, having a business plan is essential. You don’t need to have produced a Business plan before applying to us, but being aware of the principles is beneficial.
In the same way as developing a Theory of Change helps you to evidence your plans for impact, a strong business plan will help you to evidence how you will make a profit to keep delivering the impact. 
A business plan helps you to attract investors and will also help you with funding applications. For example, when we ask, 'How do you generate income to keep your social venture going?' — we’re referring to your business model.
That includes:
Who your customers are,
What you’re selling,
How you know there’s demand,
How you’ll cover your costs,
How you will generate a profit 
A good plan shows that you’ve researched your market, understand your customers, and know what it takes to be financially viable and grow.

### Our assessment system for applications has been designed through an equity lens to address the influence of societal disadvantages, which imbalance the application process.

Equality means each individual or group of people is given the same resources or opportunities.

Equity recognizes that each person has different circumstances and allocates the exact resources and opportunities needed to reach an equal outcome for all.

Inclusion

Now let’s talk about our third key element — Inclusion.
Tackling social injustice, inequality, and the climate crisis are some of the biggest challenges of our time — and social entrepreneurs are leading the way.
Many are creating meaningful jobs, supporting people’s health and independence, and making a difference in under-served communities.
At UnLtd, we value lived experience. We want to understand how your background, identity, or culture has shaped your drive to create change.
Our assessment looks at:
Your lived experience,
The diversity of the communities you support,
And how your venture addresses inequality.
We prioritise ventures working with communities facing the greatest need — because that’s where impact matters most.

### Common queries & answers

Incorporated longer than 1 year but trading less than a year

Starting Up award is applicable

Incorporated for more than 4 years

Not eligible

Changed legal structure

Any change is counted a period of continuous operation

So for the first query, the Starting Up award is applicable because how long you’ve been trading is the relevant factor.  

If you’ve been incorporated for longer than 4 years you’re not eligible and this includes any changes in legal structure as this counts as a period of continuous operation.  

So to be clear, if you’ve gone through any legal changes we go by the original incorporation date. If your venture is within the 4 year timeframe then it can be eligible.

Let’s take a deeper look at what a Theory of Change can include. 
The Theory of Change is important for you as an applicant because our application questions are based on its principles. We’ll ask about the social issue you aim to address, your approach to tackling it, the impact you hope to achieve, and how you plan to generate income to sustain your work. This doesn’t mean you need to have a fully developed Theory of Change to apply, but it’s useful to be aware of the main principles.

The example we have used is a CIC improving employment outcomes for young people facing barriers. 
No 1 your mission - state the ultimate social or environmental change you aim to achieve.Example: “Our goal is to reduce youth unemployment in disadvantaged communities.”
No 2 – your Target population - Who will benefit from your activities?Example: “Young people aged 16–24 facing barriers to employment.”
No 3 – the Social Issue and need- What issue are you addressing and why does it matter?Example: High unemployment rates lead to poverty and social exclusion.
No 4 – your pathway of change - How will your activities lead to impact?Example: Deliver training, mentorship, and job placement leading to improved skills and sustainable employment.
No 5 – the Assumptions - What conditions must hold true for success?Example: Employers are willing to hire trained individuals; participants remain engaged.
No 6 – your indicators of success - How will you measure progress?Example: a % of participants employed within 6 months; retention rates after 12 months.
And lastly No 7 – the mission alignment - How does this support your social enterprise model?Example: We generate revenue by charging employers a recruitment fee, which funds our training programs, ensuring sustainability & impact.

### Financial Sustainability

This means your venture can keep delivering social impact without relying only on grants or donations.
To be eligible for UnLtd funding, your idea needs a revenue-generating business model, not a traditional charity model.
Charities often depend heavily on external funding, while social enterprises aim to generate income through their activities — helping them grow and sustain their impact over time.

### What should I consider before starting a business? (Business Plan)

What should I consider before starting a business? (Business Plan)

Whether you're just starting out or already trading, having a business plan is essential. You don’t need to have produced a Business plan before applying to us, but being aware of the principles is beneficial.
In the same way as developing a Theory of Change helps you to evidence your plans for impact, a strong business plan will help you to evidence how you will make a profit to keep delivering the impact. 
A business plan helps you to attract investors and will also help you with funding applications. For example, when we ask, 'How do you generate income to keep your social venture going?' — we’re referring to your business model.
That includes:
Who your customers are,
What you’re selling,
How you know there’s demand,
How you’ll cover your costs,
How you will generate a profit 
A good plan shows that you’ve researched your market, understand your customers, and know what it takes to be financially viable and grow.

Starting Up award is applicable

Any change is counted a period of continuous operation

So for the first query, the Starting Up award is applicable because how long you’ve been trading is the relevant factor.  

If you’ve been incorporated for longer than 4 years you’re not eligible and this includes any changes in legal structure as this counts as a period of continuous operation.  

So to be clear, if you’ve gone through any legal changes we go by the original incorporation date. If your venture is within the 4 year timeframe then it can be eligible.
