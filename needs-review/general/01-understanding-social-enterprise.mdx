## What Is a Social Enterprise?

Social entrepreneurs put social purpose at the heart of what they do, ahead of financial gain. Their enterprises improve the world through what they do, the people they employ, or how they use any profits.

Unlike traditional businesses that prioritise shareholder returns, social enterprises are driven by a mission to create positive change. This does not mean they avoid generating revenue — in fact, commercial sustainability is essential — but profit serves as a means to achieve social outcomes rather than an end in itself.

## Models of Social Enterprise

Social enterprises generally fall into three broad categories based on how they create impact:

### Products

These enterprises provide goods that improve the lives of individuals, communities, and businesses. The impact is embedded in the product itself — whether it is an assistive device for people with disabilities, an eco-friendly alternative to a common household item, or a fair-trade product that supports producers in developing countries.

### Services

Service-based social enterprises run activities and provide services that have a positive benefit. This includes everything from education and training programmes to healthcare services, community development initiatives, and professional consultancy with a social mission.

### Employment

Some social enterprises create impact primarily through their hiring practices, helping people who face barriers to work enter employment. This model is common in sectors like catering, retail, and manufacturing, where the enterprise provides jobs, skills training, and a supportive working environment for people who might otherwise be excluded from the labour market.

## Balancing Business with Impact

One of the central challenges you will face as a social entrepreneur is balancing commercial viability with social impact. A venture that creates enormous social value but cannot sustain itself financially will eventually fail. Conversely, a venture that is commercially successful but drifts from its social mission has lost its purpose.

Getting this balance right means:

- **Defining your theory of change** — be clear about how your activities lead to the social outcomes you are targeting
- **Evidencing your impact** — develop metrics and measurement approaches that demonstrate the difference you are making
- **Building a sustainable business model** — ensure your revenue streams can support ongoing operations without constant reliance on grants
- **Staying mission-focused** — as you grow and face commercial pressures, maintain clarity about why your enterprise exists

## Key Areas of Support for Scaling

If you are looking to scale your social enterprise, there are several areas where targeted support can make a significant difference:

| Area | What It Covers |
|------|---------------|
| **Business planning and strategy** | Financial modelling, strategic advice, and long-term planning |
| **Routes to market** | Understanding your offer, identifying customers, and developing sales channels |
| **Investment readiness** | Preparing to raise capital, understanding investor expectations, and structuring deals |
| **Impact evidence** | Refining your theory of change and building robust impact measurement |
| **Team and governance** | Developing your leadership team and establishing effective organisational governance |
| **Peer collaboration** | Learning from other social entrepreneurs who have faced similar challenges |

## Promoting Peer Collaboration

Peer learning is one of the most valuable resources available to social entrepreneurs. Connecting with others who are at a similar stage, or who have already navigated the challenges you face, can provide practical insights that no textbook or adviser can offer. Look for cohort programmes, networks, and events that bring social entrepreneurs together to share experiences and build relationships.
