## What Are Social Impact Bonds?

A Social Impact Bond (SIB) is an outcomes-based contract in which investors fund social services upfront and are repaid by a government commissioner only if the agreed social outcomes are achieved. SIBs are designed to align the interests of service providers, investors, and commissioners around measurable social impact.

The typical structure works as follows:

1. **A social issue is identified** — a government commissioner defines a social problem and the outcomes they want to achieve
2. **Investors provide upfront funding** — social investors fund the delivery of services designed to achieve those outcomes
3. **A service provider delivers the intervention** — an organisation (often a charity or social enterprise) delivers the programme
4. **Outcomes are measured** — an independent evaluator assesses whether the agreed outcomes have been achieved
5. **Repayment is made if outcomes are met** — the commissioner repays investors, often with a return, if the target outcomes are achieved

## The Life Chances Fund

The Life Chances Fund was a UK government programme designed to support the development of Social Impact Bonds. It provided top-up funding for SIB proposals' outcome payments, helping to make outcomes-based commissioning viable in areas where it might not otherwise have been financially feasible.

The fund was managed in partnership with the Big Lottery Fund (now The National Lottery Community Fund) and focused on encouraging cross-sector collaboration between commissioners, service providers, and social investors.

### What the Life Chances Fund Supported

The programme aimed to:

- Encourage local authorities and other commissioners to explore outcomes-based approaches
- Provide additional funding to make SIBs viable where outcome payments alone would not cover costs
- Support innovation in service delivery for disadvantaged groups
- Generate learning about what works in outcomes-based commissioning

## The Government Outcomes Lab

The Government Outcomes Lab (GO Lab), based at the University of Oxford's Blavatnik School of Government, is a research centre focused on understanding how government can improve social outcomes through collaboration with the private and social sectors. The GO Lab publishes press summaries of awards, resources, and learning events related to Social Impact Bonds.

## Is an Outcomes-Based Approach Right for You?

If you are considering whether an outcomes-based funding model might work for your organisation, consider the following:

### When SIBs May Be Appropriate

- You are delivering a service where outcomes can be clearly defined and measured
- There is a commissioner willing to pay for outcomes rather than outputs
- Your intervention has an evidence base (or strong theory of change) supporting its effectiveness
- You need upfront capital to deliver services before outcomes can be demonstrated

### When SIBs May Not Be the Best Fit

- Your outcomes are difficult to measure or take a very long time to materialise
- The administrative and legal costs of setting up the SIB structure outweigh the benefits
- You work in an area where the causal link between your intervention and the desired outcome is unclear
- You are a small organisation without the capacity to manage the reporting requirements

## Key Considerations for Commissioners

If you are a commissioner considering an outcomes-based approach, keep these points in mind:

- **Build partnerships locally** — successful SIBs depend on strong relationships between commissioners, service providers, and investors
- **Learn from existing examples** — the Government Outcomes Lab and other bodies publish evaluation reports and case studies that can inform your approach
- **Be realistic about timescales** — developing and launching a SIB takes significant time and resources
- **Consider the full cost** — include the costs of evaluation, contract management, and investor returns in your planning
