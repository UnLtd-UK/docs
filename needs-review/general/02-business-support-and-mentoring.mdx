## Why Mentoring Matters

As a social entrepreneur, you do not need to figure everything out on your own. Experienced mentors can provide guidance on specific challenges, open doors to new networks, and help you avoid common pitfalls. The right mentor brings a combination of relevant expertise, genuine commitment, and the ability to offer an external perspective on your venture.

## Types of Mentoring Support

Mentors bring different specialisms. Understanding what you need will help you find the right match.

### Marketing

Marketing mentors can help you with sales and marketing strategy, shopper marketing, customer acquisition, and brand positioning. This is particularly valuable if you are selling consumer products and need to understand how to reach your target audience effectively.

### General Business Mentoring

General business mentors draw on broad experience running organisations. They can advise on everything from HR and budgets to fundraising and stakeholder management. This type of mentoring is especially useful if you are navigating the day-to-day realities of growing a business.

### Working with Corporates

If your growth strategy involves partnering with or selling to large organisations, a mentor with corporate experience can help you understand how to pitch your proposition, navigate procurement processes, and build effective relationships with corporate partners.

### Board-Level Strategy

For more mature ventures, mentors with board-level experience can advise on overall strategic direction, governance, senior management relations, and preparing for investment. This includes expertise in areas like private equity, business turnaround, and scaling operations.

### Innovation and Performance Evaluation

Mentors with experience in social innovation can help you design and launch new initiatives, evaluate your performance, and develop user-led propositions. This is particularly relevant if you work in the health or education sectors.

## Additional Business Support

Beyond one-to-one mentoring, there are several other forms of support you can access:

### Crowdfunding

If you are looking to raise funds or build public support, crowdfunding can be a powerful tool. Specialists in social-good crowdfunding can support organisations looking to raise from £15,000 to £50,000 or more through the campaign process.

### Marketing Consultancy

Some agencies offer free or pro bono marketing consultancy to organisations with clear social values and aims. This typically includes a set number of hours to work through your marketing challenges and develop a plan.

### PR and Corporate Communications

If you need help with media relations, crisis communications, public affairs, internal communications, social media, or corporate communications, volunteering networks can match you with experienced professionals who donate their time.

### Business Consultancy

Management consultancies sometimes offer hybrid support combining coaching, mentoring, and hands-on pro bono work. This can include:

- Assessing your business technology needs
- Reviewing your business plan and sharing implementation best practices
- Customer lifecycle management and value proposition review
- Defining sales channels and distribution models
- Coaching on project management and efficient business initiatives
- Reviewing finance function processes and team capabilities
- Advising on cash-flow forecasting models
- Coaching on working capital optimisation

### CRM and Salesforce Support

If you are considering using a customer relationship management (CRM) system or are already using Salesforce, there are programmes that provide free, practical support for social enterprises.

### Project Management Training

Training programmes covering all aspects of effective project management — from project initiation and stakeholder management through to risk management and project closure — can help you deliver your work more effectively.

### Legal Support

Legal support for social enterprises typically covers areas such as:

- Data protection and GDPR compliance
- Governance and reporting obligations
- Contract management
- Employment law and workplace policies
- Trustee recruitment and retention

Look for free webinar series and pro bono legal advice offered by law firms that support the social enterprise sector.

## How to Access Support

When seeking support, consider the following steps:

1. **Identify your need** — be specific about the challenge you are facing and the type of help that would be most useful
2. **Research available programmes** — many support programmes are offered through social enterprise networks, foundations, and corporate partners
3. **Prepare your request** — when approaching a mentor or support provider, clearly articulate your situation, what you have tried, and what outcome you are hoping for
