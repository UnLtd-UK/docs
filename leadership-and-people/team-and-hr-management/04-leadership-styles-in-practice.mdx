## Why This Matters

There is no single "correct" way to lead. The most effective leaders draw on a range of styles, adapting their approach depending on the situation, the team, and the challenge at hand. Understanding these styles gives you a toolkit you can use deliberately, rather than relying on whatever comes naturally.

As one leadership practitioner put it: "Leadership style is not a single outfit -- it is a repertoire."

## The Six Leadership Styles

The following framework, rooted in emotional intelligence research, identifies six distinct leadership styles. Each has its strengths, its risks, and its ideal context.

![Leadership Styles](/leadership-and-people/assets/leadership-styles.jpg)

| Style | Modus Operandi | In a Phrase | EI Competencies | Works Best When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Directive** (Coercive) | Demands immediate compliance | "Do what I tell you" | Drive to achieve, initiative, self-control | In a crisis, to kick-start a turnaround, or with problem employees |
| **Visionary** (Authoritative) | Mobilises people towards a vision | "Come with me" | Self-confidence, empathy, change catalyst | Changes require a new vision or when a clear direction is needed |
| **Affiliative** | Creates harmony and builds emotional bonds | "People come first" | Empathy, building relationships, communication | Healing rifts in a team or motivating people during stressful times |
| **Participative** (Democratic) | Forges consensus through participation | "What do you think?" | Collaboration, team leadership, communication | Building buy-in or consensus, getting input from valued team members |
| **Pacesetting** | Sets high standards for performance | "Do as I do, now" | Conscientiousness, drive to achieve, initiative | Getting quick results from a highly motivated, competent team |
| **Coaching** | Develops people for the future | "Try this" | Developing others, empathy, self-awareness | Helping someone improve performance or develop long-term strengths |

## Leadership Style as a Repertoire

The most effective leaders do not rely on just one or two styles. They build a repertoire and learn to read situations so they can choose the right approach.

### Common Patterns

- **Founders and entrepreneurs** often default to Directive or Pacesetting styles because they are used to doing things themselves and moving fast
- **Mission-driven leaders** frequently lean toward Visionary and Affiliative styles because they care deeply about purpose and people
- **Under pressure**, most leaders fall back on one dominant style -- usually Directive or Pacesetting -- which can be counterproductive if the situation calls for something different

### The Risk of Over-Reliance

Each style has a shadow side when overused:

- **Too much Directive** creates fear and resentment, stifles initiative
- **Too much Visionary** without follow-through feels like empty rhetoric
- **Too much Affiliative** avoids necessary conflict and tough decisions
- **Too much Participative** leads to endless meetings and indecision
- **Too much Pacesetting** exhausts the team and makes people feel they can never meet expectations
- **Too much Coaching** can feel patronising if people are already competent

## Context Matters

Understanding leadership style theory is helpful, but it is not enough on its own. The key is knowing when to vary your approach. Consider:

- **The urgency of the situation** -- A crisis may demand Directive leadership, while a strategic planning process calls for Participative
- **The maturity of your team** -- New or inexperienced team members may benefit from Coaching, while experienced professionals may thrive with a Visionary or Participative approach
- **The organisational stage** -- Early-stage ventures often need more Pacesetting and Directive energy, while scaling organisations benefit from Visionary and Coaching styles
- **The emotional climate** -- If your team is stressed or demoralised, Affiliative leadership rebuilds trust before you can push forward

## Key Reflection Questions

Take time to honestly assess your leadership style. These questions can help:

1. **Which styles are you most naturally drawn to?** Think about how you typically interact with your team and make decisions.

2. **Which styles do you use least?** These may be the ones that feel uncomfortable -- but they could be exactly what your team needs.

3. **Which style would your team respond best to right now?** Consider what your colleagues need most at this point in the organisation's development.

4. **What styles have you appreciated in others?** Reflect on leaders you have admired and what made their approach effective.

5. **When you are under pressure, which style do you default to?** Awareness of your stress response is the first step to managing it.

6. **How might you improve your use of one or more styles?** Pick one style you want to develop and look for opportunities to practise it.

7. **Across your senior leadership team or board, which styles dominate and which are missing?** Consider the collective leadership profile, not just your own. What are the advantages and gaps?

## Developing Your Repertoire

Building your leadership repertoire is a continuous process. Here are practical steps:

- **Start with self-awareness** -- Know your defaults and your blind spots
- **Observe others** -- Watch how effective leaders you admire adapt their style to different situations
- **Practise deliberately** -- Choose one underdeveloped style and look for low-risk opportunities to try it
- **Seek feedback** -- Ask trusted colleagues how they experience your leadership and what they need more or less of
- **Reflect regularly** -- After important meetings or decisions, consider which style you used and whether it was the most effective choice
- **Consider coaching** -- Working with a coach can accelerate your development, especially in styles that feel uncomfortable

## Balancing Your Leadership Team

If your senior team all share the same dominant style, you will have collective blind spots. Use the six-style framework to audit your leadership team:

- Do you have people who can be Directive when needed?
- Is there someone who naturally takes a Coaching approach?
- Can your team switch between Visionary and Participative modes?
- When the organisation is under stress, does the team have the range to respond appropriately?

A leadership team with a diverse range of styles is more resilient and more effective than one where everyone leads the same way.
