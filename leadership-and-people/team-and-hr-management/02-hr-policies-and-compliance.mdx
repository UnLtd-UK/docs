## Why This Matters

Whether you are about to hire your first employee or already have a team in place, your HR policies and procedures need to be in good health. This guide helps you clarify what is legally required, what is strongly recommended, and what best practice looks like for a growing organisation.

This checklist is aimed at UK-based organisations. If you are based outside the UK, check with an HR expert in your own jurisdiction.

## Written Statement of Employment Particulars

Every employee whose contract lasts at least one month must receive a written statement of employment particulars within two months of starting. This is a legal requirement.

Your written statement must include:

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Employer name | Legal requirement |
| Employee details (name, job title, start date) | Legal requirement |
| End date for fixed-term contracts | Legal requirement |
| Continuous employment date (if applicable) | Legal requirement |
| Hours of work (including Sunday, night, or overtime requirements) | Legal requirement |
| Payment arrangements (how much, how often) | Legal requirement |
| Holiday entitlement (and whether it includes public holidays) | Legal requirement |
| Place of work and any relocation requirements | Legal requirement |
| Multiple work locations and employer address | Legal requirement |
| Notice periods | Legal requirement |
| Collective agreements | Legal requirement |
| Pension arrangements | Legal requirement |
| Grievance contact and procedure | Legal requirement |
| How to appeal a grievance outcome | Legal requirement |
| How to appeal a disciplinary or dismissal decision | Legal requirement |

You must also provide itemised pay statements on or before each payday. This is a legal requirement for all employees.

## Minimum Statutory Pay Requirements

You must meet these minimum pay obligations:

- [ ] **National Minimum Wage (NMW)** -- You are legally required to pay at least the current national minimum wage rate. Check the latest rates at [gov.uk/national-minimum-wage](https://www.gov.uk/national-minimum-wage).
- [ ] **Statutory Sick Pay (SSP)** -- The minimum amount of sick pay you must provide to eligible employees by law.
- [ ] **Statutory Family-Friendly Leave and Pay** -- Eligible employees are legally entitled to maternity, paternity, adoption, and shared parental leave and pay as a minimum.

## Holiday Entitlements

- [ ] **Annual leave** -- All full-time employees are legally entitled to 5.6 weeks (28 days) of paid holiday per year, inclusive of public holidays. Part-time employees receive a pro-rata entitlement.

## Pre-Employment Checks

Before or on your new employee's first day, you must complete:

- [ ] **Right to Work check** -- Obtain, check, and copy the relevant documentation (passport, national identity card, registration certificate, or document certifying permanent residence). Failure to carry out correct checks can lead to a fine of up to 20,000 pounds and/or a civil penalty.
- [ ] **References** -- Carry out as soon as possible, especially if they are a condition of employment.
- [ ] **Qualification checks** -- Verify relevant qualifications promptly.
- [ ] **DBS check** -- Required for certain roles (such as working with children or vulnerable adults).
- [ ] **FCA check** -- Required for roles regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority.

## Essential Written Policies

### Legally Required

- [ ] **Disciplinary policy and procedure** -- You must set out a disciplinary procedure and share it in writing with all employees.
- [ ] **Health and safety policy** -- A legal requirement under the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974 for employers with five or more employees.
- [ ] **Data protection policy and procedure** -- You must meet the lawfulness requirements for processing personal data. Include a separate subject access request procedure.

### Strongly Recommended

These policies are not strictly required by law, but they demonstrate compliance with legislation and protect both you and your employees:

- [ ] **Equal opportunities policy** -- Shows how you comply with equality legislation.
- [ ] **Grievance policy and procedure** -- Sets out how employees can raise complaints.
- [ ] **Annual holiday policy and procedure** -- Clarifies how leave is requested and managed.
- [ ] **Absence and sickness procedures** -- Establishes how sickness absence is reported and managed.
- [ ] **Bullying and harassment policy and procedure** -- Demonstrates your commitment to a safe workplace.

### Family-Friendly Policies

- [ ] **Maternity policy and procedure**
- [ ] **Paternity leave policy and procedure**
- [ ] **Adoption policy and procedure**
- [ ] **Shared parental leave policy and procedure**
- [ ] **Parental leave policy and procedure**
- [ ] **Flexible working policy and procedure**

Having these written policies in place is good practice even where not strictly required. They set clear expectations and help you handle situations consistently.

## People Management Practices

These are not required by legislation, but they represent HR best practice that will help your organisation grow effectively:

- [ ] **Clear objectives and regular reviews** -- Set clear goals for each team member and review progress regularly.
- [ ] **Performance management** -- Establish a process for ongoing feedback, not just annual appraisals.
- [ ] **Succession planning** -- Identify who could step into key roles if someone leaves.
- [ ] **Career development plans** -- Help employees understand their growth path within the organisation.
- [ ] **Accurate job descriptions** -- Enable employees to understand their role and expectations, and give you a basis for assessing performance.
- [ ] **Annual HR systems and processes review** -- Review your HR practices at least once a year to keep them current and effective.

## Health and Safety Compliance

### Insurance

- [ ] **Employers' liability insurance** -- A legal requirement. You must have insurance covering injury or disease to employees arising from their employment.

### First Aid

- [ ] **First-aid-trained employees** -- The Health and Safety (First-Aid) Regulations 1981 require you to provide adequate equipment, facilities, and personnel to ensure employees receive immediate attention if injured or taken ill at work.
- [ ] **Suitable first aid equipment on site** -- Ensure your first aid supplies are appropriate, accessible, and regularly checked.

### Workplace Safety

- [ ] **Health and safety policy** -- A legal requirement for employers with five or more employees under the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974.
- [ ] **Annual workplace risk assessments** -- Carry these out as part of your duty of care. Review them every time there are new machines, substances, or procedures that could introduce new hazards. Also carry out risk assessments for employees with medical conditions or who are pregnant.

## Building Your HR Foundations

If you are just starting to build your HR function, prioritise in this order:

1. **Legal essentials first** -- Written statement of employment particulars, right-to-work checks, employers' liability insurance, disciplinary procedure, and health and safety policy.
2. **Core policies next** -- Equal opportunities, grievance, data protection, absence management, and family-friendly policies.
3. **People development** -- Performance management, objectives, career development, and succession planning.
4. **Annual review cycle** -- Commit to reviewing all HR systems and policies at least once a year.

Getting these foundations right early will save you significant time and risk as your team grows.
