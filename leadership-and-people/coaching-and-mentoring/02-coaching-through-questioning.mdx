## The Link Between Questioning and Listening

There is a strong link between questioning and listening. There is an equally strong link between listening and questioning. Take a moment to consider both of those statements.

A combination of listening and questioning is the most productive approach. If you only ask questions, it becomes persecutory. If you only listen, it can become challenging for the speaker. The combination of the two is powerful.

Silence — supportive listening with expectation and interest — can also function as a very effective question. When you hold space with genuine attention, the other person often continues thinking and arrives at insights they would not have reached if you had jumped in with the next question.

## Purpose of Asking Questions

When you ask a question well, you accomplish several things at once:

- **Show interest** — the person knows you care about what they are thinking
- **Channel attention** — you help direct focus toward what matters most
- **Evoke inquiry and stimulate thinking** — you prompt the person to explore their own ideas more deeply
- **Create awareness and understanding** — both for the person answering and for you
- **Invite more questions** — good questions open up further lines of thinking

## Types of Questions

Not all questions serve the same purpose. Understanding the different types helps you choose the right one for the moment.

### Open Questions

Open questions invite expansive answers. They typically begin with *What*, *When*, *How*, *And*, or *So*. They encourage the other person to think broadly and share more of their perspective.

### Closed Questions

Closed questions invite a simple "yes" or "no" answer. They have their place — for confirming facts or checking understanding — but they limit the depth of the conversation when overused.

### Leading Questions

Leading questions steer the person toward a particular answer you already have in mind. They can be useful occasionally for reframing, but in coaching they undermine the person's ability to think for themselves.

### Empowering Questions

Empowering questions help the person search for their own answers and new possibilities. They shift ownership of the thinking to the person being coached.

### Powerful Questions

A powerful question is one that makes the person stop and think. It triggers fresh thinking. Often the person will respond with something like, "That is a great question" — and then pause before answering. Powerful questions open doors the person did not know were there.

### Forward-Moving Questions

Forward-moving questions shift the conversation from analysis of the past toward action and next steps. They help people move from understanding a problem to doing something about it.

## Unhelpful Questions to Avoid

Some questioning patterns actively hinder good thinking. Watch out for these:

### "Why" Questions

"Why" questions are often accusatory in tone, even when that is not your intention. They tend to make people feel defensive and criticised. Instead of asking "Why did you do that?", try "What led to that decision?" — the shift in framing makes a significant difference.

### Too Many Closed Questions

A few closed questions are fine, but a string of them turns the conversation into an interrogation. If you find yourself asking many closed questions, follow each one with an open question to deepen the exploration.

For example, instead of stopping at "Did you go to college?" (which invites only yes or no), follow up with "So what was it about college that led you to...?"

### Leading Questions

When you already know the answer you are looking for, you are not coaching — you are directing. Resist the urge to lead the person to your conclusion and instead ask genuinely open questions.

### Multiple Questions in One

Multiple questions happen when several questions get rolled into a single sentence. The person does not know which one to answer, and the thinking gets scattered. If you catch yourself stacking questions, simply stop. Pick the most important one and ask it on its own.

## The Shorter the Question, the More Expansive the Answer

This is one of the most important principles in coaching through questioning. Long, elaborate questions constrain the answer. Short, clear questions give the other person room to think widely and respond from their own perspective.

Compare these:
- "Given everything that has happened recently and the challenges you have been facing with your team and the upcoming deadline, what do you think might be a way forward?" (constrained)
- "What would you like to do next?" (expansive)

The second question achieves more because it leaves space for the person to bring whatever is most important to them into the answer.

## Coaching Leadership Style

A coaching style of leadership adopts a questioning and listening approach. Rather than telling people what to do, you encourage them to think well for themselves, and to come up with their own ways forward and solutions — with your encouragement and support.

The best questions are those that emerge from interested, curious listening. Listen well, and the next question will come into your mind. Ask it.

This approach does not mean you never share your own perspective. It means you lead with questions first, creating space for the other person's thinking before you offer yours.

## Empowering Questions: A Practical Toolkit

These questions help the person search for answers and discover new possibilities. Use them as starting points and adapt them to your conversations.

### Exploring Options and Actions

- What can you do about that?
- What other choices can you make?
- What is your next step?
- If it does not work, what else could you do?
- What about that makes it work?

### Shifting Perspective

- What is another way to look at that?
- How does that idea sound to you?
- What do you really mean by that?
- What does your intuition tell you?
- Where do you believe that thought comes from?

### Building on Experience

- Please remind me of how you were able to do that.
- How did that work for you before?
- What do you think the main challenge is?
- What seems to confuse you?

### Looking Forward

- Where will that lead?
- How much time will that take?
- What are the benefits in that?
- How will you feel if that happens?
- What do you want to experience?

### Challenging and Stretching

- What scares you about that?
- What are you unwilling to tolerate any longer?
- Where are you limiting yourself? What if you do, and what if you do not?
- How can you stretch yourself there?
- What will energise you?

### Connecting to Values and Identity

- Who are you when you do that?
- What about that makes a real statement for what you stand for?
- When is there a gap between who you are and who you really know yourself to be?
- What are you unwilling to give up?
- What really excites you about that?

### Reframing and Opening Up

- Why are things not worse?
- If you had all the time, energy, and money to achieve your goal, what would you do?
- What do you not like about that idea?
- What part of that do you not like?
- If you tried that, how would you feel?

Keep this list accessible. Return to it before coaching conversations, and over time these questions will become a natural part of how you engage with the people around you.
