## What Is a Thinking Environment

A Thinking Environment is a set of conditions — behaviours, attitudes, and structures — that enable people to think for themselves with rigour, imagination, courage, and grace. Developed by Nancy Kline, the framework is built on a fundamental insight: **the quality of everything we do depends on the thinking we do first, and the quality of our thinking depends on the way we treat each other while we are thinking.**

A leader's first job is to create a Thinking Environment. Every subsequent act of leadership gains quality from there.

Leading effectively means articulating a vision compellingly and getting the best from people so that their work manifests that vision. Getting the best from people means getting their best thinking. This requires knowing how to be with people — how to offer them the highest quality attention based on genuine interest in what they think, and on the expectation that they can think for themselves better than anyone can think for them.

> "The Thinking Environment is not just a set of techniques. It is a way of being in the world."
> — Shirley Edwards, Vice President Organisation Effectiveness, Xerox Corporation

## The Ten Components

The Thinking Environment rests on ten behaviours, each of which contributes to the conditions in which people think at their best. All ten work together — remove one and the quality of thinking diminishes.

### 1. Attention

**Listening with palpable respect and without interruption.**

The quality of your attention profoundly affects the quality of other people's thinking. As the listener, you must be more driven by interest in what is real and true for people than you are frightened of being proved wrong. As the thinker, knowing you will not be interrupted frees you truly to think for yourself.

### 2. Equality

**Treating each other as thinking peers. Giving equal turns and attention. Keeping agreements and boundaries.**

Even in a hierarchy, people can be equals as thinkers. Knowing you will have your turn improves the quality of your attention when it is someone else's turn.

### 3. Ease

**Offering freedom from internal rush or urgency.**

Ease creates. Urgency destroys. When it comes to helping people think for themselves, sometimes doing means not doing. The absence of pressure allows deeper, more creative thought to emerge.

### 4. Appreciation

**Offering genuine acknowledgement of a person's qualities. Practising a 5:1 ratio of appreciation to criticism.**

The human mind thinks more rigorously and creatively in a context of specific, sincere, succinct praise. Appreciation is not flattery — it is the accurate recognition of what someone does well and who they are at their best.

### 5. Encouragement

**Giving courage to go to the cutting edge of ideas by moving beyond internal competition.**

To be "better than" is not necessarily to be good. Encouragement in a Thinking Environment means creating the conditions where people feel safe to take intellectual risks, explore untested ideas, and push past the comfortable and familiar.

### 6. Feelings

**Allowing sufficient emotional release to restore thinking.**

Listening through the anger makes way for thorough thinking. Crying can make you smarter. After laughter, thinking improves. Emotions are not obstacles to clear thought — they are often the pathway to it. When feelings are suppressed, thinking gets stuck.

### 7. Information

**Supplying the facts. Dismantling denial.**

Withholding or denying information results in intellectual vandalism. Facing what you have been denying leads to better thinking. People need accurate, complete information to think well — and they need the courage to confront uncomfortable truths.

### 8. Diversity

**Welcoming divergent thinking and diverse group identities.**

The mind works best in the presence of reality. Reality is diverse. Homogeneity is a myth. When groups include and value different perspectives, backgrounds, and ways of thinking, the quality of their collective output improves.

### 9. Incisive Questions

**Removing assumptions that limit our ability to think for ourselves clearly and creatively.**

The liberation of the human mind lies right inside an Incisive Question. These are questions that identify and then remove the limiting assumption that is holding someone's thinking in place. Frequently in meetings, people can ask themselves or the team: *What might we be assuming that could be limiting our thinking on this issue? If we assumed something more liberating, what might our ideas be?*

### 10. Place

**Creating a physical environment that says back to people, "You matter."**

When the physical environment affirms their importance, people think more boldly. When our bodies are cared for and respected, our thinking improves. The room, the seating, the light, the temperature — all of these communicate something about how much the organisation values the people in it.

## Research Evidence

The effectiveness of the Thinking Environment is not just anecdotal. Research by Emily Havers (MSc, University of Portsmouth) studied how meetings held in a Thinking Environment impact organisational life. The study interviewed 15 senior officers from 11 organisations across 3 continents.

It found a **95% consistency** in the outcomes of meetings held in a Thinking Environment, including that these meetings:

- Produced a measurable, positive impact on the performance indicators of organisations
- Generated better ideas, solutions, and decisions
- Created an environment in which people felt valued and equal
- Achieved resolution faster
- Gave rise to greater participation and involvement from everyone
- Fostered productive working relationships

> "There is absolutely no doubt in my mind that these meetings have produced the best results of any meetings I've been in. It's been a total change. It's been a real eye opener for me. I've been running meetings for years and years and years, and I've never experienced the feeling of success coming out of meetings that I've felt coming out of these meetings in a Thinking Environment."
> — Managing Director, Insurance Retail

> "I can positively say that as a result of our Thinking Environment culture, our business has improved by at least 20%. And that's measurable in financial terms."
> — Provincial Director, Financial Services

## The Four Building Blocks

The Thinking Environment is put into practice through four building block applications. Each creates a different structure for thinking together, and all four are used in Transforming Meetings.

### Thinking Pairs

Thinking Pairs are the foundation of the Thinking Environment. Two people take turns thinking out loud while the other listens without interruption.

**The key principle:** To be interrupted is not good. To get lucky and not be interrupted is better. But to know you will not be interrupted allows you truly to think for yourself.

Attention from one person generates thinking in another. Listening to ignite is fundamentally different from listening to reply. Transformative listening comes from genuine interest in where your partner will go in their thinking, and from your courage to trust their intelligence.

When you are the listener in a Thinking Pair:

- Settle back
- Keep your eyes on the eyes of your partner as they speak
- Cultivate fascination with what your partner will say next
- Achieve a composure that is wildly dynamic
- Do not interrupt
- Trust that not uttering a word is one of the most effective things you can do
- Know that your job is to help your partner think for themselves, not to think for them
- Remember that the expression of feelings is often part of the thinking process
- Be aware that much of what they say will be the result of your effect on them

In the quiet presence of your attention, respect, and ease, important things happen for the thinker. Fresh ideas emerge. Confusion dissipates. Painful feelings subside. Creativity explodes. Even if you already know what your partner is about to say, do not interrupt — what matters is what happens for them because they say it.

**Starting questions for Thinking Pairs:**
- *What would you like to think about, and what are your thoughts?*
- When thinking subsides: *What more do you think, or feel, or want to say?*

### Dialogue

Dialogue at its best is not two people talking — it is two people thinking. For dialogue to be rich, intelligent, and worth the time, both people must offer each other all Ten Components of a Thinking Environment throughout.

Good dialogue can be described as Thinking Pairs on the same topic with short, frequent turns back and forth. The quality of listening should be as good in Dialogue as it is in Thinking Pairs — and that is usually a significant challenge.

The challenge of sustaining a Thinking Environment is greater in Dialogue than in Thinking Pairs because there is a much higher level of emotional investment in the topic (because it is shared) and in the outcome of the exchange. The higher the level of emotional investment, the more challenging it is truly to want to know what the other person thinks.

At a minimum, Dialogue in a Thinking Environment means:

- Guaranteeing each other that you will not interrupt
- Sustaining evidence of interest in where the other person will go next — through your face, eye contact, responsive sounds, and the ease you generate as you listen
- Not taking up all of the time with your turn — respecting equality of time as well as attention

### Rounds

Everyone matters. Regardless of power differentials and hierarchical placement, everyone's thinking matters because getting everyone's best thinking produces the best results.

The best way to get the best thinking from everyone is to populate your meeting with systematic, uninterrupted Rounds. Rounds increase the generative nature of the group's thinking and usually produce superior ideas in less time.

A Round requires four actions from the Chair:

1. **Decide the question** that people will be addressing in the Round
2. **Determine the direction** (clockwise or counterclockwise)
3. **Ask for a volunteer** to begin the Round
4. **Remind people** that no one speaks again until the Round is completed

If you do only one thing to improve the thinking in your groups, institute Rounds. And be sure to decide on the question first.

### Open Discussion

In Open Discussion within a Thinking Environment, anyone can speak next in no particular order. No one raises their hand to be put in a queue. No one "tailgates" the speaker. But no one interrupts. And the person speaking stays true to their promise to be succinct.

The decision to stay interested in what the person speaking will think and say next runs counter to the common — and untrue — assumption that interruption and cross-fire produce better thinking. In fact, interruption produces adrenaline. Adrenaline reduces attention. Reduced attention creates poorer thinking.

Inevitably, Open Discussion diminishes in quality as the same 30% of the group begin to dominate. At that point, the Chair (or anyone) calls for a Round, determines the question, and determines the direction. Someone then volunteers to go first.

## The Three Streams of Attention

Paradoxically, the Thinking Partner must have 100% of their attention in three streams simultaneously throughout a session:

- **First Stream:** Attention on the content of what the Thinker is saying
- **Second Stream:** Attention on your own response to what the Thinker is saying
- **Third Stream:** Attention on the creation of a Thinking Environment for the Thinker

For the Thinker to think at their best, the Partner must generate a fine balance of these three streams. If the second stream begins to dominate — as it often does when the Partner has any investment in the outcome — the first and third streams weaken proportionately, and the quality of the Thinker's thinking is compromised.

When the three streams are in balance, the Thinker can sense the quality of attention and generate ideas without impediment.

Mastering the skill of maintaining all three streams simultaneously — particularly while wanting fervently to speak — is one of the deepest challenges of the Thinking Environment. The results are well worth the discipline.
