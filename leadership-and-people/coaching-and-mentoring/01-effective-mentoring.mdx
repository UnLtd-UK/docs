## Why Mentoring Matters

Being an entrepreneur can be both lonely and overwhelming. Surrounding yourself with good people becomes critical, and access to a supportive, experienced business mentor can make a real difference to the success of your social venture and your resilience as an entrepreneur.

The evidence is compelling: research has shown that **70% of small businesses that receive mentoring survive for five years or more**, which is double the rate compared with non-mentored entrepreneurs (Federation of Small Businesses).

A well-matched mentor brings perspective you cannot generate alone. They have navigated challenges similar to yours, and their experience becomes a resource you can draw on as you build and grow your venture.

> "My mentor was really enthusiastic for our project, he was inspiring. He also was pragmatic; he helped us to have a holistic view, a comprehensive picture of our business, and not to leave out some aspects."

## The Role of a Mentor

A mentor is someone who walks alongside you in your entrepreneurial journey. Their role is to:

- **Bounce off and challenge your ideas** — acting as a sounding board who asks tough questions and helps you stress-test your thinking
- **Help you clarify goals and next steps** — bringing structure and focus when things feel scattered or uncertain
- **Share experience openly and honestly** — drawing on what they have learned from their own successes and failures
- **Help you stay focused, overcome obstacles, and keep motivated** — providing steadiness when the road gets difficult
- **Offer encouragement and motivation** — reinforcing your confidence and reminding you of your progress

### What a Mentor Is Not

It is equally important to understand the boundaries of a mentoring relationship. Your mentor is **not**:

- **A consultant** — they are not there to carry out work for you
- **A decision-maker** — they will not tell you what you should do or what decisions you should make
- **A funder** — they are not a source of financial investment for your venture

The distinction matters. A mentor empowers you to think and decide for yourself. If you find yourself expecting your mentor to solve your problems directly, step back and reconsider how you are engaging with the relationship.

## Your Responsibilities as a Mentee

Mentoring is not a passive experience. You are the driver of this relationship, and its success depends primarily on what you bring to it.

### Own Your Goals and Expectations

Establish your own goals before your first meeting, and discuss expectations openly with your mentor. Be clear about what you want to achieve and how you would like to work together.

### Drive the Relationship

Set the agenda for each meeting. Take responsibility for your own learning. Your mentor is there to support your thinking, not to lead it. Come prepared with topics, questions, and updates on progress since your last conversation.

### Show Consideration

Respect the time and energy your mentor gives voluntarily. Be punctual, be prepared, and follow through on commitments you make during your sessions.

### Be Committed, Open, and Honest

Share feedback — both about your venture and about the mentoring relationship itself. If something is not working, say so. If your mentor's input was particularly valuable, tell them that too. Honesty builds trust, and trust deepens the relationship.

### Challenge Your Mentor

Do not expect to be told what to do. Push back on ideas that do not fit your context. Ask your mentor to explain their reasoning. A good mentor will welcome being challenged — it shows you are thinking critically rather than deferring.

## Preparing for a Mentoring Relationship

Before seeking a mentor, take a few minutes to reflect honestly on three areas:

### Your Motivation

What made you think about seeking a mentor at this point? What would you like to gain from the relationship? Be specific — vague expectations lead to unfocused conversations.

### Your Focus

Do you have specific challenges you would like your mentor to act as a sounding board for? The more clearly you can articulate these, the better your mentor can prepare and contribute.

### Your Capacity

Do you have the time and energy to build a relationship with a new person? Mentoring requires regular meetings, preparation between sessions, and follow-through on actions. If you are already stretched thin, consider whether this is the right time, or whether you need to create space first.

## Getting the Most from Mentoring

Like any relationship, mentoring needs investment to thrive. Four ingredients make the difference:

- **Time** — commit to regular, consistent meetings and protect that time in your calendar
- **Energy** — show up fully present, not distracted by the hundred other things on your list
- **Care** — invest in the human relationship, not just the transactional exchange of advice
- **Purpose** — keep the relationship anchored to clear goals that evolve as your venture develops

When all four are present, mentoring becomes one of the most powerful supports available to you as a social entrepreneur. When any of them is missing, the relationship drifts and loses its value.

The best mentoring relationships are partnerships between two committed people. Your mentor brings experience and perspective. You bring drive, openness, and a willingness to think differently. Together, you can achieve more than either of you could alone.
