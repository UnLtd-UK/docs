## Overview

In January 2021, the House of Lords Science and Technology Select Committee published a landmark report examining how the UK can help its ageing population live more healthily. The report assessed the feasibility of the Government's Ageing Society Grand Challenge mission — to "ensure that people can enjoy at least five extra healthy, independent years of life by 2035, while narrowing the gap between the experience of the richest and poorest."

The Committee's conclusion was stark: the UK is not on track to meet this target. For men, it would take 75 years to achieve the target at current rates of improvement. For women, healthy life expectancy at birth has actually decreased in the past decade.

## The Demographics of Ageing

The UK's population is ageing rapidly. By 2035, projections indicate:

- Around **16 million people** will be over 65 (an increase of 4 million from 2018)
- Nearly **5 million people** will be over 80 (a 51% increase from 2018)
- The working-age population will remain relatively static

This demographic shift will not be evenly distributed. Rural areas are expected to age faster than cities, concentrating older populations in places where service delivery is more difficult.

### Life Expectancy vs Healthy Life Expectancy

While life expectancy has continued to increase (79.4 years for men and 83.1 years for women as of 2017–19), healthy life expectancy — the time spent in good health — has not kept pace. People are living longer, but many of those extra years are spent in poor health.

The social gradient is pronounced. In England, the difference in healthy life expectancy between the most and least deprived areas is **18.9 years for men** and **19.4 years for women**.

## The Science of Ageing

Understanding of the biology of ageing is advancing rapidly. Scientists have identified nine "hallmarks of ageing" — biological processes that drive age-related decline:

1. **Genomic instability** — accumulation of genetic damage over time
2. **Telomere attrition** — shortening of chromosome ends, limiting cell division
3. **Epigenetic alterations** — changes to gene expression that accumulate with age
4. **Loss of proteostasis** — failure to remove damaged proteins
5. **Deregulated nutrient sensing** — disruption of the body's response to nutrients
6. **Mitochondrial dysfunction** — declining energy production in cells
7. **Cellular senescence** — accumulation of cells that can no longer divide
8. **Stem cell exhaustion** — reduction in the body's ability to replace worn-out cells
9. **Altered intercellular communication** — changes in cell-to-cell signalling

### Promising Treatments

Several potential treatments targeting these processes are in development:

- **Drugs targeting nutrient pathways** — Rapamycin and metformin show promise in slowing age-related decline. The TAME (Targeting Aging with Metformin) trial is a landmark study approved by the US FDA to test whether metformin can delay multiple age-related diseases.
- **Senolytics** — Drugs that kill senescent cells have shown dramatic results in animal studies, extending healthy lifespan by up to 30% in mice. The first human clinical trials began in 2018.
- **Anti-inflammatories** — Targeting chronic low-level inflammation (inflammaging) associated with ageing shows therapeutic potential.
- **Repurposed drugs** — Existing medications being tested for anti-ageing properties offer a faster and cheaper route to clinical application.

## Lifestyle Factors and Healthy Ageing

The main risk factors affecting health in older age are well established:

- **Smoking** — the single largest contributor to health inequalities, driving up to 80% of cardiovascular disease and cancer risk
- **Poor diet and obesity** — obesity significantly increases age-related inflammation and is linked to type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and certain cancers
- **Physical inactivity** — physical activity improves cardiovascular health, muscle mass, cognitive function, and mental health at every age
- **Excessive alcohol consumption** — reduces healthy life expectancy after age 55 by an average of 1.6 years
- **Cognitive inactivity and stress** — chronic stress accelerates biological ageing processes, while cognitive activity builds protective "cognitive reserve"

### Key Findings on Lifestyle

- Up to **80% of heart disease, stroke, and type 2 diabetes** could be prevented by eliminating shared risk factors
- Healthy lifestyle changes bring benefits **at any stage of life** — it is never too late
- Health behaviours are socially patterned: healthier lifestyles are more common among affluent and educated populations
- A **life-course approach** to healthy ageing is recommended, focusing on key transition points (starting school, entering work, retirement) rather than single-issue campaigns

### Health Inequalities

Health inequalities are a persistent challenge. People in the most deprived areas:

- Are three times more likely to have type 2 diabetes
- Are four times more likely to have chronic heart disease
- Face barriers to accessing health information and following advice due to limited resources

The report found that health education strategies can inadvertently increase inequalities if they are not specifically designed to reach disadvantaged communities.

## Technology and Independent Living

Technology offers significant opportunities to help people live healthily and independently in older age:

### In the Home

- **Telecare systems** — pendant alarms, fall detectors, and activity monitoring sensors can help people live safely alone. There are 1.7 million users in the UK, but an estimated 4 million could benefit.
- **Smart home technology** — sensors monitoring daily routines can detect early signs of health changes (increased toilet visits may flag infection; changes in gait can predict falls)
- **Digital services** — the upcoming digital telephone switchover in 2025 is driving modernisation of telecare systems

### Health Monitoring

- **Wearable and implantable devices** — enabling precise monitoring and timely interventions (such as continuous glucose monitoring for diabetes)
- **Robotics and AI** — assistive robots can support physical tasks, medication compliance, rehabilitation, and companionship
- **Data-driven services** — combining health data from multiple sources could enable more personalised and preventive care

### Barriers to Adoption

- Only **7% of housing in England** meets basic accessibility standards
- Just **59% of single-person households** where the resident is over 65 have broadband
- Technology is often designed without input from older people
- Trust, privacy concerns, and digital literacy remain significant barriers

## The Ageing Society Grand Challenge

The Committee found that the Government's Ageing Society Grand Challenge mission lacked leadership and a clear strategy. Key recommendations included:

- **Urgently devise a strategy** for achieving the five-year target by 2035, including a roadmap and delivery mechanisms
- **Prioritise funding** for research into biological processes of ageing and drugs targeting those processes
- **Invest in public health** — regulatory and fiscal measures to encourage healthier lifestyles, with priority given to disadvantaged groups
- **Improve housing** — use planning rules to ensure homes and communities are accessible and adaptable
- **Expand technology deployment** — invest in assistive technologies, robotics, and AI while ensuring equitable access
- **Address health inequalities** explicitly in all ageing-related strategies

The report concluded that a combination of approaches from biomedical science, technology, local public health, and government intervention will be required to meet the challenge.
