## What Is Commissioning?

Commissioning is the process by which local authorities and public bodies decide how to spend their budgets to get the best possible services for local people. It involves anticipating future needs and expectations, rather than simply reacting to present demand.

Commissioning must be kept separate from procurement. While commissioning officers hold the budget and make the business case for services, procurement officers handle the legal and administrative purchasing process.

## How the Procurement Process Works

Councils work within the parameters of the Public Contracts Regulations (previously the 2015 Procurement Act). As a result, contracts that are cumulatively worth more than £25,000 go through a formal tender process — that is, open competition.

This means that even if lots of smaller pieces of work are required over a long period of time, if the total value of all that work exceeds £25,000, it will have to go out to tender.

### Procurement Thresholds

| Contract Value | Process |
|---|---|
| Up to £10,000 | Direct arrangement with a provider, based on a written quote (Request for Quotation). The commissioner must gather three written quotes from different providers to ensure best value for money. |
| £10,000–£25,000 | Typically requires a more formal quotation process, though arrangements vary by authority. |
| Over £25,000 | Must go through a formal tender or competition process. |

### The Tender Process

When you apply to a tender, the decision about who gets the contract is based on two key factors:

1. **Value for money** — who can deliver all required elements for the best price
2. **Quality** — your ability to meet specified quality criteria

Some contracts are weighted 50/50 between value for money and quality, while others vary. Always check the weighting before you bid.

### Demonstrating Your Track Record

For tenders over £25,000, you will be asked for information about your track record upfront. This helps commissioners assess the ongoing viability of your business and might include providing your last two years of accounts. The level of scrutiny depends on the value of the tender and the type of service being commissioned.

## Pricing Your Bid

You need to be realistic about pricing so that your quality is not compromised. While there can be a temptation to under-price in order to be more competitive, you still have to deliver the service to the required standard.

Commissioners will not want to invest in something that could fall apart because it has not been priced realistically. If a service fails, they face the disruption of finding alternative provision for their clients.

## The Role of Social Value

Some contracts include a weighting for social value, which is where social enterprises can deliver a competitive advantage. Social value refers to the additional benefit to society that a provider can deliver alongside the core service. However, social value is not always explicitly weighted in tenders — it is worth looking out for when reviewing opportunities.

## How Commissioners Make Decisions

Commissioners use a cyclical process to plan and deliver services:

1. **Assess needs** — using data such as the Joint Strategic Needs Assessment (JSNA) and population health data
2. **Plan services** — setting commissioning intentions and developing specifications
3. **Procure services** — running tenders or establishing frameworks
4. **Monitor and evaluate** — tracking performance against Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
5. **Re-commission or de-commission** — deciding whether to continue, redesign, or end a service

### Market Position Statements

Most local authorities produce a Market Position Statement or commissioning intentions document. These set out an analysis of the needs of their population against the supply of services available, and identify where they seek to commission more services. These documents are publicly available and are invaluable for understanding where opportunities lie.

## Engaging with Commissioners

Building relationships with commissioners is essential for social enterprises seeking public sector work. Key ways to engage include:

- **Register with procurement portals** such as Supplying the Southwest or your local authority's procurement system (many use Pro Contract)
- **Attend provider engagement events** — most local authorities have a provider engagement or reference group that meets regularly
- **Participate in market testing events** — authorities hold soft market testing or market engagement events to gauge interest and develop their thinking before launching tenders
- **Make commissioners aware of your services** — let commissioning and social work teams know what you offer and share ideas for new services

## Understanding the NHS Commissioning Landscape

NHS commissioning works through a distinct but related system. Clinical Commissioning Groups (and their successors, Integrated Care Boards) commission health services, while NHS England commissions specialised services. The King's Fund provides detailed analysis of how NHS commissioning works and is evolving.

For suppliers looking to work with the NHS, supplier information and guidance is available through the NHS procurement channels.
