## Quick Reference: Top 10 Terms

These are the terms you will encounter most frequently when engaging with public sector commissioning.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **ITT (Invitation to Tender)** | The information pack about an opportunity to apply — essentially a call for bids. |
| **Lot** | A discrete area of work within a tender. For example, a region-wide mental health tender may be broken into geographical lots or specialisms like early prevention and crisis intervention. |
| **PBR (Payment by Results)** | A model where you receive an agreed fee upfront with the remainder paid on the basis of demonstrable results against agreed outcomes within an agreed timeframe. |
| **Outcomes-Based Commissioning** | A sum agreed against delivery of a range of outcomes demonstrated against agreed KPIs. Not prescriptive about how the service is delivered. |
| **Individual Service Funds** | A service user chooses a provider to manage their individual budget and co-ordinate their support, enabling flexibility and personalisation. |
| **Call-Off Frameworks** | A pre-arranged list of providers used to make individual purchases as client needs arise, after providers have met quality checks. |
| **Block Contracts** | An agreement to deliver a specified service for a fixed fee over an agreed timeframe. Less common in social care due to the personalisation agenda. |
| **VfM (Value for Money)** | The optimum combination of whole-life cost and quality to meet user requirements, assessed using economy, efficiency, and effectiveness criteria. |
| **Social Value** | The additional value or benefit to society that a provider delivers alongside the core activity. |
| **Assistive Technology Framework** | A framework for commissioning assistive technology products that may increase independence. Most local authorities have a call-off framework for these. |

## Full A–Z Glossary

### A

**Audit** — An independent, objective assurance and consulting activity designed to add value and improve an organisation's operations. It helps accomplish objectives through a systematic, disciplined approach to evaluating and improving risk management, control, and governance processes.

**Award Stage** — The second stage of the tendering process. This may be incorporated into the first stage to make a single tendering procedure. Sometimes a shortlist is formed during the selection stage, and in these cases the award stage forms a separate phase.

### B

**Benchmarking** — A method for gauging performance in a service area or outcome by comparing it to the performance of other organisations of a similar size and nature.

**Best Value** — A framework introduced in 1999, based on statutory indicators to help local authorities plan, measure, manage, and improve their performance. Although still in force, it has largely been replaced by the Comprehensive Performance Assessment (CPA).

**Bid** — Usually used in connection with grant funding. The funder sets out the problems which the grants are intended to address. Bids from applicants offer a variety of ways of solving the problems at various costs. Successful bids result in the award of a grant.

**Block Contract** — An agreement between a provider and commissioner to deliver a specified service for a fixed fee over an agreed timeframe. The personalisation agenda has reduced the trend for block contracting in social care as it is inflexible and prevents diversity and choice of provision.

### C

**Call-Off Framework** — A pre-arranged or registered list of providers delivering a particular type of product, activity, or service, used to make individual purchases as client needs arise. Providers typically undergo quality checks including providing insurance certificates, accounts, and policy details.

**Commissioning** — The process by which local authorities decide how to spend their money to get the best possible services for local people. Involves anticipating future needs and expectations rather than reacting to present demand.

**Commissioning Officers** — Officers who hold the budget for the purchasing of local services and make the business case for the services to be purchased. Commissioning functions must be separated from those of procurement officers.

**Competitive Dialogue** — One of the four key procurement processes allowed under EU procurement directives. Can only be used in defined circumstances — broadly for complex contracts where the final specification is yet to be decided.

**Contract** — A legally binding agreement between a government body and a provider to deliver services on behalf of the public body. It specifies the services to be provided, payment terms, and legal obligations that each party accepts.

**Contract Documents** — Documents incorporated in an enforceable agreement between a buyer and seller, including conditions of contract, specification, pricing document, form of tender, and relevant correspondence.

**Cost Effectiveness** — Analysis that compares the costs of alternative ways of achieving the same or similar outcomes.

### D

**De-Commissioning** — The process of planning and managing a reduction in service activity or terminating a contract in line with commissioning objectives, including for non-performance or under-performance issues.

**Default** — A breach of a contract condition, for example failure to deliver on time.

**Direct Payments** — Money provided to individuals so they can arrange their own care services rather than having the council arrange services on their behalf. Can be paid into a bank account or loaded onto a pre-paid card.

### E

**EU Directive** — A European Union legal instruction binding on all Member States, which must be implemented through national legislation. The European single market is covered by a number of Directives which give detailed instructions on tendering procedures.

**Evaluation** — Assessment of the extent to which a programme has met its objectives: that is, has been effective, economical, and efficient. Summative evaluation assesses after the programme is complete, while formative evaluation assesses the programme as it is being put in place and during early operation.

### F

**Framework Agreement** — An agreement between one or more contracting authorities and one or more economic operators, establishing the terms governing contracts to be awarded during a given period, in particular with regard to price and quantity.

**Funding Stream** — A sum of money allocated by government for a defined purpose.

### G

**Grant** — Payments to outside bodies made where a department is required, or wishes, to maintain detailed control over the expenditure and where a procurement approach is not suitable.

### I

**Individual Commissioning** — Commissioning on an individual basis, akin to spot purchasing. Most local authorities try to limit this as it can be costly and raise concerns about competitive procurement practice. Total spot purchases must be under £25,000 over a given timeframe.

**Individual Service Funds** — A model where a service user chooses a provider to manage their individual budget and co-ordinate their support. The personal budget is made explicit and discussed with the chosen provider, who flexibly delivers pre-agreed outcomes either directly or through sub-contracting.

**Infrastructure** — Physical facilities, structures, systems, relationships, people, knowledge, and skills that support, develop, co-ordinate, represent, and promote front-line organisations to enable them to deliver their missions more effectively.

**Invitation to Tender (ITT)** — A collection of documents sent to prospective providers, inviting bids for the provision of works, goods, or services. Comprises instructions to tender, terms and conditions of contract, and the specification.

### J

**Joint Commissioning** — The process whereby two or more organisations act together to co-ordinate the commissioning of services, taking joint responsibility for the translation of strategy into action.

### K

**KPIs (Key Performance Indicators)** — Tools which help purchasers measure the performance of suppliers against their contractual obligations. KPIs are normally detailed in the service specification document.

### L

**LAA (Local Area Agreement)** — An agreement between central and local government to achieve local solutions to meet local needs whilst also contributing to national priorities and the achievement of standards set by central government.

**Locality Commissioning** — Commissioning based around a smaller geographical area, cluster of schools, or community-focused approach.

**Lowest Price** — One of two systems which allows for tender selection on the basis of price and financial appraisal only.

### M

**Monitoring** — The ongoing collection of information about a programme and assessment of its implications. Information may be needed for effective management, wider accountability, and policy development.

### N

**Negotiated Procedure** — A tendering procedure generally used for large capital projects where a range of solutions to deliver the requirements are possible. The selection stage identifies organisations who will proceed into the negotiated award stage.

### O

**Outcome** — The totality of what a programme or project is set up to deliver or achieve.

**Outcomes-Based Commissioning** — A model where a sum is agreed against delivery of a range of outcomes demonstrated against agreed KPIs, without being prescriptive about how the service is delivered.

**Output** — The end result of carrying out an activity — usually a product. It is important to distinguish what has been produced (the output) from the effect it may be designed to help achieve (the outcome).

### P

**PBR (Payment by Results)** — A commissioning model where providers receive an agreed fee upfront and the remainder on the basis of demonstrable results against agreed outcomes within an agreed timeframe.

**Performance Indicator** — A clearly defined measure of how well an organisation is meeting its objectives. Indicators can relate to inputs, outputs, and outcomes.

**PQQ (Pre-Qualification Questionnaire)** — A document forming the basis of the selection phase of the tendering process. The PQQ acts as the gateway to the tendering process. Failure to submit a successful PQQ means the tenderer is excluded from the award stage.

**Procurement** — The purchase of goods and services by publicly funded bodies at the best possible total price, in the right quantity and quality, at the right time, generally via a contract. Procurement must be kept separate from commissioning.

**Purchase Order** — An official template used to place an order with a supplier.

### Q

**Quotation** — A verbal or (preferably) written price given by a provider in response to a request for quotation.

### R

**Re-Commissioning** — Where evidence within the cyclical commissioning process points to continuing or redesigning an existing service due to changed evidence of need, with the same or a different provider.

**Regional/Sub-Regional Commissioning** — Where whole regions or a number of authorities within regions agree to commission services together, recognising cross-boundary activity. There may also be economic benefits from several areas commissioning services from the same providers.

**Restricted Procedure** — A tendering process which uses the selection stage to set up a select list or framework of providers. The list may be used for a defined period of up to eight years to invite tenders or request quotes as required.

**Risk** — Uncertainty of outcome, whether positive opportunity or negative threat. It is the combination of the chance of an event and its consequences.

### S

**Selection Stage** — The first phase of the tendering process, involving completion and assessment of the PQQ. This is based on an appraisal of business/financial acumen and risk. Those who pass go on to the award stage. The selection stage is usually managed by procurement and legal officers.

**Service Level Agreement (SLA)** — The part of a contract which specifies the services to be delivered. Sometimes used as standalone agreements that do not set out full contractual terms, simply detailing the services and minimal contact information. Also used between departments within public authorities.

**Social Value** — The additional value or benefit to society that a provider can deliver alongside the core activity being commissioned.

**Specification** — A detailed description of the supplies or services to be purchased. If you cannot deliver exactly what the specification requires, it may not be worth submitting a tender.

**Spot Contract** — An individually commissioned package of care for a specific client, sitting outside normal contract arrangements.

**Strategic Commissioning** — When a service is commissioned covering an entire borough, city, or wider area.

### T

**Tender** — A written offer to contract to provide goods or services precisely as specified in the tender documents at a stated price or rate. Successful tenders result in the award of a contract.

**TUPE (Transfer of Undertakings)** — The Transfer of Undertakings (Protection of Employment) Regulations 1981. The purpose of TUPE is to preserve continuity of employment and safeguard employment rights of all employees whose employment transfers to a new employer as a result of a relevant transfer.

### V

**Value for Money (VfM)** — The optimum combination of whole-life cost and quality (or fitness for purpose) to meet user requirements. Assessed by the National Audit Office using the criteria of economy, efficiency, and effectiveness.

### W

**Whole Life Cost** — The full cost to an organisation of a solution over the full period that the requirement will exist. Takes into account running costs such as energy usage, maintenance requirements, staff training needs, and disposal costs, as well as the initial purchase price and product lifespan.
