## Writing Principles for Bids and Grants

Strong writing is the foundation of every successful bid or grant application. Whether you are completing a Pre-Qualification Questionnaire or drafting a full tender response, these principles will help your submission stand out.

### Keep It Clear and Concise

- **Write at an eighth-grade reading level.** Evaluators read dozens of submissions. Simple, direct language ensures your message lands quickly.
- **Delete ruthlessly.** After your first draft, cut the word count in half. Then review again and cut further. Every sentence should earn its place.
- **Make sentences short.** Long, winding sentences lose readers. Aim for one idea per sentence.
- **Use bullets, not blocks.** Break dense paragraphs into bullet points wherever possible. This improves scannability and makes key information easy to find.

### Lead with Impact

- **Open with the outcome.** Start each section by stating what you will deliver and why it matters, not with background context.
- **Say it once, not twice.** Repetition pads your word count without adding value, and evaluators will notice.
- **Bold your key messages.** Draw the reader's eye to the most important points in each section.

### Choose Precision over Style

- **Be clear, not clever.** Avoid metaphors, wordplay, or vague language. Specificity builds confidence in your ability to deliver.
- **Cut all jargon.** Unless a term is required by the funder's specification, replace sector-specific language with plain English.

## Networking with Commissioners and Funders

Building relationships with commissioners and funders before you apply gives you a significant advantage. You gain insight into priorities, language, and what decision-makers actually value.

### Preparing for Networking Events

When you receive an invitation to a funder or commissioner networking event, prepare in advance:

- **Identify who to connect with.** Research the attendees and focus on people working in your venture's area of impact.
- **Clarify your elevator pitch.** Be ready to explain your organisation's purpose, what you do, how you do it, and why it matters. Keep it concise and grounded in your Theory of Change.
- **Bring notes.** There is no shame in having prompt cards or a one-page summary of your key points. Preparation builds confidence.

### Making Genuine Connections

- **Show genuine interest and curiosity.** Ask commissioners about their priorities, the challenges they face, and how they see the landscape evolving.
- **Speak with authenticity.** Talk naturally and with passion about the social issue your venture addresses. Evaluators and funders respond to conviction backed by evidence.
- **Build the relationship over time.** A single conversation rarely leads to funding. Follow up, stay in touch, and position yourself as a credible partner before opportunities arise.
- **Step outside your comfort zone.** If networking feels daunting, start small. Attend events with a colleague, set a goal of speaking to two or three people, and build from there.

### Using Social Media to Extend Your Reach

Hashtags and social media can amplify your networking efforts. After an event, share takeaways online and tag relevant organisations. This keeps you visible between face-to-face meetings and signals that you are actively engaged in the sector.

## Finding Tenders and Grants

Before you start searching, understand the difference between the two main types of funding opportunity.

### Tenders vs Grants

| Type | Definition | Key Characteristic |
|------|------------|--------------------|
| **Tender** | An invitation to bid for a contract to supply goods or services | Your organisation can generate income and profit from delivery |
| **Grant** | Non-repayable funding awarded for a specific project or purpose | Typically non-profit; funds must be used for the stated project |

Many organisations begin with grants and gradually move toward traded income through tenders. Both require strong writing, but the evaluation criteria and structures differ.

### Key Government Databases

Register on these platforms to receive alerts about relevant opportunities:

- [Find a Grant](https://www.find-government-grants.service.gov.uk/) -- The UK government's central database for grant funding opportunities.
- [Contracts Finder](https://www.gov.uk/contracts-finder) -- Lists public sector contracts worth over 12,000 pounds. You can search by sector, location, and value.

<Note>
These databases primarily cover England. Scotland, Northern Ireland, and Wales each have their own procurement and grant portals. Check your devolved government's website for the equivalent platforms.
</Note>

### Setting Up Effective Alerts

Both platforms allow you to create filtered alerts based on your sector and focus area. To get the most from these:

- **Define your aims and objectives first.** Without clarity on what your organisation does and who it serves, you will be overwhelmed by irrelevant leads.
- **Review and refine your filters regularly.** As your organisation grows, your target opportunities will change.

### Starting Small

If you are a small or early-stage organisation, large contracts can feel overwhelming. Keep these points in mind:

- **Look for lots.** Many large contracts are broken into smaller lots. You do not need to bid for every lot -- choose the ones that match your capacity.
- **Start with discrete, manageable contracts.** Build a track record with smaller bids before pursuing major opportunities.
- **Consider partnerships.** Bidding as part of a consortium allows you to access larger contracts. If you do, designate one person to ensure consistency across the joint submission and establish clear working agreements upfront.

## Understanding Bid Language and Process

Tender documents have their own vocabulary and structure. Knowing what to look for saves you time and improves your chances of submitting a compliant, competitive bid.

### Procurement Procedures

| Procedure | What It Means |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Open procedure** | You submit your full bid directly -- there is no pre-selection stage |
| **Restricted procedure** | A two-stage process where you first complete a Pre-Qualification Questionnaire (PQQ) before being invited to submit a full bid |

### Key Elements of a Tender Document

Work through these elements systematically when you receive a tender specification:

- **Lots.** Review all available lots and identify which ones align with your capabilities. You do not need to bid for every lot.
- **Closing date.** Note it immediately and work backwards to build your timeline. Late submissions are almost always rejected.
- **Tender timetable.** This outlines key milestones including the clarification window, submission deadline, evaluation period, and contract award date.
- **Clarification questions.** Use the clarification window to ask questions about anything unclear in the specification. Be aware that your questions and the answers will typically be shared with all bidders.
- **Instructions to Bidders.** Read these carefully. They specify formatting requirements such as font size, ink colour, and structure. Evaluators will mark you down for ignoring instructions.
- **Word count limits.** Respect them strictly. Exceeding the word count results in lost marks or disqualification.
- **Tender evaluation method.** This tells you how your bid will be scored, including the weightings for quality, price, and other criteria. The lowest price does not always win -- understand what the evaluator values most.
- **Grade label descriptions.** These explain the scoring scale used to assess submissions. Understanding the difference between, for example, a "good" and "excellent" response helps you target the top grade.

### Identifying Win Themes

Win themes are the core messages that run through your bid and demonstrate why you are the best choice. To develop them:

- **Start from the customer's perspective.** What matters most to the commissioner or funder? What problems are they trying to solve?
- **Structure your response around delivery.** Explain how you plan to deliver, what you will deliver, and what impact it will have.
- **Be realistic on cost.** Do not submit artificially low prices. Evaluators will question whether you can deliver at that cost, and it undermines your credibility.

## Reusing and Learning from Previous Submissions

Over time, your bid library becomes one of your most valuable assets.

- **Save every submission.** Many applications ask for the same foundational information -- your organisation's background, governance, safeguarding policies, and financial standing. Having these responses ready saves significant time.
- **Build a bank of PQQ responses.** Pre-Qualification Questionnaires often share common questions. A well-maintained library lets you respond quickly and consistently.
- **Request feedback on unsuccessful bids.** Most funders provide feedback. Use it to identify patterns and improve your next submission.
