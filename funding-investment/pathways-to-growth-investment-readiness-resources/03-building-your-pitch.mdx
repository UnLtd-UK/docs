## Preparing Before You Fundraise

### Avoid the Cold Approach

Investors are overwhelmed. Avoid having them discover your business for the first time on the day you are fundraising. Instead, bring them in much earlier.

You will meet potential investors from day one of your start-up. Do not waste a valuable opportunity to build familiarity as you progress.

### Start a Founder's Newsletter

A regular email loaded with great news about your start-up is one of the most effective ways to build investor relationships. Add everyone you meet along the way:

- Friends and family
- Potential angels
- Corporate investors
- Start-up key opinion formers
- Press

### Structure Your Newsletter

Keep sending regular and consistent news about your start-up taking shape. Do not ask for money at this stage. Cover these areas:

- **Product and tech** -- development updates and milestones
- **Traction** -- growth metrics and user engagement
- **Team** -- new hires, role changes, capacity building
- **Asks** -- requests for introductions, expertise, or advice

### Send It Consistently

Aim for monthly, or at least once per quarter. By the time you need money, potential investors will have heard from you 3 to 12 times already.

### What This Achieves

After receiving great updates for the past 3 to 12 months, your potential investors will:

1. Know who you are, your background, and your start-up story
2. Be familiar with your business, your product, and have seen it grow
3. Be fully primed to your narrative and your vision

Now is the time to ask for investment.

### Example Newsletter Structure

**Opening:** Short niceties and a reminder of the newsletter's purpose.

**Section 1 -- Highlight Your Traction:**
Show excellent growth KPIs. For example: monthly visits, unique visitors, month-over-month growth rates, key indicators like direct traffic, SEO visitors, and subscriber numbers.

**Section 2 -- Technology Update:**
Make sure potential investors know how you are investing in tech. Cover new launches, platform improvements, and infrastructure upgrades.

**Section 3 -- Product Growth:**
Share production metrics, new content formats, and expanding offerings that demonstrate your product is maturing.

**Section 4 -- Team Changes:**
Showcase hiring, growth, and new responsibilities that demonstrate investment in your team.

**Section 5 -- Ask for Help:**
Leverage your newsletter recipients' networks. Ask for specific expertise in areas you are expanding into, such as:

- Industry-specific best practices
- Strategic partnerships
- Monetisation and yield management experience

**Closing:** Thank recipients and confirm when the next edition will arrive. Stay consistent.

## What to Include in Your Pitch Deck

Your pitch deck is the single most important document for communicating your investment case. Here is a proven structure for social enterprises and impact-driven ventures.

### 1. Introduction (Why You)

Tell people who you are, why you are here, and your motivation. This is your chance to connect personally with investors and show your passion for the mission.

### 2. Impact

- Who are your beneficiaries?
- What are the unique needs, challenges, or barriers faced by those beneficiaries?
- How will your programme, service, or product address those needs?

### 3. Market Size and Opportunity

- Identify two or three problems your programme, product, or services will tackle
- Identify market size and opportunity in measurable numbers

### 4. Solution

- Present a clear and concise solution that investors can easily follow

### 5. Programme, Service, or Product

- Show off the actual product or service your business is selling
- Demonstrate demand and how you deliver value

### 6. Traction (History of Trading)

- Focus on the growth you have already experienced
- Highlight major milestones achieved and next steps for your business

### 7. Team

- Introduce your core team members and their relevant experience
- Introduce your board and advisors

### 8. Competition and Peers

- Define your competition
- Explain why you are different and what your competitive advantage is

### 9. Financials (Headline)

- Show how your business makes and spends money in service of social impact and business growth
- Show how investment and your pipeline of customers will grow your income

### 10. Investment and Use of Funds

- Tell investors what you need from them and why
- Be specific about the amount, the type of capital, and what you will use it for

## Understanding Angel Investors

Angel investors are individuals who invest their own money into early-stage ventures. They often bring more than just capital -- they can provide mentorship, industry connections, and strategic guidance.

For social enterprises, angel investors who are aligned with your mission can be particularly valuable partners. They tend to be more patient with returns and more understanding of the dual mission of financial sustainability and social impact.

### Key Takeaway

Build your founder's newsletter, add every potential investor you meet along the way, and send it once a month packed with updates on your progress. By the time you fundraise, your investors will already be familiar with your story and primed to invest. Pair this ongoing relationship-building with a well-structured pitch deck that clearly communicates your impact, your business model, and your investment ask.
