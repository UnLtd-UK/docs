## What Is a Theory of Change?

A Theory of Change explains the changes you hope to bring about (outcomes) for your beneficiaries and how you intend to do this (activities). It should be transparent, logical, and accessible, and is often expressed as a diagram.

## How a Theory of Change Works

A Theory of Change typically follows this flow:

| Inputs | Activities | Outputs | Outcomes |
|--------|-----------|---------|----------|
| Staff time, beneficiaries, workshop materials, volunteers | e.g. creative writing workshops with young people | e.g. number of young people worked with, number of workshops delivered | e.g. young person's writing skills improve, increased interest in literature, increased self-confidence |

All of these connect to your overarching **Mission**.

## Why It Matters

A well-developed Theory of Change:

- Articulates what you hope to achieve and how
- Helps identify assumptions in your activities
- Forms a basis for claims around attribution of outcomes
- Determines what changes need to be measured
- Identifies or hypothesises a rationale for causality
- Appeals to your funders and helps in their funding decisions
- Demonstrates to investors that your social impact model is documented, realistic, and impactful

## Building Your Theory of Change

### The Process

- Use existing evidence as your starting point
- Keep it simple -- clarity is more persuasive than complexity
- Run a workshop with your team
- Acknowledge limitations and complexities
- Get a wide range of stakeholders involved (trustees, staff, volunteers, beneficiaries)
- Treat it as a work in progress and update it to reflect practical experience and new evidence

### Refining Your Theory of Change

- Separate outcomes into short, medium, and long term
- Decide what outcomes you will measure and how
- Make clear any unverified assumptions
- Connect activities and outcomes to existing academic evidence

## Worked Example

**Problem Statements:**
- Youth offenders lack opportunities to engage with drama as a means to improve their soft skills
- Absence of theatre provision in the local area

**Mission:** Improve life chances of young people through theatre

| Activities | Outputs | Short-term Outcomes | Medium-term Outcomes | Long-term Outcomes |
|-----------|---------|---------------------|----------------------|--------------------|
| Drama workshops for young people who have been through the justice system | 1 workshop per week; End of term production; Beneficiary self-evaluation | Greater sense of routine for beneficiaries; Fewer exclusions amongst participants | Improved levels of self-confidence; Development of empathy-related behaviours | Re-engagement with formal education provision or employment |
| Mentoring programme -- beneficiaries go into schools to engage younger people at risk of exclusion in drama | 1 mentoring session per week | | | |

**Key Assumptions:**
- Drama workshops contribute to development of self-confidence and empathy
- Re-engagement with formal education provision can be linked to drama workshops (attribution)
- Exclusion can be linked to success of mentoring programme (attribution)

**Areas for Development:**
- Identifying rigorous evidence base for social outcomes associated with drama workshops
- Identifying longer-term outcomes for mentoring programme
