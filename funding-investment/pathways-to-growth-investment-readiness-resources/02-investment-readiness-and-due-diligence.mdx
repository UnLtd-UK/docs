## What Does It Mean to Be Investment Ready?

Being investment ready means you have a proposition that is worth investing in and you know what you are asking for, with the documentation and evidence to back up your plan. It is a mixture of knowing what you are asking for, what you can reasonably expect from the market based on your size and past performance, and how all of this is packaged up and presented to investors.

## Types of Investment

There are two main types of social investment:

**1. Borrowing (Debt):** Taking out a loan which you agree to repay over a set period of time. Most debt investments are paid back with interest. For example, an investor loans your organisation 10,000 pounds and you repay a total of 11,000 pounds at 229 pounds per month over 4 years.

**2. Shares (Equity):** Selling shares in your organisation to an investor. Equity investors receive a share of any profits paid out and get to have a say in how the organisation is run. For example, an investor pays 10,000 pounds to own 10% of your organisation.

## Core Questions Investors Will Ask

### Establishing Your Investment Ask

In nearly all investment conversations, you will be asked these questions:

- **How much do you need?**
- **What form of capital do you need it in?** (debt / equity / revenue share / mix)
- **Why do you need it?**
- **What round is this?** And of how many anticipated rounds? What do the future rounds look like?
- **Who are you looking to raise from** (type of investor) and what is the size / interest from your network right now?

### Establishing Your Proposition and Opportunity

Having convincing, emotive, and robust answers to these questions is crucial:

- **Why are you addressing this problem?** Size the opportunity and include a cost to those experiencing the pain.
- **Why have you proposed this solution?** What have you built to tackle this problem and why is this your approach?
- **Why now?** What has changed in the market, technology, or human behaviour to make now the right time?
- **Why you?** What is so special about you and your team that will allow you to win?
- **Do you have a profitable revenue model?** If not, do you already have a committed sales pipeline?

## In-Depth Self-Assessment Questions

You do not need answers to all of these questions to be investment ready, but you should probably have answers for at least 75% of them.

### Plans for Growth

- What are your plans for growth? What is the vision of scale? Is this appropriate for the market?
- What pace of scaling will you adopt and why is this appropriate?
- Are there crucial pieces of the jigsaw missing that need to be in place to achieve this vision?
- Are the presented sales and cashflow forecasts sensible and sustainable? Are you overly reliant on just one or two large clients?
- Is there a clear and sensible runway for launch of new products, services, or markets?
- What do you feel are the main risks in the growth plan? What are you doing to mitigate them?
- Have clear and achievable milestones for operational and organisational growth been presented?
- To what extent is the financial growth clearly tied to the scaling of social impact?
- What are the long-term plans for growth? Do you want to position this to be bought out or are you in it for the long term?

### Need for Support

- If partner organisations are required for routes to market or delivery, are the partnerships in place and sufficiently strong, credible, and scalable?
- Does your organisation have additional external supporters (e.g. accelerator programmes, advisors, or networks)?
- Who are the existing investors and/or board members and what experience do they bring?

### Business Model

- Is your business model documented and proven?
- What are the major risks with the business model? How are they being mitigated?
- Are there barriers to entry and/or a clear competitive advantage?
- Are the presented sales and cashflow forecasts sensible and sustainable?
- Does all relevant IP reside cleanly with the venture?
- What is to stop a bigger player entering the market?
- Is the model truly scalable? Do the margins increase as the turnover grows?
- How is performance monitored, reported, and reviewed?
- Is there a clear evidence or track record of selling sustainably?
- What history do the individuals involved have of successfully hitting sales targets?

### Social Impact

- Is the social impact model (Theory of Change) documented, realistic, and impactful?
- Are the operational and measurement process plans clear and realistic?
- Is there a social mission lock in place? Is this appropriate for the business?
- How do you currently collect impact data? Is this appropriate?
- Is there a positive trend on impact metrics?
- Is the Theory of Change being executed as intended?

### People and Team

- Is the chosen plan for growth realistic given your team's skills and track record?
- What are the founders' strengths and what do they bring to the organisation?
- What are the key areas where you have challenges or skill gaps?
- How can you demonstrate focus and commitment while being flexible to changing circumstances?
- Are you or your team sufficiently financially literate?
- How much are the key management getting paid? Are all costs truly reflected in projections?
- Are there skill gaps? Does management recognise and plan to fill them?
- What is the quality of the board or advisers? How active are they?
- How has the organisation managed to develop its brand and reputation?

### Financial Questions

- Comment on any significant information in the balance sheet
- **Creditors:** Does the venture have the liquid assets to pay outstanding amounts? Can it make monthly payments when due?
- **Debtors:** If debtor amounts represent more than 60 days of sales, question the ability to collect payment
- Why are you asking for this much? What is your company's valuation?
- What are your future investment needs and why?

## Preparing for Due Diligence

### What Is Due Diligence?

Due diligence is what investors call the process of assessing and evaluating a potential investee organisation to establish the opportunity (and related risks) of investing in them.

To facilitate this assessment, investors need certain aspects of your organisation to be captured and formalised, such as a business plan, financial projections, and impact model.

If your organisation cannot meet these requirements, an investor is simply unable to determine your suitability for investment, and you may be precluded from investment regardless of your potential to succeed and generate meaningful impact.

### Six Areas Investors Evaluate

Investors evaluate six key areas, balancing risk against return:

| Area | Focus |
|------|-------|
| **Market Opportunity** | Demand, market size, competitive landscape |
| **Business Model and Product** | Revenue generation, product uniqueness and maturity |
| **Finance and Growth Plan** | Financial health, cash flows, projections |
| **Social Impact** | Mission alignment, beneficiary outcomes, measurement |
| **Team, Ops and Governance** | Leadership capability, operational systems, accountability |
| **Policies (inc. DEI)** | Regulatory compliance, responsible business practices |

### Preparing for Each Area

**Market Opportunity:** Understand whether you can scale your impact, based on clear market demand and differentiation from competitors. Discuss customer needs and segments, the size and growth potential of your market, and the competition.

**Business Model and Product:** Assess how your organisation generates revenue and spends money in service of your impact and financial goals. Evaluate the uniqueness, demand, and maturity of your product or service.

**Finance and Growth Plan:** Examine the past, present, and future financial health and sustainability of your organisation, including your cash flows, revenue streams, and cost structures. This also involves discussing your projected financials and associated growth plan.

**Social Impact:** Understand your mission, beneficiaries, and target outcomes to achieve social impact objectives. Discuss how you plan to measure your impact and review past achievements. Understand how impact goals align with financial growth goals and track progress over time.

**Policies (inc. DEI):** Review your organisation's operational policies, considering DEI and compliance with regulatory standards. Understand commitment to responsible business practices and highlight risks to be addressed before or after investment.

**Team, Ops and Governance:** Assess your leadership team's lived and learned experience, skills, and ability to carry out the organisation's mission effectively. This includes evaluating how well you manage operations, the systems you use to allocate resources, and the governance structure that ensures accountability, transparency, and alignment with social impact goals.

### Due Diligence Document Checklist

| Area | Required Documents |
|------|--------------------|
| **Market Opportunity** | Competitor analysis (competition, differentiation, barriers to entry); Market size and growth potential |
| **Business Model and Product** | Business model (what you offer, to whom, at what price and cost); Maturity and uniqueness of your product or service |
| **Finance and Growth Plan** | Growth plan and use of investment; Historical and forecast financials; Confirmation or evidence of revenue |
| **Social Impact** | Theory of Change (beneficiaries, programmes, social impact); Approach and plans to scale, monitor, and evaluate impact |
| **Policies (inc. DEI)** | Policies and procedures complying with industry regulations (e.g. Safeguarding, Data Protection, Health and Safety) |
| **Team, Ops and Governance** | Organisation chart; Key staff and Board members' profiles; Example Board or Advisory packs |
