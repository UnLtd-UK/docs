## Why financial planning matters for investment

Investors need to see that you understand your numbers. A credible financial model and a well-maintained cash flow forecast demonstrate that you can manage money responsibly, plan for growth, and repay investment. These tools are essential components of your investment readiness documentation.

## 5-Year Financial Model

This Excel workbook provides a detailed financial model template designed for social enterprises and charities seeking investment. It covers a 5-year projection period.

### Workbook structure

The model contains six sheets:

### 1. Dashboard

A summary view of key financial metrics, populated from the other sheets. Use this for presenting to investors.

### 2. Assumptions

The core input sheet where you define:

- **General assumptions:** Model start date, duration (60 months / 5 years), fiscal year definitions, price inflation, and cost inflation rates.
- **Revenue assumptions:** Pipeline assumptions, conversion rates, number of customers won, contract tiers, and pricing by revenue stream.
- **Cost assumptions:** Cost of sales, overhead costs, and staffing costs.

### 3. Monthly cashflow (best case)

A month-by-month cashflow projection over 60 months covering:

| Category | Details |
|----------|---------|
| **Revenue** | Revenue streams with seasonality adjustments |
| **Cost of sales** | Direct costs associated with revenue |
| **Overhead costs** | Additional staff costs and other overheads |
| **EBITDA** | Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortisation |
| **Cash position** | Beginning cash, cash from operations, cash from investments, closing cash |

### 4. Monthly cashflow (worst case)

A conservative scenario with reduced revenue assumptions and adjusted cost projections, following the same structure as the best case. Having both scenarios shows investors you have thought through different outcomes.

### 5. Annual summary

A year-by-year view including one year of historic data, one budget year, and five forecast years:

- Revenue by stream with growth rates.
- Cost of sales with growth rates.
- Key metrics including gross profit and margin.
- Other costs (staffing, accounting, ancillary costs).

### 6. Staffing example

Detailed staffing projections covering payroll and volunteer costs, office costs, staff and volunteer training, equipment costs, and headcount growth.

### How to use the financial model

1. Start with the **Assumptions** sheet and enter your organisation's specific data.
2. The **Monthly Cashflow** sheets will populate automatically from the assumptions.
3. Enter historic figures manually in the **Annual Summary** sheet.
4. Customise the **Staffing Example** sheet with your actual and projected team.
5. Use the **Dashboard** for presenting to investors.

## 12-Month Cash Flow Forecast and Actual Template

This spreadsheet template helps you track monthly cash flow by comparing forecasted figures against actual results over a 12-month period.

### Key terms

| Term | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Beginning total cash** | Total cash including operating and investment activity at the start of the month |
| **Total expenses** | Total of all cash spent on operating activities |
| **Net cash from operations** | Operating cash at the beginning of the month plus all income minus all expenses |
| **Capital and interest loan payments** | Payment made to pay down the organisation's loan obligations |
| **Ending cash** | Net cash from operations minus loan payment |

### Template structure

The workbook contains two sheets:

**Explanation sheet** -- Provides guidelines for using the spreadsheet, including definitions of key terms.

**Year 1 Cashflow sheet** -- A 12-month cash flow grid with forecast and actual columns for each month, covering:

- **Income categories:** Income lines 1 through 6, with a total income row.
- **Expense categories:** Expense lines 1 through 9, with a total expenses row and net cash from operations.
- **Investment activity:** Investment in (positive number), capital and interest payments (negative number), and net cash from investment activity.
- **Summary:** Ending cash and monthly cash flow projection vs monthly actual variance.

### How to use the cash flow template

1. Open the spreadsheet and save a working copy.
2. Enter your organisation's name and date.
3. Rename the generic income and expense categories to match your organisation.
4. Enter forecast figures at the start of each month.
5. Enter actual figures as they become available.
6. Review variances to identify trends and adjust your planning.
