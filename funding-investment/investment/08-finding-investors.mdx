## What angel investors look for

Before you start reaching out, it helps to understand what investors are evaluating. While every angel investor and angel group has their own preferences, these are the most common criteria:

- **Founders that are focused.** Trying to do too many things at once is a red flag. Focus on what is going to sell now, then move on to the next idea.
- **Founders that are serious.** Angels do not invest in hobbies -- they invest in businesses. Show that you are committed.
- **Customer-driven businesses.** Will you go above and beyond to keep your customers satisfied? How? This is a critical factor.
- **SEIS/EIS eligible investments.** Most angel groups will not invest unless you are S/EIS eligible, so make sure you have your Advance Assurance sorted.
- **Positive change.** Some angel groups look specifically for companies that change the environment or society for the better.
- **Interest from customers.** You need to show that you have a competitive product or service that has already attracted customer interest.
- **Founders that know their stuff.** Know your market and know your competitors. Give investors a reason to trust that you know what you are doing.

## How to use these lists

Remember to research each investor to ensure alignment with your industry and funding stage before reaching out. A scattergun approach wastes your time and damages your reputation. Target investors who are actively investing in your sector, stage, and geography.
