## What is due diligence?

Due diligence is the process investors use to assess and evaluate a potential investee organisation. It helps them establish both the opportunity and the risks of investing in you.

To facilitate this assessment, investors need certain aspects of your organisation to be captured and formalised -- a business plan, financial projections, an impact model, and more. If you cannot meet these requirements, an investor simply cannot determine your suitability for investment, and your organisation may be passed over regardless of its potential to succeed and generate meaningful impact.

## The six key assessment areas

Investors assess six areas, balancing **risk** against **return**:

| Area | What investors evaluate |
|------|----------------------|
| **Market opportunity** | Can you scale your impact based on clear market demand? |
| **Business model and product** | How does your organisation generate revenue? |
| **Finance and growth plan** | What is your financial health and sustainability? |
| **Social impact** | Will you achieve the impact you claim? |
| **Team, operations, and governance** | Can your leadership deliver the mission? |
| **Policies (including DEI)** | Are you compliant with regulatory standards? |

## Preparing for each area

### Market opportunity

You need to demonstrate that you can scale your impact based on clear market demand and differentiation from competitors. Be ready to discuss:

- Customer needs and segments you serve.
- The size and growth potential of your target market.
- Your competitive landscape and what sets you apart.

### Business model and product

Investors want to understand how your organisation generates revenue and spends money in service of your impact and financial goals. Be prepared to explain:

- Your revenue model and cost structure.
- The uniqueness, demand, and maturity of your product or service.
- How your business model supports both financial sustainability and social impact.

### Finance and growth plan

This area covers the past, present, and future financial health of your organisation. You should be ready to present:

- Historical financial performance (cashflows, revenue streams, cost structures).
- Projected financials and the assumptions behind them.
- Your growth plan and how investment will be deployed.

### Social impact

Investors want to understand your mission, beneficiaries, and target outcomes. Be ready to discuss:

- How you plan to measure your impact and what tools you use.
- Your past achievements and whether you have met your impact targets.
- How your impact goals align with your financial growth goals.
- How you will track progress over time.

### Team, operations, and governance

Investors assess your leadership team's experience, skills, and ability to carry out the mission effectively. They will look at:

- How well your organisation manages its operations.
- The systems you use to allocate resources.
- Your governance structure and whether it ensures accountability, transparency, and alignment with your social impact goals.

### Policies (including DEI)

Investors review your operational policies, considering diversity, equity, and inclusion alongside regulatory compliance. You should be able to demonstrate:

- Commitment to responsible business practices.
- Compliance with relevant industry regulations.
- Awareness of any risks that need to be addressed before or after investment.

## Document and data checklist

Use this checklist to ensure you have everything prepared before entering due diligence:

| Area | Required documents |
|------|--------------------|
| **Market opportunity** | Competitor analysis (competition, differentiation, barriers to entry); market size and growth potential |
| **Business model and product** | Business model document (what you offer, to whom, at what price and cost); evidence of product or service maturity and uniqueness |
| **Finance and growth plan** | Growth plan and use of investment; historical and forecast financials; confirmation or evidence of revenue |
| **Social impact** | Theory of Change (beneficiaries, programmes, social impact); approach and plans to scale, monitor, and evaluate impact |
| **Policies (including DEI)** | Policies and procedures complying with industry regulations (e.g. safeguarding, data protection, health and safety) |
| **Team, operations, and governance** | Organisation chart; key staff and board members' profiles; example board or advisory packs |

## Tips for a smooth due diligence process

- **Start early.** Do not wait until an investor asks for documents. Build your due diligence pack proactively.
- **Be honest about gaps.** If something is not in place yet, show that you have a plan to address it.
- **Keep documents current.** Outdated financials or an old business plan will raise red flags.
- **Organise everything clearly.** Use a shared folder or data room so investors can find what they need quickly.
- **Prepare your team.** Make sure key team members can speak confidently about their areas of responsibility.
