## Why you should start building relationships now

Investors are overwhelmed with pitches and proposals. If the first time they hear about your business is the day you ask for money, you are starting from a disadvantage. The most effective fundraisers bring potential investors into their story much earlier.

You will meet potential investors from day one of your venture. Do not waste the opportunity to keep them engaged as you grow.

## Start a founder's newsletter

A founder's newsletter is a regular email packed with great news about your venture. It is not a fundraising ask -- it is a relationship-building tool. Add everyone you meet along the way to your list:

- Friends and family
- Potential angel investors
- Corporate investors
- Startup key opinion formers
- Press contacts

## What to cover in each edition

Keep sending regular and consistent news about your venture's progress. Do not ask for money at this stage. Focus on four areas:

| Area | What to include |
|------|----------------|
| **Product and tech** | Development updates, launches, architecture improvements |
| **Traction** | Growth KPIs, user metrics, traffic data |
| **Team** | New hires, team growth, expanded responsibilities |
| **Asks** | Requests for expertise, introductions, or advice (not money) |

## How often to send it

Send your newsletter every month, or at minimum once per quarter. By the time you need investment, potential investors will have heard from you three to twelve times already.

## Why this approach works

After receiving your updates for three to twelve months, potential investors will:

1. **Know who you are** -- your background, your story, and your motivations.
2. **Be familiar with your business** -- your product, your market, and your growth trajectory.
3. **Be fully primed to your narrative** -- your vision, your milestones, and your next steps.

When you do ask for money, the conversation starts from a position of familiarity and trust rather than cold outreach.

## Structuring each edition

### 1. Highlight your traction

Lead with your strongest growth KPIs. For example:

- Monthly visits and unique visitors.
- Month-over-month growth rates.
- Key health indicators (direct traffic, SEO visitors, subscriber counts).
- New acquisition channels you have opened.

### 2. Give a technology update

Show potential investors how you are investing in your technology:

- New platform launches (mobile apps, localised versions).
- Infrastructure improvements.
- Cost optimisations.

### 3. Showcase product growth

Demonstrate product development and increasing variety:

- Content production volume and quality.
- New formats and features.
- Community engagement metrics.

### 4. Highlight team changes

Hiring, promotions, and new responsibilities show momentum:

- New team members and their roles.
- Fundraising progress (if applicable and appropriate to share).

### 5. Ask for help

Use your newsletter to leverage the network you have built. Make specific, actionable requests for:

- **Expertise** in areas you are expanding into.
- **Introductions** to people with relevant experience.
- **Advice** on strategy and optimisation.

Close each edition with a reminder of when the next one will arrive -- and stay consistent.
