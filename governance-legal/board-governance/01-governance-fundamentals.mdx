## Why Good Governance Matters

Good governance is the foundation of every successful organisation. It provides the framework for accountability, transparency and strategic direction. Whether you are running a social enterprise, charity or community interest company, effective governance helps you:

- Build trust with stakeholders, funders and investors
- Make better strategic decisions
- Manage risk effectively
- Demonstrate accountability to your community and beneficiaries
- Attract and retain talented board members
- Meet legal and regulatory requirements

## Understanding Organisational Structure

Every organisation has three core layers, each with distinct roles:

### Ownership

This is where ultimate power sits. Depending on your legal structure, owners are either **shareholders** (in companies limited by shares) or **members** (in companies limited by guarantee and some other structures).

Owners control the mission and the board. They approve the Articles of Association, elect directors, and have the final say on major decisions at general meetings.

### Strategic Direction

The **board of directors** (or trustees) is responsible for setting the strategic direction of the organisation. Directors are elected by owners and are legally responsible for the company's sustainability. They appoint the CEO or Managing Director and hold them accountable.

### Operations

The **CEO and staff** manage day-to-day operations and deliver the organisation's social impact. Employees, volunteers, beneficiaries and funders all work at this level.

<Note>
As a founder, you may occupy multiple roles simultaneously --- owner, director, employee, volunteer, beneficiary and even funder. Understanding where each role sits in the organisational structure helps you manage boundaries and avoid conflicts.
</Note>

## Shareholders vs Members

The ownership model you choose is a fundamental and **irreversible** decision.

| Feature | Shareholders (CLS) | Members (CLG) |
|---|---|---|
| **Ownership basis** | Shares --- can be bought, sold and transferred | Membership --- cannot be sold |
| **Voting power** | Depends on percentage of shares owned | One member, one vote (always equal) |
| **Dividends** | Can receive dividends if the board approves | Cannot receive dividends |
| **Types** | People or organisations | People or organisations |
| **Power to change** | Depends on share class and % held | Equal power among all members |

### Share Classes and Rights

If you use a company limited by shares, you can create different share classes in your Articles of Association. Shares have three main rights that can be varied:

- **Dividends** --- the right to a share of profits
- **Voting at general meetings** --- the right to vote on resolutions
- **Electing directors** --- the right to choose who sits on the board

A **Golden Share** can be created to protect your social mission --- for example, a share that controls the social clause and asset lock but has no voting rights or right to dividends.

## Control Mechanisms

Good governance relies on several layers of control:

1. **Company Law** --- sets the legal framework all companies must follow
2. **Company Articles** --- your constitution, defining how the organisation is run
3. **Shareholders'/Members' Agreement** --- additional agreements between owners
4. **Company Policies** --- internal rules covering areas like conflicts of interest, data protection and expenses
5. **Contracts** --- agreements with employees, volunteers, suppliers and customers

## Key Principles of Good Governance

Every board should be guided by these principles:

- **Accountability** --- being answerable to your stakeholders for decisions and actions
- **Transparency** --- being open about how decisions are made and resources are used
- **Probity** --- acting with integrity and honesty in all dealings
- **Focus on sustainability** --- balancing financial viability with social impact
- **Long-term thinking** --- prioritising the longer-term success of the organisation over short-term gains

## Going Above and Beyond

Effective governance is not just about compliance. The most successful boards go beyond the minimum requirements to:

- Regularly evaluate their own effectiveness
- Invest in board member training and development
- Actively seek diverse perspectives and backgrounds
- Challenge assumptions constructively
- Stay connected to the communities they serve
- Plan for succession and board renewal
