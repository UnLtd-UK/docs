## Planning Your Board Recruitment

Recruiting the right board members is one of the most important things you can do for your organisation. A diverse board with a wide range of skills, experience and perspectives strengthens resilience and gives your organisation the best chance of fulfilling its mission.

## Starting with a Skills Audit

Before you start recruiting, assess what your board currently has and what it needs. A **board skills audit** helps you identify gaps and prioritise your search.

### How to Conduct a Skills Audit

1. List the key skills, knowledge and experience your board needs (e.g. finance, legal, marketing, sector expertise, lived experience)
2. Ask each current director to self-assess against these areas
3. Identify gaps between what you have and what you need
4. Prioritise which gaps to fill first based on your strategic priorities

### Skills to Consider

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Financial** | Accounting, audit, investment, fundraising |
| **Legal** | Corporate law, employment law, regulatory compliance |
| **Sector knowledge** | Your industry, social enterprise, charity sector |
| **Strategic** | Business planning, growth strategy, risk management |
| **Technical** | IT, digital, data, cybersecurity |
| **People** | HR, leadership development, diversity and inclusion |
| **Community** | Lived experience, local knowledge, beneficiary perspective |

## Creating a Board Recruitment Policy

A formal recruitment policy demonstrates good governance and helps ensure fairness and transparency. Your policy should cover:

- **Purpose** --- why you recruit to the board and what you are looking for
- **Process** --- how candidates are identified, assessed and appointed
- **Eligibility** --- any requirements or restrictions (e.g. eligibility declaration, conflict checks)
- **Diversity commitment** --- how you will actively seek diverse candidates
- **Term of office** --- how long directors serve and whether terms can be renewed
- **Induction** --- how new directors will be onboarded

## The Recruitment Process

### Step 1: Define the Role

Write a clear **job description** and **person specification** covering:

- The organisation's background and mission
- The board's role and how it operates
- Specific responsibilities of the role
- Skills, experience and qualities you are looking for
- Time commitment expected (meetings per year, preparation time, committee work)
- Term of appointment
- Any remuneration or expenses

### Step 2: Advertise Widely

Do not rely solely on personal networks. Cast a wide net to attract diverse candidates:

- Post on your website and social media
- Use board recruitment platforms such as [Getting on Board](https://www.gettingonboard.org), [Reach Volunteering](https://reachvolunteering.org.uk) and [Trustees Unlimited](https://www.trusteesunlimited.co.uk)
- Ask existing board members and partners to share the opportunity
- Approach professional networks, industry bodies and diversity organisations
- Contact local chambers of commerce, business networks and community groups

### Step 3: Create a Recruitment Pack

Your recruitment pack should include:

- A **cover sheet** with your branding and the pack title
- A **summary sheet** with your organisation's background, achievements, future aims and what you are looking for
- The **job description** and person specification
- Your **NED code of conduct**
- Details of the **application process**, including deadlines and interview dates

### Step 4: Assess and Interview

- Review applications against the person specification
- Shortlist candidates and invite them for interview
- Use a consistent interview format with questions aligned to your criteria
- Consider a meet-and-greet with existing board members or staff
- Check references and complete any eligibility declarations

### Step 5: Appoint and Induct

- Formally appoint the new director at a board meeting
- Complete Companies House filing
- Provide a thorough induction covering the organisation's strategy, finances, governance structure, key policies and current challenges
- Assign a "board buddy" to help them settle in

## Building a Diverse Board

Diversity is not just about fairness --- it leads to better decision-making. Boards that include people with different backgrounds, experiences and perspectives are more likely to identify risks, spot opportunities and understand the communities they serve.

### Practical Steps for Diversity

- Make diversity an explicit part of your recruitment policy
- Use inclusive language in your job descriptions and adverts
- Advertise through channels that reach under-represented groups
- Consider removing barriers such as requiring extensive governance experience (which may exclude younger or less privileged candidates)
- Be flexible about meeting times and formats
- Offer training and development to help new directors build confidence
- Track and report on board diversity
