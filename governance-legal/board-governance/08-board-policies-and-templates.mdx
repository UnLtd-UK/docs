## Essential Governance Documents

Every well-governed organisation needs a suite of core documents that set out how the board operates. This page provides guidance on the key policies and templates you should have in place.

## Conflict of Interest Policy

A conflict of interest arises when a director's personal interests could, or could appear to, influence their judgement or decision-making on behalf of the organisation.

### What Your Policy Should Cover

- **Definition** of what constitutes a conflict of interest (financial interests, personal relationships, outside appointments, gifts and hospitality)
- **Declaration process** --- how and when directors must declare conflicts
- **Register of interests** --- a formal record maintained by the company secretary or chair
- **Management procedures** --- what happens when a conflict is identified (e.g. the director may need to leave the room during discussion and voting)
- **Annual review** --- all directors should review and update their declarations at least annually

### Managing Conflicts in Practice

When a conflict is declared at a board meeting:

1. The director declares the nature and extent of their interest
2. The chair decides whether the conflict is material to the discussion
3. If material, the director withdraws from the discussion and does not vote
4. The withdrawal and the reason are recorded in the minutes
5. The declaration is added to the register of interests

## Articles of Association

Your Articles of Association are the primary constitutional document for your company. They set out the rules that members, shareholders and directors must follow.

### Key Contents

- The company's objects (its purposes)
- Membership or shareholding provisions
- Directors' powers and responsibilities
- How directors are appointed and removed
- Board meeting procedures (quorum, voting, chair's casting vote)
- General meeting procedures
- Provisions for changing the Articles

### Model Articles

Companies House provides model articles that apply by default. However, most organisations benefit from customised articles. Two common templates are:

- **Model articles for companies limited by shares** --- suitable for social enterprises using a share-based ownership model
- **Model articles for companies limited by guarantee** --- suitable for charities, membership organisations and social enterprises using a membership model

### Changing Your Articles

Changes to Articles of Association require a **special resolution** (75% majority) at a general meeting. The process is:

1. Draft the proposed changes
2. Give proper notice to all members/shareholders (usually 14 days minimum)
3. Hold a general meeting and pass the special resolution
4. File the amended Articles with Companies House within 15 days

An alternative is a **written resolution**, which can be circulated to members for signature without holding a meeting. Written resolutions require the same 75% threshold for special resolutions.

## Board Skills Audit Template

A skills audit helps you identify gaps in your board's collective expertise.

### How to Use It

Create a matrix with skills categories across the top and directors' names down the side. Ask each director to rate their expertise in each area on a scale (e.g. 1 = limited, 2 = some experience, 3 = expert).

### Suggested Categories

| Category | Skills to Assess |
|---|---|
| **Finance** | Accounting, audit, investment, fundraising, financial planning |
| **Legal** | Corporate law, employment law, contracts, intellectual property |
| **Strategy** | Business planning, growth strategy, risk management, change management |
| **Operations** | Project management, service delivery, quality assurance |
| **People** | HR, recruitment, leadership development, diversity and inclusion |
| **Marketing** | Communications, digital marketing, public relations, stakeholder engagement |
| **Sector** | Industry knowledge, social enterprise, charity sector, public sector |
| **Technology** | IT strategy, digital transformation, data protection, cybersecurity |
| **Governance** | Board experience, regulatory compliance, governance frameworks |
| **Community** | Lived experience, local knowledge, community engagement |

## Board Expenses Policy

Directors should not be out of pocket for fulfilling their board duties. A clear expenses policy covers:

- What expenses can be claimed (travel, accommodation, subsistence, childcare to attend meetings)
- Rates and limits (e.g. standard class travel, maximum accommodation rates)
- How to submit claims (forms, receipts, deadlines)
- Approval process

## Board Diversity Policy

A diversity policy for board recruitment should set out:

- Your commitment to building a board that reflects the diversity of your community
- Specific actions you will take (e.g. advertising through diverse networks, removing unnecessary barriers)
- How you will monitor and report on progress
- Any targets or aspirations for board composition
