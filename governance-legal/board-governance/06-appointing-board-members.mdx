## The Appointment Process

Appointing new board members is a formal process with legal implications. This page covers the practical steps from nomination through to registration, whether you are appointing non-executive directors to a company board or trustees to a charity.

## Eligibility Requirements

Before anyone can be appointed as a director, they must meet certain eligibility criteria.

### Legal Requirements

A person **cannot** serve as a company director if they:

- Are an undischarged bankrupt (unless they have court permission)
- Are subject to a director disqualification order
- Are under 16 years of age

### Eligibility Declaration

It is good practice to ask prospective board members to complete an **eligibility declaration form** before appointment. This should confirm that the candidate:

- Is not disqualified from acting as a director
- Is not an undischarged bankrupt
- Has not been convicted of an offence involving dishonesty or deception
- Does not have any unspent criminal convictions that would be relevant
- Is willing to comply with the organisation's code of conduct and conflict of interest policy
- Understands and accepts the legal duties of a director

## Methods of Appointment

### Election by Members/Shareholders

The standard method in most organisations. Candidates are nominated and elected by the members or shareholders at a general meeting. Your Articles of Association will specify:

- How nominations are made
- How many directors can be appointed
- Whether there is a maximum or minimum number
- The voting process and any quorum requirements

### Co-option by the Board

Some Articles allow the existing board to co-opt (directly appoint) new directors between general meetings. Co-opted directors typically serve until the next annual general meeting, at which point they must stand for election by the members.

### Nomination Process

For a structured nomination process:

1. Issue a **nomination form** explaining the role and asking candidates to provide their background, skills and reasons for wanting to join
2. Set a clear deadline for nominations
3. Review nominations against the skills gaps identified in your board skills audit
4. Interview shortlisted candidates
5. Present recommended candidates to the board or membership for approval

## Specific Considerations by Organisation Type

### Companies (CLS and CLG)

- Directors must be registered with Companies House within 14 days of appointment
- You will need their full name, date of birth, nationality, occupation and a service address
- Directors must also provide their usual residential address (kept on a private register)

### Charities

- Trustees must meet the Charity Commission's eligibility requirements
- In England and Wales, certain people are automatically disqualified (e.g. those with unspent convictions for offences involving deception or dishonesty)
- You should carry out a DBS check where appropriate
- Notify the Charity Commission of changes to your trustees

### Scottish Charitable Incorporated Organisations (SCIOs)

- Trustees are registered with the Office of the Scottish Charity Regulator (OSCR)
- OSCR must be notified of changes to the charity trustees

### Community Interest Companies (CICs)

- Follow the same Companies House registration process as other companies
- Ensure new directors understand the CIC's community interest statement and asset lock

## After Appointment

Once a new board member has been formally appointed:

- **Register the appointment** with Companies House, the Charity Commission or OSCR as appropriate
- **Update your records** including the register of directors and persons with significant control
- **Provide an induction pack** including Articles of Association, recent board minutes, financial statements, strategic plan, key policies and a list of current directors and staff
- **Arrange an induction meeting** with the chair, CEO and key staff members
- **Set up practical access** to board papers, email lists and any governance portals
- **Schedule an initial review** after three to six months to check how they are settling in
