## Your Legal Responsibilities as a Director

When you become a director of a company, you take on significant legal responsibilities. These duties apply equally to all directors --- whether executive or non-executive, paid or unpaid. Understanding these duties is essential to fulfilling your role effectively and avoiding personal liability.

## Statutory Duties Under the Companies Act 2006

The Companies Act 2006 sets out seven general duties that every director must observe:

### Duty to Act Within Powers

You must act in accordance with your company's Articles of Association and only exercise your powers for the purposes for which they were given.

### Duty to Promote the Success of the Company

You must act in the way you consider, in good faith, would be most likely to promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole. In doing so, you should have regard to:

- The likely consequences of any decision in the long term
- The interests of the company's employees
- The need to foster business relationships with suppliers, customers and others
- The impact of operations on the community and the environment
- The desirability of maintaining a reputation for high standards of business conduct
- The need to act fairly between members of the company

### Duty to Exercise Independent Judgement

You must exercise your own independent judgement and not simply follow the instructions of others (including other directors or shareholders) without applying your own mind to the decision.

### Duty to Exercise Reasonable Care, Skill and Diligence

You must exercise the care, skill and diligence that would be exercised by a reasonably diligent person with both the general knowledge, skill and experience that may reasonably be expected of a person carrying out your role, and any additional knowledge, skill and experience that you actually have.

### Duty to Avoid Conflicts of Interest

You must avoid situations where you have, or could have, a direct or indirect interest that conflicts with the interests of the company.

### Duty Not to Accept Benefits from Third Parties

You must not accept benefits from third parties that are conferred because of your position as a director or because of anything you do or do not do as a director.

### Duty to Declare Interests in Transactions

If you have any interest, direct or indirect, in a proposed transaction or arrangement with the company, you must declare the nature and extent of that interest to the other directors.

## Practical Responsibilities

Beyond the statutory duties, directors are responsible for:

- **Confidentiality** --- maintaining the confidentiality of board discussions and company information
- **Time and attention** --- devoting sufficient time to the business and your role
- **Accountability to shareholders/members** --- reporting to and being answerable to owners
- **Stewardship of assets** --- protecting and managing company assets responsibly
- **Stewardship of impact** --- ensuring the organisation delivers on its social mission
- **Financial records** --- maintaining proper books of accounts
- **Filing obligations** --- ensuring accounts, annual returns and tax returns are filed on time
- **Statutory books** --- maintaining the company's statutory registers
- **Legal compliance** --- ensuring the company complies with all relevant legislation

## Executive vs Non-Executive Directors

While all directors share the same legal duties, their day-to-day roles differ significantly:

| Aspect | Executive Directors | Non-Executive Directors |
|---|---|---|
| **Involvement** | Hands-on, day-to-day | Hands-off, strategic oversight |
| **Independence** | Not independent | Independent |
| **Time commitment** | Full-time | Part-time |
| **Employment** | Employed by the company | Self-employed / service agreement |
| **Primary role** | Strategy development and execution | Challenge, monitor and advise |
| **Knowledge** | Internal, operational | External, broader perspective |

### What Non-Executive Directors Are Not

It is important to understand the boundaries:

- **NEDs are not advisers.** They participate in board decisions with equal responsibility --- they do not simply advise from the sidelines
- **NEDs are not mentors.** While they may provide guidance, they must maintain independence and cannot become too close to individuals in the business

## Directors' Insurance

Directors can be personally liable for breaches of duty. **Directors' and officers' (D&O) insurance** covers the costs of defending against claims of wrongful acts, including breaches of trust, misleading statements, neglect and wrongful trading.

It is strongly recommended that all directors ensure appropriate D&O insurance is in place.

## When Things Go Wrong

Directors who breach their duties may face:

- Personal financial liability for losses caused to the company
- Disqualification from acting as a director
- Criminal prosecution in serious cases (e.g. fraudulent trading)
- Reputational damage

The best protection is to understand your duties, act in good faith, keep proper records of decisions and seek professional advice when needed.
