## What Non-Executive Directors Do

Non-executive directors (NEDs) play a vital role in the governance of any organisation. They bring independent oversight, external expertise and strategic challenge to the board without being involved in day-to-day operations.

Despite the "non-executive" label, NEDs have the **same legal duties and responsibilities** as executive directors. They are registered at Companies House and are equally accountable for the company's actions.

## Why Your Organisation Needs NEDs

Having non-executive directors on your board signals maturity and credibility. NEDs help you:

- **Support growth** --- bring strategic expertise to help scale your business
- **Satisfy stakeholders** --- funders, investors and large customers expect independent board oversight
- **Provide skip-level reporting** --- offer a channel for issues to surface that might not reach the founder directly
- **Challenge constructively** --- ask difficult questions and test assumptions
- **Bring external perspective** --- offer knowledge and networks from outside your organisation

## Qualities of an Effective NED

The best non-executive directors bring:

- Broad business experience across different sectors or disciplines
- Ability to assess people and situations analytically
- Ability to both hold their ground and persuade others
- Financial literacy and experience
- Awareness of the legal environment and directors' duties
- Familiarity with best practice in board governance (particularly for SMEs and social enterprises)
- Ability to act as a sounding board
- Sense of humour and humility

## Becoming a Non-Executive Director

If you are considering becoming a NED, there are several reasons it might be right for you:

- To gain experience from the other side of the board table to complement your executive or consulting career
- To provide insight into board responsibilities, governance and processes
- To make use of your expertise and contacts to support an organisation whose mission you care about
- To develop new skills in a different sector or industry
- To give back and contribute to your community

### Before You Commit

Consider these questions:

- Do you have sufficient time to dedicate to the role (typically 1--2 days per month plus preparation)?
- Are you genuinely interested in the organisation's mission and sector?
- Can you bring skills or experience that the board currently lacks?
- Are you prepared to challenge the executive team constructively?
- Do you understand and accept the legal responsibilities?

## NED Code of Conduct

Non-executive directors should adhere to high standards of conduct. A typical NED code of conduct covers:

### Independence and Integrity
- Act honestly and in the best interests of the organisation at all times
- Maintain independence of judgement and do not allow personal interests to conflict with the organisation's interests
- Declare any conflicts of interest promptly and fully

### Confidentiality
- Treat all board discussions, papers and company information as strictly confidential
- Do not use confidential information for personal advantage or to benefit third parties

### Commitment
- Attend all board meetings and relevant committee meetings
- Prepare thoroughly by reading board papers in advance
- Devote sufficient time to understand the organisation's business and the issues it faces

### Constructive Challenge
- Contribute constructively to board discussions
- Challenge the executive team and fellow directors where necessary
- Support collective decisions once they have been made, even if you disagreed during discussion

### Compliance
- Comply with the organisation's Articles of Association, policies and procedures
- Comply with all relevant laws and regulations
- Complete any required declarations, training or development

## The NED Service Agreement

Unlike executive directors who have employment contracts, NEDs typically operate under a **service agreement** (sometimes called a letter of appointment). This should cover:

- The term of appointment and any provisions for renewal
- Expected time commitment (days per month, board meetings per year)
- Remuneration (if any) and expenses policy
- Confidentiality obligations
- Conflict of interest procedures
- Notice period and termination provisions
- Insurance arrangements (D&O cover)

<Note>
In charities, directors (often called trustees) generally **cannot be paid** for their role. However, reasonable expenses can be reimbursed. Some charities operate a "unitary board" where certain directors are also paid employees.
</Note>

## Managing Your Board Relationship

If you are a founder working with NEDs:

- **Manage carefully** --- remember that NEDs are your equals on the board, not your subordinates
- **Do not chair the board yourself** --- the founder should not be the chair, as this compromises independent oversight
- **Know when to step back** --- both founders and NEDs need to recognise when it is time to move on from a board role
