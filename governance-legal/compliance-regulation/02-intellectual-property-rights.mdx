## Why Intellectual Property Matters

"Creations of the mind" --- such as designs, brand names, inventions, musical recordings, videos, written works and artworks --- are known as intellectual property (IP). As the owner of intellectual property, you have legal rights to prevent others from making unauthorised use of it.

Although IP is particularly important to creative businesses, almost every business has some form of intellectual property, whether it is a brand name, logo, or original content on a website or social media account.

## Types of Intellectual Property Rights in the UK

### Copyright

Copyright protects original works including:

- Literary, dramatic, musical and artistic work (including illustration and photography)
- Non-literary written work such as software, web content and databases
- Sound and music recordings
- Film and television recordings
- Broadcasts
- The layout of published editions of written, dramatic and musical works

Without the copyright owner's permission, a protected work must not be copied, distributed, performed or displayed in public, adapted, or uploaded to the internet.

Copyright arises **automatically** when an original work is created --- you do not need to register it.

### Patents

Patents protect inventions that are capable of being made or used industrially, such as a new device, material or manufacturing process. A patent prevents anyone from making, using, selling, importing or exporting your invention without your permission.

Patents are granted by the [Intellectual Property Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/intellectual-property-office).

### Design Rights

Design rights protect the unique appearance of a product created by features such as shape, colour and decoration.

| Type | What It Covers | Duration | Registration Required? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unregistered design right** | Three-dimensional features (shape and configuration) | Up to 15 years | No --- arises automatically |
| **Supplementary unregistered design (SUD)** | Two-dimensional features (colours, surface patterns) | 3 years | No --- arises automatically |
| **Registered design** | Both 2D and 3D features | Up to 25 years | Yes --- register with the IPO |

### Trade Marks

A trade mark is a recognisable word, phrase, symbol or design that distinguishes your goods or services from those of others. You can apply to register your trade mark with the Intellectual Property Office, which prevents anyone from using it in relation to similar goods and services without your permission.

Once registered, you can sell, franchise or license your trade mark to others.

### Database Rights

Databases can benefit from two types of protection:

- **Copyright** protects the selection or arrangement of the database contents
- **Database rights** protect the content itself, allowing you to stop others extracting or reusing all or a substantial part of it

A database does not need to be original to qualify for database rights, but there must have been "substantial investment" in obtaining, verifying or presenting the data.

## Protecting Your IP Abroad

Some UK intellectual property rights provide automatic international protection. For example, works protected by UK copyright are also protected in countries that are members of international conventions such as the Berne Convention and the Rome Convention.

However, UK patents, trade marks and design rights **do not** automatically protect you abroad. You must register them separately in each country where you need protection. Under various international agreements, you can usually make a single application covering multiple countries.

For more information, visit [Protecting your UK intellectual property abroad](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/protecting-your-uk-intellectual-property-abroad).
