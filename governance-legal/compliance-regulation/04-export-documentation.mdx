## Getting Your Export Documentation Right

The documentation required when sending goods overseas can seem complicated, but with the harmonisation of international trade documentation and standard terms, the process has become easier and more efficient. Getting your export documentation right is vital to ensure goods reach their destination on time and that you receive payment.

The United Nations Layout Key for Trade Documents provides a standard layout for export paperwork, so the same information appears in the same place on all documents. Standard trade terms known as **Incoterms** are also commonly used in international trade.

## Commercial Documents

### Commercial Invoice

A bill you prepare for the goods you are exporting. It acts as a basis for assessing any import duty the buyer may have to pay, and serves as evidence of goods' value for insurance claims. In some cases, it also forms a contract of sale. You must complete it according to the customs requirements of the destination country.

### Pro Forma Invoice

An invoice sent in advance of shipment. You may need this if your overseas customer has to obtain foreign currency allocation or an import licence before you dispatch the goods. Pro forma invoices are also used for commercial samples and free-of-charge consignments.

### Export Cargo Shipping Instruction (ECSI)

Your instruction to a freight forwarder or carrier, setting out terms and conditions for moving the goods and stating who is responsible at each stage.

## Official Documents

### Single Administrative Document (SAD)

An official document used for exports and imports passing between the EU and non-EU countries, including the UK. Also used for movement of non-EU goods within the EU. For more information, visit the [EU Customs overview](https://ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/business/customs-procedures/general-overview/single-administrative-document-sad_en).

### Export Licences

Required under the Export of Goods (Control) Orders for certain products such as drugs, chemicals, antiques, military-related items, high-tech industrial goods and scientific instruments. It is your responsibility to determine whether your goods need a licence. Guidance is available at [gov.uk/export-goods](https://www.gov.uk/export-goods).

### Certificates of Origin (CO)

Provide evidence that goods have been obtained, produced, manufactured or processed in a particular country. Required by some importing countries.

### Movement Certificates

Required when exporting goods from the EU to a country covered by EU trade agreements, ensuring preferential tariff treatment.

### Health Certificates

Often required for food, livestock, agricultural and horticultural products, issued by a state-appointed veterinary inspector or health board. In the UK, this is managed by [Defra](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/guidance-on-exporting-live-animals-or-animal-products).

### Clean Report of Findings

May be required by governments of certain importing countries (usually developing countries with restricted foreign currency). Issued by an independent inspection house to verify that goods match the pro forma invoice in quality, price and quantity.

### Import Licences

Individual countries have their own specific requirements. Many require import licences for certain items, and some prohibit certain goods entirely. Although obtaining import licences is normally the buyer's responsibility, you should confirm they have done so.

## Transport Documents

| Document | Purpose | Transfers Title? |
|---|---|---|
| **Bill of Lading** | Evidence of shipping contract; receipt for goods; document of title | Yes |
| **Sea Waybill** | Contract for sea carriage; needed by importer to collect goods | No |
| **Air Waybill** | Receipt from airline for goods received for carriage | No |
| **CMR** (road) / **CIM** (rail) | Receipt and evidence of carriage contract for road/rail transport | No |

### Packing List

Itemises everything in a shipment including the weight and dimensions of each package. Used to ensure safe transport and for customs clearance.

### Dangerous Goods Note (DGN)

Required when shipping hazardous goods. You must provide the carrier with full details including the official class of danger. For air transport, use the [IATA Dangerous Goods Declaration](https://www.iata.org/whatwedo/cargo/dgr/Pages/index.aspx).

### Standard Shipping Note (SSN)

Used for non-hazardous goods to provide handlers (port authorities, container bases) with the information needed to process your shipment. For more details, visit [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/standard-shipping-note).

### ATA (Temporary Admission) Carnets

Useful when taking goods through multiple countries (e.g. to a trade fair) and bringing them back. Carnets are issued by selected chambers of commerce, valid for up to one year, and can be used multiple times. Export and import restrictions still apply.

### Certificate of Insurance

Issued by your insurance company to certify cover has been arranged. It should detail the degree of protection, policy number, dates, destination, transport method, route, cargo description and insured value.

## Where to Get Help

- [Government guidance on moving goods internationally](https://www.gov.uk/topic/business-tax/import-export)
- [Department for Business and Trade](https://www.great.gov.uk) --- resources and advice for exporters
- [ChamberCustoms](https://www.britishchambers.org.uk/chambercustoms) --- customs clearance, training and consultancy from the British Chambers of Commerce
- [British Chambers of Commerce](https://www.britishchambers.org.uk/page/join-a-chamber) --- local export documentation and customs declaration services
- [British Exporters Association (BExA)](https://www.bexa.co.uk) --- free guides and resources for exporters
- [Chartered Institute of Export & International Trade](https://www.export.org.uk) --- training, events and consultancy
- [Export Control Joint Unit](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/export-control-organisation) --- export controls and licensing for military and dual-use items
- [British International Freight Association (BIFA)](https://www.bifa.org) --- find freight forwarders and specialists
