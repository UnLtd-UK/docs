## Why You Need Business Insurance

When starting or running a business, it is essential to consider the risks you face and take out appropriate insurance cover. Some types of insurance are required by law, while others are strongly recommended to protect your organisation against common liabilities.

## Legally Required Insurance

### Employers' Liability Insurance

If your business employs staff, you are **legally required** to have employers' liability insurance. This covers claims made by employees who are injured or become ill as a result of their employment. The definition of "employees" includes casual, seasonal, temporary and permanent staff, as well as trainees.

### Business Vehicle Insurance

All vehicles used for business purposes must, by law, be covered by third-party vehicle insurance under a policy that includes business use. Some businesses need specific commercial vehicle policies to cover vans, taxis, coaches or vehicles used on farming or industrial premises.

## Liability Insurance

### Public Liability Insurance

Covers claims arising from death or injury to members of the public, customers and suppliers, or loss of or damage to someone's property, occurring as a result of your business activity.

### Environmental Liability Insurance

Covers the cost of repairing environmental damage caused by your business, for example as the result of a chemical leakage or fire at your premises.

### Product Liability Insurance

Covers claims made by anyone who suffers injury or property damage as a result of a fault in a product you manufacture or supply.

### Employment Practices Liability Insurance

Covers costs arising from claims by employees or former employees relating to discrimination, workplace misconduct and wrongful dismissal.

### Professional Indemnity Insurance

Covers claims arising from negligent advice provided by your business, as well as breach of copyright, data protection breaches and medical malpractice.

## Property and Business Protection

### Property Insurance

Protects against loss or damage to business property (stock, equipment, premises) from fire, flood, theft or vandalism.

### Business Interruption Insurance

Covers loss of income when your business cannot trade as usual due to circumstances specified in the policy, such as equipment breakdown, premises damage or lack of access. It compensates for shortfalls in pre-tax profit and any increased business costs arising from the interruption.

### Cyber Insurance

Protects your business against damage to, or loss of information from, computer systems and networks arising from a cyberattack. It can cover your own costs as well as third-party losses sustained by your customers.

### Goods-in-Transit Insurance

Covers your goods against loss, theft or damage while in transit or being stored during a journey.

## Financial Protection

### Trade Credit Insurance

Provides cover when trade customers fail to pay, or fail to pay on time. It pays out a percentage of the outstanding debt when a customer is declared insolvent or defaults for longer than a specified period. You can cover all customers or selected individual accounts.

### Loss-of-Licence Insurance

Covers financial losses from losing a licence to sell alcohol, typically providing cover against reduced gross income or reduced business value where the licence was lost for reasons beyond your control.

## People Protection

### Key Person Insurance

Provides a lump sum to your business in the event of the death of someone critical to its operation. Policies can be extended to cover specific critical illnesses that may prevent a key person from working.

### Directors' and Officers' Insurance

Covers the costs of defending company directors and key managers against charges of wrongful acts, including breaches of trust, breaches of duty, misleading statements, neglect, error and wrongful trading.

### Income Protection Insurance

Provides business owners with an income if they are unable to work due to accident or illness, typically paying between 50% and 70% of pre-tax earnings.
