## Why Your Legal Structure Matters

When setting up your business or social enterprise, choosing the right legal structure is one of the most important decisions you will make. Your structure determines how you are owned and governed, your personal liability, how you can raise finance, and how profits can be distributed.

<Note>
Some structural decisions are **irreversible**. You cannot convert from a Company Limited by Guarantee (CLG) to a Company Limited by Shares (CLS) or vice versa. You cannot "un-CIC", "un-Charity", "un-CIO" or "un-BenCom". Take your time and create the right DNA for your organisation.
</Note>

## Key Factors to Consider

Before choosing a structure, work through these questions:

### Investment and Growth
- Over the next five years, how would you like to grow? What size organisation do you want to be?
- What investment might you need --- debt finance or equity investment? How much?

### Ownership
- Who needs to be an owner of the business, now and in the future?
- How would you like others to input to your business (as shareholders, non-executive directors, or directors)?
- How would you like to pay yourself and other owners (through profits, salary, or both)?

### Liability
- How much liability are you willing to accept?
- How much risk are you willing to accept?

### Social Mission
- What form of mission lock is best for you --- a social mission statement in your articles, a CIC, or none at all?
- What do you want a social mission lock to achieve (e.g. demonstrate commitment, cap profits)?

### Funding
- Who are your ideal grant funders?
- What legal structures do they require?

## Overview of Legal Structures

### Sole Trader

The simplest structure. You trade in your own name with no legal separation between you and the business. You have unlimited personal liability for all business debts.

### Partnership

Two or more people share ownership and responsibility. Partners have unlimited liability and share profits. Governed by the Partnership Act 1890 or a partnership agreement.

### Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)

Similar to a partnership but with limited liability for members. LLPs must register with Companies House and file annual accounts. They offer tax transparency (profits taxed in members' hands).

### Company Limited by Shares (CLS)

Ownership is through shares, which can be bought, sold and transferred. Shareholders' liability is limited to the value of their shares. Directors manage the company. Profits can be distributed as dividends.

**For social enterprises:** You can add social clauses and asset locks in your Articles of Association. This is required by Social Enterprise UK, Social Enterprise Mark, BCorp and some funders/investors. Lock changes at 75% or more shareholder majority, or use a Golden Share.

### Company Limited by Guarantee (CLG)

Ownership is through membership (not shares). Members are equal in power with one member, one vote. Members cannot receive dividends, and membership cannot be sold. Directors are elected by members. Often used by charities and social enterprises.

### Community Interest Company (CIC)

A CIC is a special type of limited company designed for social enterprises. CICs have a permanent social and asset lock, and must submit an annual CIC report demonstrating community benefit. They can be limited by shares or by guarantee.

### Charitable Incorporated Organisation (CIO) / Scottish Charitable Incorporated Organisation (SCIO)

A legal form designed specifically for charities. CIOs/SCIOs have limited liability, are registered with the Charity Commission (or OSCR in Scotland), and are exempt from corporation tax on charitable activities. Directors (sometimes called trustees) cannot be paid.

### Community Benefit Society (BenCom)

A society run for the benefit of the community. BenComs use community shares where each member has one vote regardless of how many shares they hold. They can apply for charitable status.

## Social Enterprise Badges

Because "social enterprise" is not a legal term, badges help signal your social mission:

| Badge | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **CIC** | Permanent social and asset lock with annual reporting |
| **Social Enterprise Mark** | Third-party accreditation of social enterprise credentials |
| **Social Enterprise UK membership** | Membership of the national body for social enterprises |
| **BCorp** | Certified meeting high standards of social and environmental performance |
| **Fair Trade** | Certified commitment to fair trade principles |

## Key Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Members** | Owners of a CLG --- equal in power (one member, one vote), elected by other members |
| **Shareholders** | Owners of a CLS --- power depends on percentage of shares owned |
| **Directors** | Elected by members/shareholders, legally responsible for the company |
| **Trustees** | Directors of charities/CIOs (sometimes used interchangeably with "directors") |
| **Non-Executive Directors (NEDs)** | Directors who do not work in day-to-day operations; part-time, independent |
| **Shadow Directors** | People who are not formally appointed but whose directions the board follows |
| **Persons with Significant Control** | Individuals who hold significant influence over the company |
| **Asset Lock** | A provision preventing assets from being used other than to further the organisation's objectives |
| **Articles of Association** | The primary constitutional document setting out rules for running the company |
