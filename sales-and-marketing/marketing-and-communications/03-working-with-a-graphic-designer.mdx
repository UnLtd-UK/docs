## Why Work with a Graphic Designer?

Graphic designers produce eye-catching and effective designs that can be used in a variety of ways. You might commission a designer to create a business or product logo, images and artwork for your website, stationery, leaflets, packaging, signage, or exhibition stands. Professional design helps your organisation make a strong first impression and communicate your brand consistently.

## Finding a Graphic Designer

There are several ways to find graphic designers who may be a good fit for your project:

- The "find a designer" directory on the [Chartered Society of Designers](http://www.csd.org.uk/resources/find-a-designer) website.
- The "find a design agency" directory on the [Design Business Association](http://www.dba-community.org.uk) website.
- Freelancer marketplaces specialising in graphic design and creative services, such as:
  - [99Designs](https://99designs.co.uk)
  - [Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/hiring)
  - [DesignHill](http://www.designhill.com)
- The [UK Graphic Designer](http://www.ukgraphicdesigner.co.uk) directory.

## Choosing the Right Designer

When evaluating graphic designers, consider the following questions:

- **Pricing structure:** Do they charge per hour, or will they quote a fixed project fee?
- **Client experience:** What type of clients do they work for, and how long have those relationships lasted?
- **Conflict of interest:** Would there be any overlap with their existing or previous clients? Do they work for any of your competitors?
- **Sector knowledge:** How much do they know about your particular business sector? While not essential, a designer with relevant experience may save you time and money because they will not need as long to familiarise themselves with your field.
- **Portfolio and references:** Ask for examples of their work and references from other clients. Also review the designer's own website, which will typically showcase their skills and design styles.

## Preparing Your Project Brief

The design process usually begins with an initial consultation where you and the designer discuss the project brief. The more information you provide about your project and business goals, the better the results will be.

Your brief should cover:

| Element | What to include |
|---|---|
| **Scope of work** | The nature of the work being commissioned and the ways it will be used. For example, if a new logo will appear on websites, social media, stationery, and adverts, it may be needed in a variety of sizes and formats. |
| **Collaboration** | Whether the designer will liaise directly with other suppliers such as website developers, copywriters, or photographers. |
| **Objectives** | The business objective of the design work. This helps the designer create designs that meet your aims effectively. |
| **Timeline** | An estimated timetable, including deadlines for each stage of the project. |
| **Budget** | An overall project fee or an estimate of how many hours the designer will spend on all aspects of the work. |
| **Deliverable formats** | The file formats in which the final designs should be supplied (e.g. PNG, SVG, PDF, editable source files). |

## The Design Process

After your initial consultation, most design projects move through a series of stages:

1. **Initial concepts:** The designer develops early ideas based on your brief.
2. **First drafts:** The designer presents initial drafts for your review. This is your opportunity to check that their vision matches what you need.
3. **Refinement:** The design is revised based on your feedback and agreed amendments.
4. **Proofing and sign-off:** You review the final version and formally approve it.
5. **Implementation:** The finished designs are delivered and put into use.

## Typical Costs

The rates charged by graphic designers vary considerably depending on their skills, experience, and the complexity of your project. Common pricing structures include:

| Pricing model | Typical range |
|---|---|
| **Hourly rate** | Around £30 to £90 per hour |
| **Day rate** | Around £200 to £400 per day |
| **Fixed project fee** | Varies widely depending on scope |
| **Retainer** | Ongoing monthly arrangement |

When hiring a designer who charges hourly or daily, ask for an estimate of the total time required to complete the project. This will help you keep tight control of your budget and avoid unexpected costs.
