## Setting Sales Targets

Before building your pipeline, establish how much you need to sell. Clear targets give your sales activity direction and make it possible to measure progress.

### Vision Orbit

Start with the big picture. Use a vision orbit exercise to set high-level sales ambitions -- where do you want to be in one, three, and five years? These top-level figures become the anchor for everything else.

### Breaking Down Targets

Once you have an overall revenue goal, break it down into actionable segments:

- **By product or service** -- How much revenue do you expect from each offering?
- **By customer type** -- How much will come from existing customers versus new business?
- **By time period** -- What are your monthly or quarterly milestones?

This breakdown helps you see whether your targets are realistic and where the greatest effort is needed.

## Prioritising Multiple Revenue Streams

If you have several products, services, or funding channels, you need a systematic way to decide where to focus. Score each revenue stream out of 10 across the following criteria:

| Criterion | What to assess |
|---|---|
| **Strength of market proposition** | How compelling is your offer in this market? |
| **Availability of customers** | How large and accessible is the potential customer base? |
| **Ease of conversion** | How confident are you in reaching and converting these customers? Consider your experience, their budget, and their level of interest. |
| **Cost of sales** | How much time, travel, and information-gathering is needed to close a deal? |
| **Cost of delivery** | What are the likely margins? How operationally demanding is fulfilment? |
| **Impact on social mission** | How strongly does this revenue stream advance your core purpose? |

Total the scores and compare. The streams with the highest totals deserve the greatest share of your time and resources. Revisit this scoring periodically as your market and capabilities evolve.

## Understanding the Sales Funnel

Your sales funnel maps the journey from a large pool of leads down to paying customers. At each stage, some prospects drop away, so you need to understand your conversion rates to know how many leads you must generate to hit your targets.

Work backwards from your target:

1. **Target customers** -- How many paying customers do you need? Break this down by existing renewals versus new business, and by product.
2. **Fans** -- Among your existing customers, who will buy again? Who might buy additional products? Who can you upgrade? Who will refer others? Consider the **customer lifetime value** of each relationship.
3. **Prospects** -- How many warm prospects do you need to generate your target number of customers? Apply your warm conversion rate.
4. **Leads** -- How many cold leads must enter the top of the funnel to produce enough prospects? Apply your cold conversion rate.

For each stage, be clear about **who is responsible** and **how you are tracking** movement through the funnel.

## Pipeline Management

A well-managed pipeline is not a static contact list. It is a living tool that reflects the current state of every opportunity, guides your priorities, and keeps your team aligned.

### Pipeline Management Principles

- **Use a cloud-based tool** -- Whether it is a shared spreadsheet, a CRM system, or a dedicated pipeline tool, keep your pipeline somewhere your team can access and update it in real time. This avoids version-control problems and ensures everyone works from the same information.
- **Keep it current** -- Update your pipeline after every interaction. Record the date of last contact, what was discussed, and what the next step is. A pipeline with stale data is worse than no pipeline at all.
- **Prioritise with scoring** -- Use a structured scoring method (such as BANTS) to decide where to focus your effort. Not all opportunities are equal, and your time is limited.

### BANTS Scoring for Pipeline Prioritisation

The BANTS framework scores each opportunity from 1 to 3 (Red = 1, Amber = 2, Green = 3) across five criteria:

| Letter | Criterion | What to assess |
|---|---|---|
| **B** | Budget | Does the customer or funder typically offer funding or budgets in the range you need? |
| **A** | Am I Eligible | Do you meet their criteria? Is there a good match with their priorities and geography? |
| **N** | Need | Do they need or want what you offer? Do their interests and strategies align with yours? |
| **T** | Timeline | Do their timelines suit you? Factor in stakeholder introductions and turnaround time. |
| **S** | Scope | Is there potential for additional value beyond the immediate deal, such as expertise, resources, or connections? |

Opportunities with the highest total score are your priority targets.

### Pipeline Stages

Define clear stages that every opportunity moves through. Common stages include:

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| **Cold** | Identified but no contact made |
| **First Contact** | Initial outreach completed |
| **Interested** | Prospect has engaged and expressed interest |
| **Signing Up** | Actively moving towards a commitment |
| **Existing Customer** | Deal closed, now in delivery or renewal |
| **Closed** | Opportunity lost or completed |

For grant pipelines, typical stages include: Not Started, Enquiry Sent, Application Started, Application Submitted, Interview, Successful, and Rejected.

## Pipeline Templates

Two downloadable templates are available to help you get started. Choose the one that best fits your needs, or adapt them to your own context.

### Business Development Pipeline Template

This template is designed for tracking commissions, partnerships, and grant opportunities. It includes:

- An **instructions sheet** with guidance on pipeline management best practices and the BANTS scoring methodology.
- A **pipeline tracker** with columns for organisation details, contact information, fund or budget size, pipeline stage, individual BANTS scores, priority level, and interaction notes.

**How to use it:**

1. Populate the tracker with all potential opportunities.
2. Score each opportunity across the five BANTS criteria (1--3).
3. Use the total score to assign a priority level (High, Medium, or Low).
4. Order by priority and begin outreach. Consider starting with lower-priority leads to practise and refine your approach.
5. Update notes and last-contact fields after every interaction.

### Sales and Grants Pipeline Template

This dual-purpose template is for organisations that pursue both commercial sales and grant funding. It includes:

- A **sales tracker** with columns for contact details, organisation, sector, suitability rating, pipeline status, BANT scores (out of 4), deal size, ownership, and detailed notes.
- A **grants tracker** with columns for funder details, programme name, application deadline, amount, status, RAG-rated likelihood, and progress notes. It includes a running total of amounts applied for.
- A **lists sheet** with customisable dropdown values for pipeline stages and suitability ratings.

**How to use it:**

1. For sales leads, add each prospect to the Sales Tracker, fill in contact details, and score them across the four BANT criteria. Use the total to prioritise follow-up.
2. For grants, add each funding opportunity to the Grants Tracker, note deadlines, and assign a RAG likelihood rating.
3. Update status fields and notes after every interaction.
4. Customise the dropdown values on the Lists sheet to match your own pipeline stages.
