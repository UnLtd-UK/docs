## Principles for Sales Success

Before diving into techniques, anchor your approach in four guiding principles:

- **Start with the end in mind** -- Know the outcome you want from every conversation before you begin.
- **Be intentional, not accidental** -- Plan your sales activity rather than waiting for opportunities to arrive.
- **Slow down to speed up** -- Invest time in understanding the prospect's situation thoroughly; it shortens the overall sales cycle.
- **Keep the main thing the main thing** -- Stay focused on your highest-value activities and do not let distractions dilute your effort.

## Networking and Prospecting

### The Power of Your Network

Your business network is not about how many people know your name. It is about how many will send you customers or actively help you advance your goals. Online connections have value, but the relationships that generate referrals and introductions require a more personal touch.

### Four Types of Network Contacts

Your network contacts fall into four categories, listed in descending order of value:

| Category | Description | Why they matter |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Satisfied clients** | Current and past customers who have experienced the value you deliver first-hand. | They trust you completely and will recommend you without hesitation. |
| **2. Close friends and family** | People who know you well and are willing to use their connections to help. | They are loyal, though their endorsements may carry less commercial weight. |
| **3. Warm referrals** | People your clients, friends, or family have contacted on your behalf. | You have a "residue of trust" from the original referrer, though the link is weaker. |
| **4. Extended contacts** | People you have met through your warm referrals. | The trust is thinnest here, but these contacts expand your reach significantly. |

To build your pipeline, identify at least five contacts in each category. Start outreach with Category 1 and work outward.

### Lead Generation

Face-to-face meetings remain the most powerful channel for converting prospects. Whether in person or via video call, direct conversation builds trust faster than any other medium. Prioritise getting meetings over sending emails.

### Follow-Up Cadence

Consistent follow-up is what separates effective salespeople from everyone else. Establish a regular cadence of contact using a mix of phone calls, emails, and meetings. Many deals are won simply because you stayed in touch when competitors did not.

## The PRIMED Questioning Model

When you get face-to-face with a prospect, the quality of your questions determines the quality of the conversation. The PRIMED model structures your discovery conversations around five key areas:

| Letter | Area | Questions to explore |
|---|---|---|
| **P/R** | Present Reality | What is currently happening? What is the situation today? |
| **I** | Issues | What are the key problems? What is their impact on individuals, the organisation, the department, and their customers? |
| **M** | Map of Future | What is their vision of the future? What goals, targets, and KPIs are they working towards? |
| **E** | Evaluation | What alternatives have they explored? What criteria will their decision be based on? |
| **D** | Decision | How are decisions made? Who is involved in the process? |

### Using PRIMED to Prepare

Before each meeting, map out what you already know and what you still need to find out:

| Area | What you know | What you need to find out | Actions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Present Reality** | | | |
| **Issues** | | | |
| **Map of Future** | | | |
| **Evaluation** | | | |
| **Decision** | | | |

Fill this in before every significant prospect conversation. It keeps you focused and ensures you do not leave a meeting without the information you need.

## Consultative Selling

Consultative selling means leading with the customer's problem rather than your product. The pattern is simple:

1. **Ask an open question** -- Draw out the prospect's situation and challenges.
2. **Listen** -- Give them space to explain fully. Do not jump ahead.
3. **Summarise** -- Play back what you have heard to confirm understanding.
4. **Repeat or close** -- Either go deeper with another question or, if you have enough information, move towards a solution and a close.

This approach builds trust, uncovers the real buying motivation, and positions you as a partner rather than a vendor.

## Handling Objections

Objections are a natural and healthy part of any sales conversation. They signal that the prospect is engaged and thinking seriously about your offer. The key is to handle them calmly and systematically.

### Common Objections to Prepare For

Think through the objections you hear most often. Which ones come up repeatedly? Which ones feel most difficult to handle? Preparing your responses in advance removes anxiety and keeps the conversation moving forward.

### The ACE Framework

Use the ACE framework to handle any objection:

| Step | Action | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Acknowledge | Show that you have heard the concern. Do not dismiss it or become defensive. |
| **C** | Clarify | Ask questions to make sure you understand the real objection. Often the stated concern masks a deeper issue. |
| **E** | Explain | Provide your response, addressing the concern directly with evidence, examples, or reassurance. |

After explaining, **check** that you have resolved the concern, then **move forward** to the next stage of the conversation.

### Advanced ACE Variation

For more complex objections, expand the framework with two additional steps:

1. **Acknowledge** the concern.
2. **Clarify** the objection.
3. **Isolate** -- confirm this is the only concern: "If we can resolve this, is there anything else holding you back?"
4. **Trial close** -- test readiness to proceed: "If I can address this, would you be happy to move forward?"
5. **Explain** your response.

This variation prevents you from resolving one objection only to discover another, and another, behind it.

## Closing the Sale

Closing is not a single moment -- it is the natural conclusion of a well-run conversation. If you have understood the prospect's needs, handled their concerns, and demonstrated value, the close should feel like a logical next step.

### Five Closing Approaches

Choose the approach that fits the situation and your personal style:

| Approach | What you say | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | "Are you happy to proceed with this?" | When the prospect has given clear buying signals and there is nothing left to resolve. |
| **Alternative** | "Would you prefer to go ahead with Option 1 or Option 2?" | When you want to move past the "whether" question and focus on "which." |
| **Assumptive** | "So if all of that works for you, am I right to say we can go ahead?" | When the conversation has been positive and you want to confirm momentum. |
| **Hypothetical** | "You have said you need X. If we can provide all of that, are you happy to go ahead?" | When there is a specific requirement you can meet, and meeting it removes the last barrier. |
| **Scarcity** | "Availability is limited. To secure this for you, I would need a purchase order by..." | When there is genuine urgency -- a limited offer, a deadline, or constrained capacity. Use honestly. |

## Shortening the Sales Cycle

Long sales cycles drain resources and increase the risk of losing deals. Use these strategies to move things along:

- **Be direct about timelines.** Early in the process, ask the prospect to articulate what needs to happen and what steps are required before a decision. Write all of this into your follow-up email so both sides are working from the same plan.
- **Name the process.** Be upfront: "We know from experience that this process can take time. What can we do to make it as efficient as possible for both of us?"
- **Seek a no when things stall.** If a deal is dragging, try to get a clear no so you can redirect your effort. A definitive no is more valuable than an indefinite maybe.
- **Learn from lost deals.** Ask why people did not buy and identify your top three reasons. Use these insights to address objections earlier in future conversations.
- **Use qualification questions.** Find the real reason for buying early, so you can tailor your approach and avoid wasted effort.
- **Balance depth and breadth.** If your sales cycle is inherently long, you may need to spend less time on each individual prospect and more time getting new leads into the pipeline.

## Negotiation Tips

Negotiation is a distinct skill from selling. Once a prospect has decided they want to buy, the negotiation determines the terms. Keep these principles in mind:

- **Aim high.** Start with your best-case scenario rather than an already-compromised offer. You can always come down; you cannot go up.
- **Know your levers.** If price is fixed, consider what else you can adjust: quality, quantity, scope, or timing of delivery.
- **Understand the other side's pressures.** Why is this deal important for them? How can you reassure them that their needs will be met?
- **Never give something away for free.** Every concession should come with a reciprocal ask: "I can do this if you can do that."
- **Be clear on your goals.** Know what you want to achieve, what would make the deal worthwhile, and when you are prepared to walk away.
- **Give yourself time.** Never say yes immediately. Make it a rule that your default answer is "let me get back to you."
- **Say no with confidence.** Do not apologise for protecting your interests. If saying no feels difficult, start with small refusals and build up.
- **Outline the implications.** Make sure the other party understands what agreeing would actually involve: "If I do this, it will take two days to source the data."
- **Get them to help you prioritise.** Ask: "If I can only deliver a few things, which is most important for you?"
- **Be explicit about trade-offs.** State what is achievable within the constraints: "I can do Y, but in the time available it will only be to this standard."
- **Offer an exchange.** "I can help you with X, and I am going to need you to pick up Z."
