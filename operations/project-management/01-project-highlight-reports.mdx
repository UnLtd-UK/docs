## What is a Highlight Report?

A highlight report is a concise, regular update that communicates the status of a project to stakeholders. It is typically produced monthly and covers four key areas: work completed, live issues, risks, and upcoming work.

The highlight report is one of the most important communication tools in project management. It keeps stakeholders informed, surfaces problems early, and creates a visible record of project progress.

## The Four Sections

### Work Completed

This section covers all significant work completed in the previous month. When writing this section:

- Focus on work of **significance** — not every small task needs listing
- Where possible, these should relate to **work tasks scheduled** into your project plan for that time period
- If your project management is on track, you should see the "upcoming work" items from the previous month move across to this section — this makes it a good visual indicator of how efficiently you are progressing through your tasks

### Issues

Issues are **live problems** you are dealing with, unlike risks which are future problems that may or may not happen. Issues typically incur costs, time delays, or reputational damage, as well as stress and knock-on complications to other parts of the project.

When reporting issues:

- Make sure you have a **real understanding** of the issue, its effects on every other dependency, and know what the solutions may be
- Ideally you would not want to see the same issue appearing **two months in a row** — if it persists, it may need escalating or a different approach

### Risks

Risks are problems that **could happen in the future**. They are typically scored using a simple equation:

**Risk level = Likelihood of risk happening x Impact of risk happening**

Most often this is rated out of 3 for each factor, giving a possible risk level of 1 to 9. You will have many risks as they can cover a wide range of eventualities — HR, resourcing, contractor delays, communications, and more.

In your highlight report:

- Include your **top three most significant risks**
- Be ready to discuss the **strategies you have put in place** to avoid them or react to them if they do happen

### Upcoming Work

This section looks forward at the significant work coming in the next month:

- With good project management, all upcoming work tasks should be **new each month** — not carry-overs
- Include the **title of each element of work** and a brief description if necessary
- Keep it **high-level** — otherwise readers will either get confused or get drawn into micro-management

## Example Highlight Report

Here is an example of a completed highlight report for a house-building project:

| Section | Details |
| --- | --- |
| **Work Completed** | Foundations built; concrete foundation poured; external frame built |
| **Issues** | Concrete poured was two different colours; weather forecast for current week and next month is rain and high winds |
| **Risks** | Cash flow problems mean potential staff shortages at the end of the build; bumpy path to house means glass might break in transit |
| **Upcoming Work** | Walls to be installed; second floor laid; stud work complete; windows to be delivered; windows to be installed |

## Tips for Effective Highlight Reports

- **Be consistent** — use the same format every month so stakeholders can quickly find the information they need
- **Be honest** — the report should be an accurate reflection of project status, not an exercise in optimism
- **Keep it concise** — the highlight report is a summary, not a detailed project log
- **Link to your project plan** — the work completed and upcoming work sections should map back to your scheduled tasks
- **Escalate early** — if an issue or risk is significant, the highlight report should not be the first time stakeholders hear about it
