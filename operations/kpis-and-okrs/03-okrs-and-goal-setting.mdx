## What Are OKRs?

Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) are an effective goal-setting and leadership tool for communicating what you want to accomplish and what milestones you need to meet in order to accomplish it. OKRs are used by some of the world's leading organisations to set and enact their strategies.

An OKR has two components:

- **Objectives** — what you want to accomplish
- **Key Results** — how you will measure progress towards that objective

## Setting Your Objectives

Your objectives should be significant, action-oriented, concrete and inspiring. They should speak to the ambition and "why" behind what your organisation does.

**Example objective:** "Build a successful MVP"

When setting objectives, aim for **one to three** at a time. Too many objectives dilute focus and make it difficult to track meaningful progress.

### What Makes a Good Objective

- It is aligned with your organisation's mission and purpose
- It is ambitious but achievable
- It is clear enough that everyone understands what success looks like
- It inspires action and motivates your team

## Defining Key Results

Key results are how you measure progress towards each objective. They should be **SMART** — specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound.

If you find SMART goals too rigid, consider whether each key result:

- Relates to your organisation's purpose
- Shows variation over time
- Helps you learn, understand and improve
- Is in the hands of the people doing the work, so they can change the system
- Can be used by leaders to act on the change you want to see

### Example Key Results

For an objective like "Build a successful MVP":

- Achieve 100 weekly active users
- Achieve a Net Promoter Score (NPS) above 40
- Secure 5 paid customers

Aim for **one to five key results** per objective. Each should be quantifiable and have a clear target.

## Assessing Your Strategic Position

Before setting goals, it is useful to understand where your organisation stands. A **SWOT assessment** is a practical tool for this:

| | Helpful | Harmful |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Internal factors** | **Strengths**: What do you do particularly well? What skills do you have? What is unique about you in the marketplace? What do your customers most like about you? | **Weaknesses**: What are you not comfortable doing? What resources, staff, or skills do you lack? What is pushing up your costs or wasting resources? What systems or processes are holding you back? |
| **External factors** | **Opportunities**: Are there new situations you can take advantage of? Are there gaps in the market you can fill? Are there partnerships that might be fruitful? | **Threats**: Who is your competition and what do they offer? Are there environmental changes that could cause problems? What roadblocks are in your path? |

## Building a Balanced Scorecard

A balanced scorecard helps you track performance across four key perspectives, ensuring you are not over-indexing on a single dimension:

### Financial Performance

- Secured investment and minimised debt
- Profitability and cash flow
- Reduced operating costs
- Efficient billing

### Stakeholder Satisfaction

- Improvement in sales funnel conversion and duration
- Retention of customers at renewal point
- Feedback scores
- Key impact measures
- Delivering new products and services

### Systems and Processes

- Paperless invoicing
- Updating of CRM and core databases
- Stock maintenance
- Quality control
- Adherence to standard operating procedures

### Engaged Team

- Recruitment and retention
- Learning and new skills
- Remuneration
- Number of volunteer opportunities

## CEO Decision-Making and Performance

As a CEO or organisational leader, your responsibilities span both management and leadership. Understanding the distinction helps you set the right KPIs and OKRs for each:

| Management | Leadership |
| --- | --- |
| Implementing tactical actions | Creating new visions and aims |
| Detailed budgeting | Establishing organisational financial targets |
| Measuring and reporting performance | Deciding what needs measuring and reporting |
| Applying rules and policies | Making new rules and policies |
| Organising people and tasks within structures | Deciding structures, hierarchies and workgroups |
| Recruiting people for jobs | Creating new job roles |
| Problem-solving | Problem-anticipation |
| Planning | Visualising and conceiving new opportunities |
| Improving productivity and efficiency | Inspiring and empowering others |
| Delegating and training | Planning and organising succession |
