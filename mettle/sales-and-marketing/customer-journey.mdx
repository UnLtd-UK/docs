Darshan Sanghrajka | Super Being Labs | September 2016
Introduction
● This workshop is designed as the introductory module for UnLtd award winners.
● This course helps award winners to understand their customers (stakeholders and
beneficiaries) in a way that helps them scale both growth and impact.
● This course and handbook should enable you to have a confident and practical
understanding of mapping out a customer journey and what to do with those insights.
Why is this important?
● Being a social venture is a competitive advantage in the world we live in - these are great
businesses that do good and do well - we need to get every UnLtd venture to understand
this from the start.
● At the same time, they can’t just rely on being a ‘social enterprise’ to get them ahead;
We’ve seen many examples of this being the only thing being communicated from social
ventures but in reality their stakeholders, customers and beneficiaries are looking for
specific value and that needs to be communicated.
● Mapping a customer journey is an incredibly useful way to understand and improve a
venture’s customer experience. By using empathy, it not only helps a venture understand
how a customer interacts with them now, but also identifies the opportunities for
improvement.
○ If their brand doesn’t make sense to their target audience, if their customers don’t
rave about their experience, if they don’t have product/market fit, if they don’t
understand the barriers stopping people from becoming customers, if they don’t
know how to plot out a growth plan in a way that allows for adaptation along the
way, then they’re going to struggle to scale.
v1.0 - 2016

○ We can tackle all this by helping the ventures understand how to understand their
customer, their product, their brand positioning and most importantly how to tie
this all together to achieve growth.
○ This is even more important considering that social ventures often are cash-
strapped to begin with and word of mouth is a powerful tool to help them grow.
Notes for this workshop.
The design of this workshop is based on the following points:
● This workshop is designed to be facilitated by Award Managers or trainers.
● It can be delivered in a half day workshop (3 hours) or a full day workshop (6 hours).
● All participants have started their social ventures and understand their challenges.
● They will do some homework beyond this workshop and really tackle the exercises with
their teams.
● This can work with award winners at any stage of their journey.
● This can be delivered to award winners without them needing to do any preparatory
reading.
● There are slides (PPT) that accompany this guide.
Aims and Outcomes
Through this course you’ll practically learn how to do and teach the following:
1. How to map and design a delightful and achievable customer experience
2. How to develop a scalable product or service based on points one and two
3. How to create a word of mouth strategy that drives your growth plan
4. How to keep testing and adapting your growth plan

“What is a customer journey map?”
This slide provides a very simple explanation of what a customer journey is. This is your chance to
demystify a potentially very detailed process.
For your own knowledge - A Customer Journey Map is an incredibly useful tool to understand and
improve your customer experience. A great customer journey map documents your customer
experience from your customer's eyes, helping you to understand not only how customers
interact with you today, but also identifies improvement opportunities. It genuinely is insightful
getting to see things which you might not have noticed before.

Slide 3 - The Customer Journey Map
What does each bit mean on the Customer Journey Map? (You can download the template from
here
1. Stages Across The Top
These are the various stages a stakeholder will go through before, during and after experiencing
your product or service. Your focus should be to nudge them through each stage to ensure they
have the best experience with you and return to keep doing so, as well recommending you to
others:
Search: This is the stage the stakeholder is in when they are on the lookout for a solution to a
problem they have. As mentioned at the workshop, you might include customer education in this
phase - as they might not know that there is a solution to a problem or worse still, they might not
even know they have a problem.
v1.0 - 2016

Interest: This is the stage the stakeholder is in when they are interested and choosing between a
number of solutions.
Sign-Up: This is the stage where the stakeholder has chosen you and is beginning their journey
with you.
Do: This is the stage where the stakeholder is fully experiencing your product or service.
Come Back: This is the stage where the stakeholder returns to you and/or brings other people to
you too.
2. Stages Across The Side
These are the various behaviours the stakeholder will have across their journey. Put yourselves in
their shoes and fill this out honestly.
Doing: This describes what they are doing when they are at each stage of their journey.
Thinking: This describes what they are rationally thinking at each stage of their journey.
Feeling: This describes what they are emotionally thinking at each stage of their journey.
Experience: This describes what they are experiencing at each stage of their journey.
Delight: This is where you can be creative - you’ve seen what they’re doing, what they’re thinking,
how they’re feeling and what they’re experiencing at each stage. Now jot down ideas which would
delight them at each stage - things that would make them want to work with/buy/use your
product
or service. Be creative. If you can’t think of much right now, no worries - we’re here to help.
Opportunities - Throughout - This whole map should get your brain innovating even more than it
already does. This is where you can jot down ideas you might have to make your product or
service even more incredible.
Opportunities - Stage Specific - This is where you can jot down ideas that you might have to
v1.0 - 2016

improve each stage.
Opportunities - End & Feedback - This is where you can jot down ideas that you might have to
improve how you do things once the customer has benefited from your product or service + how
you can maximise the amount of feedback you get. This is crucial, not just in measuring your social
impact but also capturing positive feedback and improving on any negative feedback.
How flexible can you be?
The trick with this map is that you can be flexible. You can change the headings - for example, if
you feel there is a stage before the first stage on the current map, then just add one in or change
the headings. The purpose is to capture how your customer feels when experiencing your product
or service and getting it down all on one page, so that you can think about how to delight them.
Ultimately, this will mean you grow through incredible word of mouth.
The above should help you understand the Customer Journey Map - how do you fill it in?
1. First of all, pick a stakeholder - don’t forget to think about stakeholders that might not be
apparent at first glance. (This exercise should be done for all stakeholders - whether they are
investors, customers or beneficiaries).
2. Now put yourselves in their shoes and fill in the Customer Journey map. Be as empathetic as
you can - if you are unsure, don’t assume - ask a real stakeholder and be sure you validated
insights.

For the ventures to do this properly - here are some activities that will prepare them.
Slide 4 - Can You Catch Someone’s Attention?
We need to get all participants fired up before they can tackle any of this.
Activity
This is an exercise to get the participants fired up and thinking passionately. Get them to
imagine they see someone important across the street and only have 30 seconds to get
their venture noticed - what would they shout? Get them to stand up and do this. This
will get them to write down a one liner about their product or service.
v1.0 - 2016

Slide 5 - Who Are Your Stakeholders?
● This is a useful first step for award winners to identify a range of stakeholders to include
those they are currently engaging with and those who perhaps they should be engaging
with. It is important to understand what stakeholders value and what is important to
them.
● Remember ‘stakeholders’ is a broad term - this can be investors, customers and
beneficiaries. It’s important to note all of them down and then begin to understand their
motivations.
● It is also important to refrain from making assumptions about what stakeholders value.
The process has to start with getting to know those who come into contact with your
brand. Is there already existing research into this and/or will there be a need to speak to
real customers to understand their motivations? This can be anecdotal or analytical.
(Where possible also look at website data and social media to validate anecdotal data)
v1.0 - 2016

Activity
● Get ventures to write down all those who can say ‘No’ to their product or service. This
will give them a list of the most important stakeholders.
● Then get them to write down who they come into contact with their brand and what
they definitely know about them and what the gaps are. A useful framework on a sheet
of paper is:
○ What we know
○ What we think we know
○ What we need to find out (What we don’t know)
v1.0 - 2016

Slide 6 - 50 No’s.
● This exercise is quick-fire but a rapid way of getting ventures to put themselves in a
customer’s shoes.
Activity
Ask the participants to come up with 50 things that would stop customers from buying from
them.
1. Give them a max of 20 minutes to come up with this list - challenge them to be super
harsh.
2. They should struggle to get all 50 but the harder they try, the more holes they’ll uncover
and these can then be filled in.
3. Also emphasise the need for honesty in the room today – it’s OK you are amongst friends.
v1.0 - 2016

Slide 7 - The one liner
The brand proposition might start off at one place but through the process of designing a
delightful customer journey, the participants should now start to hone their brand proposition.
It should now start to distinguish their ventures from competitors.
Activity
1. Ask the ventures to articulate what their venture is in just one line.
2. Ask them to go through their communications messages and channels (both offline and
online). Do they match the expectations and experiences of their customers and other
stakeholders consistently? Where are the inconsistencies?

Slide 9 - Delighting your customers
Now that the ventures have filled out the customer journey map - they should be able to see
where the opportunities for improvement are. This is where word of mouth can be created and
the venture can begin to scale their growth and impact. A company with a social mission that
makes their customers happy, has a huge competitive advantage.
Activity
Ask the participants to delight their customers - there is a real joy in making someone happy and
that will pay off with word of mouth. For a social enterprise, this is great for the social mission
too - the more loyal customers you have, the more advocates you have for your mission.
1. Task them to come up with 10 things they can do to delight their customers along the
customer journey to break apart the identified barriers.
2. This exercise is a lot of fun, as it’s all about being creative with no barriers. Let the
imagination run wild and then they can start to tighten up the ideas.

3. This should provide a template for what to do next.
Slide 10 - Review, amend and measure
At this point - they should be able to redo the customer map for each stakeholder and then work
out the metrics to use in order to measure what changes are working and if not, why not.
Activity
1. Redo the customer journey maps and record how you will measure the difference these
changes make.
2. Work out how to make it easy for your customers to tell others about all great
experiences at each touchpoint - the easier it is to do so, the more likely they are to do it.
3. Work out the resources needed to make the changes and the estimated return on that
investment.
