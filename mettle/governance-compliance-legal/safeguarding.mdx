## Safeguarding and Child Protection: A Guide for Your Venture

### Working with children, young people, and other vulnerable groups

The welfare and support of children and young people and other vulnerable groups to achieve their full potential is critical to building a more positive future both locally and nationally.

If your venture involves work with children, young people, or other vulnerable groups you must obtain a satisfactory disclosure which may be obtained from the Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS).

More about this:

The Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) has launched an official Facebook page, and this can be found here.

The DBS Outreach and Regional advisory service applies to England, Wales and Northern Ireland click here for information on their Partnership and Engagement team and Regional Outreach service.

For further guidance on how to obtain a check in your location please click on the appropriate link below:

| Region | Link |
| --- | --- |
| England & Wales | https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/disclosure-and-barring-service |
| Scotland | https://www.mygov.scot/disclosure-types |

### Can I use my award to help with the safeguarding needs of my venture?

Yes! Your award budget can be used for:

- Obtaining a DBS check as detailed above (for you and key members of your team)
- Attending safeguarding and or child protection training (it's important that you and your team understand your safeguarding responsibilities and have systems & processes in place to meet these obligations).

Your Support Manager can be contacted to chat about your award spend should you wish to make changes to your budget.

### Can UnLtd help with practical support around this?

UnLtd staff do not have the specialist expertise to give advice on your safeguarding and protection policies and procedures. These must be relevant and specific to the activities of your venture.

We have produced this guide to support you through this process.

### Safeguarding Checklist and Frameworks – what do I need?

The National Council for Voluntary Organisations have developed a resources section to get you started here.

They have compiled a list of organisations which have checklists, tools, and frameworks to review what you are already doing for safeguarding and what else needs to be done.

### Who can help?

We recommend that you attend appropriate training within your local community or network. This gives you the opportunity to gain valuable information about the safeguarding reporting structures in your area.

Your local authority / council area may run free safeguarding training, it is worth checking to see if this is available and what level of training is available.

If this is not possible, there are online training options and also valuable online resources available in the section below.

## Signposting to Support

<Warning>
  If you or someone you know is in immediate danger, call 999. For non-emergency health advice, call NHS 111.
</Warning>

Individuals should be encouraged to speak to their GP in all instances where they have any health support issues e.g. mental health, depression, anxiety, struggling to cope, unmet healthcare needs.
