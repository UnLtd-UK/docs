## Engage Your Team and Leadership

Whether you are the key decision-maker or are planning to present to a leadership team, you need to identify how the benefits of B Corp certification apply to your business and how your company's mission can be enhanced through certification. For the certification to be truly meaningful, your leadership and employees need to be involved and on board.

As you work through the B Impact Assessment (BIA), you will need information from others in your company. There are two recommended approaches to organising and successfully completing the process.

### Convene a Stewardship Team

Involve people in the process -- either through a formal B Corp Committee or informally -- to guide both the assessment and later improvement of your score.

1. **Identify the right people** -- Bring in representatives from Human Resources, Operations, Finance, Product, and other relevant departments who can help you accurately answer questions on the assessment.
2. **Set up meetings** -- Determine a timeline for the certification process.
3. **Assign questions and deadlines** -- Just as with any cross-departmental project, management will be key in completing the assessment.

Working through the assessment as a group can be especially valuable. Social enterprises that have gone through the process together report that the collaborative element creates a healthy competitive dynamic, pushing each business to improve its practices.

### Gather Your Documents

You will need the following documentation to complete the BIA accurately:

1. Payroll register or employee roster
2. Employee handbook
3. List of your "significant suppliers":
   - If you have more than 10 employees: top 80% of suppliers/vendors by dollar volume
   - If you have fewer than 10 employees: top five suppliers
4. Breakdown of revenue from your various products and services
5. Profit and Loss statement
6. Any resource-use tracking (energy, greenhouse gas, water, etc.)
7. Any sustainability or corporate social responsibility goals, reports, progress, or tracking documents

## Common Changes Needed to Certify

The BIA is rigorous -- that is what gives the certification its value. Often, companies do not make the 80-point threshold on the first attempt. Improvement is a pivotal phase in many companies' journeys to certification. Here are the four most common changes businesses need to make.

### Formalise Your Policies

Your company may already offer paid family leave, recycle, or prefer local suppliers on an ad hoc basis. However, the BIA prioritises positive practices that are codified in company documents, because formalised policies are more likely to be maintained over time.

**How to improve:**
- Engage your team and company leadership to create organisation-wide policies covering all employees
- Have team leaders document the standards and practices already existing in their departments
- Create a shared folder accessible to all employees, or collect all policies in a handbook

Review your employment practices carefully. Areas to document include employee names, graduation programmes, employee handbooks, payment rates, and staff practices.

> "While we were going through the process, one way we improved our score was to document our policies better... There were still some policies about employee welfare that we hadn't formalized, so it was really helpful for us to look at the B Impact indicators to make those policies official for our team."
> -- Cameron Crake of Raven + Lily

### Assess Your Supply Chain

If you are not already considering the impact of your supply chain, implement a simple questionnaire for each of your suppliers to get a snapshot of their policies and inputs. Then create a formalised purchasing policy outlining minimum requirements for vendors and suppliers.

> "We knew we'd have affinity with B Corps, but what we didn't recognize was the number of people who would call us who aren't a B Corp, but are choosing us because we're a B Corp."
> -- Denise Taschereau, CEO and Co-Founder at Fairware

### Track Your Impact Metrics

You will find it difficult to gain points if you do not track metrics such as energy use, employee attrition, or waste production. Take a strategic approach toward tracking impact metrics and internal key performance indicators. This gives you a clear image of your strengths and earns you points on the assessment.

Consider the difference between outputs and outcomes: KPIs often measure outputs, but the BIA rewards you for demonstrating meaningful outcomes. Think about setting targets and maintaining a business diary to track what you want to achieve.

### Adopt a Mission-Aligned Governance Structure

Meeting the legal requirement of B Corp Certification in advance can earn you between 5 and 7.5 points. If none of the specified legal options are available to you, you can still earn 2.5 points by signing the B Corp Agreement. See the legal requirements page for full details.

> "For our board and our shareholders to see that yes we were profitable, but in addition to that, we made a difference in tens of thousands of lives, we changed the world for the better, I think those are just as important."
> -- David Young, CEO of Participate
