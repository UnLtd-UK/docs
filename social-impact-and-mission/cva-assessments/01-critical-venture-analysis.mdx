## What Is a Critical Venture Analysis?

A Critical Venture Analysis (CVA) is a structured due diligence framework developed by UnLtd for assessing social ventures applying for funding or support. The CVA brings together information from the entrepreneur's application form, financial model, and due diligence meeting into a single assessment document.

The CVA serves two purposes: it helps you work with a social entrepreneur to identify key challenges and optimise the support you can offer, and it provides a structured write-up for assessment panels.

## Venture Overview

Before diving into detailed assessment, capture the fundamentals of the venture:

| Area | What to Record |
| --- | --- |
| **Venture name** | Legal name and trading name |
| **Entrepreneur name** | Lead founder or applicant |
| **Legal structure** | CLS, CLG, CIC, charity, etc. |
| **Operating since** | Date of incorporation or trading start |
| **Turnover** | Last 12 months revenue |
| **Employee count** | Full-time and part-time staff |
| **Beneficiaries reached** | Last 12 months |
| **Investment requirements** | Amount and type of funding sought |
| **Geographical spread** | Where the venture operates |

## Assessing Growth Plans

When evaluating a venture's plans for growth, consider these key questions:

- What is the vision of scale, and is it appropriate for the market?
- What pace of scaling will the venture adopt, and why is this appropriate?
- Are there crucial pieces missing that need to be in place to achieve this vision?
- Is the sales and cash flow forecast sensible and sustainable? Is the venture reliant on just one or two large clients?
- Is there a clear runway for launching new products, services, or markets, and is the development budgeted in projections?
- What are the main risks in the growth plan?
- Have clear and achievable milestones for operational and organisational growth been presented?
- To what extent is financial growth clearly tied to scaling social impact?

## Evaluating the Business Model

The business model assessment covers both the commercial proposition and performance to date:

### Commercial Viability

- Is the business model documented and proven?
- What are the major risks, and how are they being mitigated?
- Are there barriers to entry or a clear competitive advantage?
- Does the applicant understand the competitive landscape in depth?
- Does all relevant intellectual property reside cleanly with the venture?
- Is the model truly scalable? Do margins increase as turnover grows?

### Sales Performance

- Is there clear evidence of a track record of selling sustainably (at a profit, with rising sales)?
- Are sales hitting previous forecasts and showing strong growth? Compare previous forecasts with actuals.
- Is income diverse, but not too diverse?
- How does the entrepreneur manage the sales pipeline? Do they have a clear view of where each deal sits in their sales cycle?

### Financial Health

- Is the balance sheet healthy? Is there existing debt?
- Comment on any significant information in the balance sheet.
- **Creditors:** Does the venture have liquid assets to pay outstanding amounts? For short-term liabilities (trade creditors), can they pay within 30 days? For long-term liabilities (bank loans, hire purchase), can they make monthly payments as they fall due?
- **Debtors:** If debtors represent more than 60 days of sales, question the venture's ability to collect payments. Are there disputes, customer financial difficulties, or dissatisfaction?

## Assessing Social Impact

Social impact assessment is central to evaluating any social venture:

- Is the social impact model (Theory of Change) documented, realistic, and impactful?
- Are operational and measurement process plans clear and realistic for achieving social impact?
- Is there a social mission lock in place? Is this appropriate for the business?
- How does the venture currently collect impact data? Is the approach appropriate?
- Is there a positive trend on impact metrics? Is the venture achieving its social impact targets?
- Is the Theory of Change being executed as intended? Is social impact being delivered as anticipated?
- To what extent is the financial growth of the organisation clearly tied to scaling social impact?

## Evaluating the People

### The Entrepreneur

- Is the chosen growth plan realistic given the team's skills and track record?
- What are the key areas where the entrepreneur faces challenges? Where are the skill gaps?
- Is the entrepreneur focused on and committed to delivering the plan, while remaining flexible and responsive to feedback?
- Has the entrepreneur demonstrated sufficient financial literacy to engage with investors?
- Can the entrepreneur absorb support? What other programmes are they on?

### The Team

- Is the management team balanced and robust? Can it scale?
- Are all core operational areas covered (design, development, distribution, finance, sales)?
- Are there skill gaps? Does the management recognise these gaps and show willingness to fill them?
- How much are key management getting paid? If salaries are low, what are the implications of moving to realistic levels? If high, will stakeholders react positively?

### The Board and Partners

- What is the quality of the board or advisers? How active are they?
- Is there evidence of selecting board members with diverse experience and experience of growing organisations?
- If partner organisations are required for routes to market or delivery, are partnerships in place? Are partner organisations sufficiently strong, credible, and scalable?
- Does the organisation have additional external supporters, such as accelerator programmes?

## Assessing the Growth Capital

For ventures seeking investment, evaluate the capital requirements:

- Are all existing shareholdings and loans documented?
- Is the amount and timing of investment sought sufficient to finance the growth?
- Can the business service the investment sought?
- Is the most appropriate type of finance being sought?
- Is it realistic to help secure this finance within 12 months?

## Programme Engagement

When assessing whether a venture is a good fit for an accelerator or support programme, consider:

- Is the entrepreneur working full-time on the venture? If not, is there a full-time team member committed to the programme?
- Do they have a partner or co-founder who would also be involved? (Having two people from the organisation often leads to better engagement.)
- Can they engage with the full programme duration? Do they have the capacity?
- Are they on any other programmes or facing key events that could limit engagement?
- Can they prioritise tasks and manage multiple responsibilities simultaneously?
- Are they willing to travel for meetings, training, and events?

## Document Checklist

Ensure you have reviewed or secured the following documentation:

- The venture's business plan and executive summary
- Three years of historic accounts, profit and loss, and balance sheet (or whatever period is available)
- Management accounts since the last audited or certified accounts
- Projections including profit and loss, balance sheet, and cash flow for at least three years
- Capitalisation table (for organisations that have issued shares) -- if unavailable, gather as much detail on shareholdings as possible
- Personal statement of net worth

## Writing Your Assessment

When completing your CVA, keep these principles in mind:

- **Focus on critical challenges:** You do not need to answer every question or cover every section. Focus on genuine concerns, risks, and critical challenges you have identified.
- **Explore, don't just list:** Rather than answering "yes" or "no," explore what the ideal situation would be, how far the venture is from that ideal, and why.
- **Include mitigations:** Wherever possible, explain what actions you have discussed and planned with the entrepreneur to address the risks you have highlighted.
- **Collaborate with the entrepreneur:** Complete the assessment in collaboration with the entrepreneur so they are aware of the challenges and fully bought in to the recommended support approach.
- **Keep it concise:** Aim for no more than four pages. The CVA should complement, never duplicate, the executive summary.
