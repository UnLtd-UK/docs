## Planning Your Data Collection

A data collection plan sets out what indicators you will track and how you will gather the data. For each indicator, answer six questions:

| Question | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **What** | The indicator you are tracking. Make it specific and clear, including any necessary calculations. |
| **Why** | Your goal for collecting this data. Specify whether it measures an output, outcome, or impact. |
| **How** | The methodology and tools -- surveys, interviews, tracking systems, indices. Consider the cost in time and labour. |
| **When** | The timing and frequency. Behaviour outcomes are best measured at least one month after your service, ideally three months. Gathering data too often may not show development and costs more. |
| **Who** | The person responsible. Include data collection in their role description and allocate sufficient time. |
| **Purpose** | How you will use the data -- which stakeholders, which reports, which decisions. |

Set SMART targets for each indicator: Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Realistic, and Time-limited.

## Before-and-After Evaluation

The most robust approach to measuring change is collecting data at two points:

- **Baseline (before)** -- before or as soon as a beneficiary starts using your service
- **Endline (after)** -- at least one month after the baseline, ideally three months

Using the same survey for both points allows you to measure the change for each individual and across your cohort. The same survey link or form can be used for both collections.

### Practical Methods

| Method | When to Use |
| --- | --- |
| **Online survey** | Share a link via email. Best when beneficiaries have reliable internet access. |
| **Manual recording** | Ask questions by phone or in person and record responses in an online survey or spreadsheet. Good for beneficiaries with limited digital access. |
| **Print-out** | Beneficiaries fill in printed forms, and you transfer responses to a spreadsheet. Useful for group settings. |

## Validated Measurement Scales

Using validated scales means your data is comparable to national benchmarks and other organisations. Here are the most commonly used scales in UK social impact measurement:

### Wellbeing

| Scale | Questions | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Short Warwick-Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale (SWEMWBS)** | 7 | Positively worded statements (e.g. "feeling optimistic about the future", "feeling useful", "dealing with problems well"). Widely used for cost-benefit analysis. |
| **ONS Personal Wellbeing (ONS4)** | 4 | Life satisfaction, happiness yesterday, anxiety yesterday, and feeling things in life are worthwhile. Comparable to national ONS data. |

### Confidence and Self-Esteem

| Scale | Questions | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale** | 10 | Statements measuring global self-worth and self-acceptance. |
| **General Self-Efficacy Scale** | Varies | Measures belief in your ability to handle situations and achieve goals. Useful for independence outcomes. |
| **GHQ-12 (single item)** | 1 | A single question on feeling of confidence, drawn from the General Health Questionnaire. |

### Loneliness

| Scale | Questions | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Campaign to End Loneliness** | 3 | Positively worded items. Less stigmatising for respondents. |
| **ONS/UCLA Loneliness** | 4 | Three UCLA items plus one self-rating question. Comparable to ONS national data. |

### Satisfaction

**Net Promoter Score (NPS)** -- a single question ("How likely are you to recommend this service?") scored 0-10. Useful for beneficiaries engaged three or more months.

## Example Survey Structures

### Health and Social Engagement

For ventures working with older people or health-related outcomes, consider questions covering:

- Self-rated health (Excellent to Poor)
- GP visits in the past 6 months
- Frequency of community engagement and leaving the house
- Frequency of contact with friends and family
- Loneliness and social isolation (using validated scales above)

### Sport and Community Development

The Sport for Development Coalition collective survey tool provides a comprehensive question set across:

- **Physical activity and wellbeing** -- life satisfaction, happiness, anxiety, self-rated health, physical activity frequency
- **Individual development** -- resilience, goal-setting, self-efficacy
- **Community development** -- loneliness, social mixing, belonging, trust, volunteering
- **Demographics** -- age, gender, ethnicity, religion, disability, postcode

### Youth Supplementary Questions

When working with young people, consider adding questions on:

- Aspirations and attitude to education
- Confidence related to employment prospects
- Truancy and school engagement

## Key Considerations

- **Which outcomes and scales** do you want to use? Choose scales that match your Theory of Change outcomes.
- **When will beneficiaries start?** Time your baseline collection to catch them before or at the very start of your service.
- **How many people** are in your survey group? Aim for at least 10 beneficiaries at any one time for meaningful data.
- **How will you gather data?** Choose a method your beneficiaries can realistically engage with.
- **Who will contact beneficiaries?** Assign clear responsibility within your team.
