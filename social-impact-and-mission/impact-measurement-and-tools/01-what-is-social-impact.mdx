## Defining Social Impact

Social impact is the meaningful change that an organisation creates through its actions. Several definitions are commonly used:

> "The net effect of an activity on the community and the well-being of individuals and families." -- Centre for Social Impact

> "A significant positive change that addressed a pressing social challenge." -- University of Michigan Business School

> "The social change that an organisation creates through its actions (i.e. the impact of its interventions)." -- UnLtd

For something to count as genuine social impact, it must meet three criteria:

1. **Actual change** -- it would not have happened without your work
2. **Significant change** -- it matters to the person, people, or community affected
3. **Lasting change** -- it has staying power beyond the immediate interaction

Social impact can span many domains: personal wellbeing, household dynamics, social and community life, economic circumstances, environmental conditions, health outcomes, and political engagement.

## Social Impact vs Social Value

These two concepts are related but distinct:

| Concept | Focus | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Social impact** | The change experienced by beneficiaries -- what actually shifts in their lives, communities, or environment | Understanding whether your work is making a difference |
| **Social value** | A framework for comparing the economic and fiscal case of interventions, combining the value of economic benefit with social benefits | Making the financial case for investment in social interventions |

Social impact is about what changes. Social value is about what that change is worth in economic terms.

## Why Measure Social Impact

Measuring your social impact serves both internal and external purposes:

**Internal management**
- See how effective you are at achieving your mission
- Use evidence to plan improvements and allocate resources
- Identify which activities generate the most impact per pound spent

**Proving your model**
- Communicate the value of what you do to stakeholders
- Use your impact as a marketing tool -- for many social enterprises, impact is the USP
- Inspire and motivate your staff and volunteers

**Opening funding opportunities**
- Different funders and social investors buy different outcomes
- Robust impact data strengthens applications and investor confidence
- Demonstrating measurable impact can unlock procurement contracts where social value is weighted

## The Social Impact Management Cycle

UnLtd's Social Impact Management Cycle (SIM-C) provides a structured approach to measuring and improving your impact. It consists of three stages:

### 1. Design

Set out what you will measure. This includes defining the problem you address, mapping your stakeholders, building your Theory of Change, and selecting Key Performance Indicators.

### 2. Do

Take the actions needed to measure your impact. Collect data using the methods and tools you have planned, at the frequency and timing you have committed to.

### 3. Learn and Adapt

The hardest but most significant stage. Analyse your data, draw insights, and adapt your ways of working based on what you learn. This relies on leadership, organisational culture, and a genuine willingness to change.

### Key Principles

When designing your cycle, keep three things in mind:

- **Proportional** -- you can scale the cycle up for a large organisation or down for testing a single idea
- **Integrating** -- combine each step with your core business activities rather than treating measurement as a separate workstream
- **Have a reason** -- do not start measuring until you know why you are doing it and what you will do with the data

## Three Concepts to Keep in Mind

As you begin measuring impact, three concepts will recur throughout your work:

**Deadweight** -- What would have happened anyway without your intervention. Your impact is only the change above and beyond what would have occurred naturally.

**Attribution** -- Who else or what else may have contributed to the benefit your beneficiary experienced. If a young person gets a job after your programme, how much of that achievement is because of your work versus their school, family, or other support?

**Benefit period** -- How long the benefit of your intervention lasts. Different interventions have different benefit periods, and understanding this is essential for accurate impact claims.
