## Communicating Your Impact

Measuring impact is only valuable if you use the data effectively. Impact reporting should serve specific audiences and drive specific actions.

### Principles for Good Reporting

- **Focus on outcomes, not just outputs** -- reporting that you ran 63 training events is less compelling than reporting that each disabled candidate who gained employment generated over £47,000 of social value
- **Use the low/medium/high touch framework** -- contextualise your beneficiary numbers by explaining the depth of engagement
- **Tell stories alongside statistics** -- qualitative case studies bring numbers to life and help readers understand what change looks like in practice
- **Be honest about attribution** -- acknowledge what other factors may have contributed to the outcomes you report
- **Report regularly** -- build an evidence base over time rather than relying on one-off evaluations

### Choosing Your Communication Approach

| Audience | What They Need | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Funders and investors** | Outcome data, cost-effectiveness, SROI figures | Annual impact report, dashboards, evaluation summaries |
| **Commissioners** | Evidence of outcomes against contract targets, unit cost savings | Structured monitoring reports |
| **Beneficiaries and communities** | Accessible stories of change, evidence that feedback is acted upon | Infographics, case studies, community presentations |
| **Internal team** | Operational insights, areas for improvement, progress against targets | Detailed Theory of Change reviews, data dashboards |
| **General public** | Simple, compelling narrative of impact | Social media, website content, annual review |

## Case Study: Evenbreak Social Impact Report

Evenbreak is a disability employment social enterprise that publishes an annual social impact report. Their 2023 report demonstrates several best practices:

### Structure

The report opens with headline social value (£47,000+ per disabled candidate gaining employment) and a summary of key achievements, then works through:

- **The problem** -- the 30% disability employment gap between disabled (50%) and non-disabled (80%) people in paid employment
- **Barriers and solutions** -- a clear table mapping each barrier to Evenbreak's specific response
- **Quantified outputs** -- 500,000 jobs advertised, 10,200 new candidates, 63 training events, 547 coaching sessions
- **SROI data** -- individual case studies with social value ranging from £1,229 to £94,410
- **ESG framework** -- environmental, social, and governance commitments
- **Awards and accreditations** -- external validation

### What Makes It Effective

- Leads with social value, not just activity numbers
- Maps the problem clearly before presenting solutions
- Includes both aggregate data and individual stories
- Reports on ESG alongside impact
- Uses third-party evaluation (ARK) for credibility
