## From Theory of Change to Measurement

Once you have designed your Theory of Change, the next step is devising ways to measure the different stages that lead to your desired impact. The aim is two-fold:

1. **Measure what change** you are achieving
2. **Understand how** your work is generating that change

Using your Theory of Change as a starting point, identify at least one indicator for each activity, output, outcome, and your overall impact.

## What to Measure

Your indicators should cover two dimensions of your work and two dimensions of your change:

### Measuring Your Work

- **Intensity** -- how often you engage with your users and the resources required for your activities
- **Delivery** -- how many users gain access to your activities and what is their feedback on the goods and services you provide

### Measuring Your Change

- **Depth** -- the extent to which your work has made a difference for individual beneficiaries
- **Breadth** -- how many users you have affected through your work

Covering all four dimensions gives you a complete picture. Reporting only breadth (number of people reached) without depth (how much changed) can be misleading, and vice versa.

## Methods and Tools

There are three main types of data you can use, and it is valuable to combine all three for triangulation:

### Quantitative Data

First-hand data gathered directly from your users through surveys, forms, registration systems, or tracking user behaviour and events. Quantitative data tells you how much and how many.

### Qualitative Data

Anecdotal evidence, stories, and case studies collected through interviews, focus groups, diaries, or beneficiary photography. Qualitative data tells you how and why change happens, and captures nuance that numbers miss.

### Secondary Data

Data created outside your organisation that helps estimate or contextualise impact. This includes demographic data, economic indices, deprivation scores, and published research about your sector or geography.

## Checking Your KPIs

Before finalising your indicators, check that each one is:

- **Specific and clear** -- precise and easy to understand, with a defined calculation method
- **Relevant** -- meaningful to the stakeholders you are reporting to
- **Logical** -- directly illustrates your work and supports the changes you are claiming
- **SMART** -- Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Realistic, and Time-limited

## Beyond Beneficiary Numbers

Headline beneficiary numbers can be misleading. A venture that screens 300 people briefly, assesses 30 in detail, and provides intensive support to 3 has very different levels of impact across those groups.

The CAN Invest beneficiary scale addresses this by classifying engagement intensity:

### The Low/Medium/High Touch Framework

| Level | Characteristics |
| --- | --- |
| **Low touch** | Brief, one-off, or passive interactions -- screenings, website visits, awareness campaigns |
| **Medium touch** | Repeated, more personal interactions -- phone calls, referrals, group sessions |
| **High touch** | Intensive, sustained engagement -- one-to-one coaching, long-term programmes, ongoing case management |

### Measuring Intensity

For each beneficiary group, capture:

- **How many** interactions they have with you
- **How often** those interactions take place
- **How long** each interaction lasts
- **What medium** is most commonly used (in-person, phone, online)

Use this data to produce an intensity score and classify your beneficiary model. Research from UnLtd found that almost half of surveyed entrepreneurs worked at a high-touch level, with an estimated 35,000 individuals benefiting in a high-touch way from the sample alone. Low-touch ventures tend to report higher total beneficiary numbers, but high-touch ventures typically deliver deeper change per person.

## Key Data Sources and Indices

When selecting or benchmarking your indicators, these external sources can provide context:

| Source | What It Provides |
| --- | --- |
| **Nomis** | Labour market and socio-economic data |
| **Index of Multiple Deprivation (IMD)** | Postcode-based deprivation indices |
| **Labour Market Survey (A01/A08)** | Up-to-date employment rates, including disability data |
| **Thriving Places Index** | Holistic local authority performance across wellbeing, sustainability, and equality |
| **ONS Wellbeing** | National wellbeing performance data |
| **IRIS+ by GIIN** | Standardised impact metrics catalogue for social investors |
| **Global Value Exchange** | Searchable database of social outcomes and their financial proxies |
