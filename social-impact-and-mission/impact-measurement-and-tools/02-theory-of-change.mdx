## What Is a Theory of Change?

A Theory of Change is a visualisation tool that maps out what impact your organisation aims to achieve and how it intends to get there. It explains the changes you hope to bring about for your beneficiaries (outcomes) and how your activities will create those changes.

A good Theory of Change should be:

- **Transparent** -- clear about the assumptions it rests on
- **Logical** -- each stage connects to the next in a credible causal chain
- **Accessible** -- understandable to people outside your organisation

It serves as both an internal strategy document and the foundation for gathering impact data, preparing reports, and communicating with stakeholders. A Theory of Change is never truly finished -- it should be updated as you learn from practice.

## The Logic Model Pyramid

The most common structure uses four levels, from the ground up:

| Level | Question | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Activities and Resources** | What do you need to have and do? | The inputs, staff time, materials, partnerships, and actions required to deliver your services |
| **Outputs** | What goods and services do you provide? | The direct deliverables -- workshops run, people served, products distributed |
| **Outcomes** | How do you change things for your users? | Changes in beneficiaries' knowledge, attitudes, behaviour, or circumstances |
| **Impact** | What is the one big change you want to see? | The overarching social change your work contributes to |

### Testing the Logic

Work through this chain to check whether your Theory of Change holds together:

1. With these **resources** and by doing these **activities**, I can provide these goods and services
2. Access to these goods and services will change users' **knowledge, attitudes, and practices**
3. These changes in practice will drive the desired **impact**
4. My organisation's impact is helping to resolve the **problem** I identified

If any link in this chain feels weak, that is where you need to gather more evidence or refine your approach.

## The One-Sentence Theory of Change

Kevin L. Brown developed a six-part formula for summarising an entire Theory of Change in a single line. It is not meant to be public-facing messaging -- it is a tool for strategic clarity:

> **Because** (WHY), **we work** (WHERE) **to help** (WHO) (WHAT) (HOW), **in order to** (WHEN).

### Examples

**Sabre Education:** Because 90% of a child's brain develops before age five **(WHY)**, we work in Ghana **(WHERE)** to help the early childhood education sector **(WHO)** provide the best possible early childhood education **(WHAT)** by partnering with government to implement play-based learning at scale **(HOW)**, in order to achieve SDG 4.2 by 2030 **(WHEN)**.

**Justice Defenders:** Because there can be no peace without justice and no justice without peace **(WHY)**, we work in Uganda, Kenya, and The Gambia **(WHERE)** to help defenseless communities **(WHO)** get a fair hearing in court **(WHAT)** through legal education, training, and practice **(HOW)**, in order to elevate one million people in conflict with the law within 10 years **(WHEN)**.

If you struggle to complete this sentence, you will likely struggle to pitch your venture and communicate effectively with funders.

## Separating Outcomes by Timeframe

A more detailed Theory of Change separates outcomes into three horizons:

| Timeframe | Description | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Short-term outcomes** | Immediate changes during or just after your intervention | Greater sense of routine; increased knowledge |
| **Medium-term outcomes** | Changes that develop over weeks or months | Improved self-confidence; changed behaviour |
| **Long-term outcomes** | Sustained changes that persist well beyond your intervention | Re-engagement with education or employment |

This separation helps you identify what to measure at different stages and makes your assumptions about how change unfolds more explicit.

## Problem Tree Analysis

Before building your Theory of Change, it can help to analyse the problem you are addressing in depth. Problem tree analysis provides a structured method for unpacking root causes.

### How It Works

1. Write the issue you want to analyse in the middle of a large sheet
2. Identify **immediate causes** -- the tangible, visible factors people witness. Write each on a separate card and place them below the issue
3. For each immediate cause, ask "But why does this happen?" and place the answers below
4. Continue asking "But why?" to uncover **underlying causes** and then **root causes**
5. Draw lines between causes to show how they relate

Use the PESTLE framework (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental) to prompt exploration of different types of causes at family, local, and national levels.

## Practical Tips

Advice gathered from practitioners and peer support:

- **Limit your outcomes** -- aim for your top 3, no more than 5
- **Test with external people** who know your sector or audience
- **Check with beneficiaries** -- does the beneficiary recognise the change you describe?
- **Check with your team** -- does the change fit with existing processes?
- **Make it useful** -- create a detailed version for internal improvement and a simpler version for funders and external communication
- **Learn from others** -- look at what similar organisations are measuring. Resources like [IRIS+ by GIIN](https://iris.thegiin.org/) and the [Global Value Exchange](https://www.socialvalueuk.org/resources/global-value-exchange/) can help

## Template

Use this structure to draft your Theory of Change:

| | Activities | Outputs | Short-term Outcomes | Medium-term Outcomes | Long-term Outcomes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Problem statement(s):** | | | | | |
| **Mission:** | | | | | |
| **Key assumptions:** | | | | | |
| **Impact risks:** | | | | | |

Record your key assumptions explicitly -- what needs to happen or be in place before you can achieve your goals? Note the impact risks: financial constraints, network gaps, data limitations, or capacity issues that could prevent you from delivering the change you intend.
