## Why Cost-Benefit Analysis Matters

Cost-benefit analysis (CBA) enables you to express your social impact in economic terms. By comparing the costs of your intervention against the monetary value of the outcomes it produces, you can demonstrate that investing in your work generates a positive return for society.

This is particularly useful when:

- Applying for funding from commissioners or investors who require economic evidence
- Making the case for preventative services that reduce costs elsewhere in the public system
- Comparing your approach against alternative interventions

## Unit Cost Databases

Unit cost databases provide standardised reference figures for the fiscal, economic, and social costs associated with public service outcomes. They allow you to calculate the economic value of changes your work creates by referencing established cost figures.

### Greater Manchester Unit Cost Database

The most widely used resource in the UK is the Unit Cost Database, originally developed by New Economy (now part of Greater Manchester Combined Authority). It covers costs across seven core themes:

| Theme | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Crime** | Anti-social behaviour, domestic violence, offending, substance misuse |
| **Education and Skills** | School attendance, exclusion, attainment, NEETs, adult skills |
| **Employment and Economy** | Unemployment, worklessness, welfare benefits |
| **Fire** | Fire incidents, false alarms, fire safety |
| **Health** | A&E attendance, GP visits, mental health, substance misuse treatment |
| **Housing** | Homelessness, temporary accommodation, housing support |
| **Social Services** | Children in need, looked-after children, adult social care |

The 2022 edition (v2.3.1) adds an **Environment** theme covering energy costs, carbon emissions, and environmental damage.

Each entry in the database provides:

- A unique cost code and description
- The unit of measurement (per incident, per person, per year)
- Which public body bears the cost
- Fiscal, economic, and social cost values (original and inflation-adjusted)
- The original academic or government source
- A RAG (Red/Amber/Green) data quality rating

### How to Use Unit Costs

1. Identify the outcomes your work creates (e.g. a person moving from unemployment to employment, or avoiding a hospital admission)
2. Find the corresponding unit cost in the database
3. Multiply by the number of outcomes you have evidenced
4. Sum the values to calculate the total economic benefit of your intervention
5. Compare against the cost of delivering your programme

## The Unified CBA Framework

For more rigorous analysis, the Unified CBA (UCBA) framework developed by Limestone Analytics provides a standardised specification for cost-benefit models. Its key features include:

- **Benefits, Costs, and Stakeholders (BC&S)** -- a table listing all costs, benefits, and transfers against financial and external stakeholders
- **Analytical Sensitivity** -- identifies critical assumptions and shows which elements are sensitive to them
- **Quantity and Value tables** -- one per benefit or cost, specifying narrative, timeframe, inputs, and calculations
- **Separation of perspectives** -- distinguishes between financial actors (direct contributors) and external actors (receiving indirect impacts)
- **Dashboard** -- reporting criteria including Net Present Value, return rates, and visual summaries

The framework enables peer review, teamwork, and implementation in any platform (Excel, R, or other tools).

## Social Return on Investment

Social Return on Investment (SROI) is a framework that assigns monetary values to social outcomes, allowing you to express impact as a ratio of value created per pound invested.

### Case Study: Evenbreak

Evenbreak, a disability employment social enterprise, commissioned an SROI evaluation. Researchers conducted in-depth interviews with ten disabled people who gained employment through Evenbreak, exploring outcomes across four themes: skills, employment, health and wellbeing, and financial circumstances.

**Key findings:**

- Social value per participant ranged from £1,229 to £94,410
- The average social value per disabled candidate gaining employment was **£47,091**
- Total SROI across the ten participants: **£470,910**

This kind of analysis demonstrates the scale of social value created and provides powerful evidence for funders, commissioners, and policymakers.

The Good Finance Outcomes Matrix is an online tool that helps social enterprises and charities understand and communicate their outcomes. It maps common social outcomes to measurement approaches and evidence standards.
