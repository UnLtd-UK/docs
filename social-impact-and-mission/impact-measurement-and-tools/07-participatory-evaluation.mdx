## Why Participatory Evaluation

Traditional surveys and questionnaires do not work well with every audience. Children, young people, and adults with limited literacy or confidence may engage more meaningfully through creative, hands-on approaches. Participatory evaluation uses arts-based and interactive methods to generate genuine evaluation data while also being an empowering experience for participants.

## The Evaluator's Cookbook

The Evaluator's Cookbook was developed by the National Evaluation of the Children's Fund and brings together participatory evaluation techniques tested with children and young people across the UK. The exercises are organised like a meal: **Starters** (warm-ups), **Main Courses** (substantive activities), and **Puddings** (closing exercises).

### Starter Exercises

Short warm-up activities to generate initial evaluation ideas and set a framework for the session.

| Exercise | Time | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gingerbread People** | 15-20 min | Participants draw or write inside a line drawing (person, school, home) what it is really like, and outside it how it appears to others |
| **Locks and Keys** | 30 min | Identify an objective (cloud), what stops you (padlock), and how to overcome it (key) |
| **Hands and Scales** | 30 min | Draw hand outlines or scales. On one side write positive aspects; on the other, negative |
| **Stretching the Point** | 15-30 min | Hold elastic and attach ends to cards representing different options, creating a human web of preferences |
| **Targets** | 15 min | Draw concentric circles. Place sticky notes closer to the centre for stronger feelings |

### Main Course Exercises

More substantial activities providing creative opportunities to explore issues in depth.

| Exercise | Time | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A Space of Our Own** | 30-45 min | Build 3D models from shoe boxes showing how participants would like a subject area to be |
| **CD Covers** | 30 min | Create collage CD covers showing how a subject appears to others (front) and personal experience (inside) |
| **Collages** | 30 min | Fold paper in half. One side shows the experience as it is; the other shows how they would like it to be |
| **Machines** | 15 min | Each participant creates a rhythmic phrase and movement, then all combine into a connected performance |
| **Masks** | 60-75 min | Create character masks. Say what the character would say, then what they really want to say |
| **Mirrors to the Future** | 60-90 min | Decorate mirror tiles with future aspirations. Hang cards below showing obstacles and strategies |
| **Puppets** | 60 min | Create card tube puppets representing characters and develop dialogues between them |
| **Wire Person** | 90 min | Build a 1-metre figure with doors in belly and head. Place images inside showing what makes them happy, healthy, or sad |

### Closing Exercises

Shorter activities to round off and evaluate sessions.

| Exercise | Time | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Journeys** | 15 min | Draw a path. Write aspirations at the end and place footprints showing how to get there |
| **Bubbles** | 15-30 min | Use speech bubbles (how others perceive) and thought bubbles (what you actually think) |
| **Thermoevaluator** | 15 min | Giant thermometer: low is cold and uninspiring, top is great and inspiring. Place comments at the appropriate level |
| **Webs** | 15-20 min | Hang decorated luggage tags on a web, placing most-wanted items near the centre |

### Presenting Findings

Creative presentation methods include:

- **Pockets** -- fabric or paper pockets filled with findings, photos, and audio recordings
- **Trees** -- cardboard or wire trees with branches representing research areas, decorated with leaf-shaped findings
- **Projections** -- project children's artwork onto surfaces or people wearing white overalls, with spoken highlights

## The Network Approach to Social Change

Beyond individual venture evaluation, measuring impact at a systems level requires thinking about networks and collaboration.

Network entrepreneurs approach social change differently from traditional nonprofit leaders. Rather than thinking of their organisation as a hub with partner spokes, they see themselves as a node within a broader constellation that revolves around shared missions and values.

### Putting the Network Approach Into Practice

- Analyse other people and organisations working on similar challenges across sectors and geographies
- Determine how other places and organisations have addressed similar problems
- Understand points of leverage for addressing the problem at a larger scale
- Connect with influencers in the space who can amplify collective impact
- Move from a scarcity mindset to an abundance approach that embraces larger, bolder solutions

This network perspective is important for impact measurement because it acknowledges that social change rarely comes from a single organisation. Effective evaluation should account for the contributions of multiple actors and the systems-level shifts that collective action can create.
