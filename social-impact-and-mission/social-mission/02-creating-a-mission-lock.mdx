## Tools for Creating Your Mission Lock

### Purposely

[Purposely](https://getpurpose.ly/) is an online tool that helps you create a social mission lock within a company limited by shares. The tool walks you through a step-by-step process to:

1. Define your social purpose statement
2. Draft your operational commitment
3. Choose your entrenchment method (supermajority clause, golden share, or redeemable golden share)
4. Generate bespoke articles of association ready for filing with Companies House

Purposely also provides a downloadable guide to help you finalise your articles before submission.

### B-Corp Certification

[B Corp certification](https://bcorporation.uk/) is another pathway that demonstrates your commitment to social purpose. Certified B Corporations amend their legal governing documents to require their board of directors to balance profit and purpose.

**What B-Corp provides:**
- Third-party validation of your social and environmental impact
- Public transparency through the B Impact Report on bcorporation.net
- Legal accountability through amended governing documents

**Important considerations:**
- The "lock" B-Corp provides is most similar to a social purpose statement in your articles -- it does not entrench provisions against shareholder resolutions in the way a golden share or supermajority clause does
- Charities cannot be B-Corps
- The B Impact Assessment is free to complete and provides a valuable audit of your business practices
- There is an annual certification fee based on your revenue
- Visit [bcorporation.uk](https://bcorporation.uk/) for detailed guidance

## Model Articles of Association

If you are setting up a purpose-driven company limited by shares, you can use model articles that incorporate social purpose provisions. A typical set of model articles for a social enterprise includes:

### Key Social Purpose Provisions

**Objects clause (Article 2):** Your articles should specify your company's social purpose as its primary object. For example: *"The objects of the company shall be to carry on business as a general commercial company and, in doing so, to promote [your social purpose]."*

**Directors' duties (Article 5):** Directors must act in the way they consider, in good faith, would be most likely to promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole, having regard to the company's objects and its statement of responsible business principles.

**Responsible business principles:** Your articles can include a set of principles that guide how your business operates. Common principles cover:

- **Long-term sustainable performance** -- Operating true to a purpose that serves society and generates fair returns
- **Honesty with customers and suppliers** -- Building lasting relationships, dealing honestly, paying promptly
- **Responsible employment** -- Treating everyone with dignity, providing fair pay, fostering innovation
- **Good citizenship** -- Considering each person affected by decisions, contributing fairly to society through taxes
- **Guardianship for future generations** -- Protecting the natural world and conserving finite resources

**Impact reporting:** Directors should prepare an annual impact report explaining how the company has promoted its objects and sought to have a material positive impact on society and the environment.

**Winding-up provisions:** If the company is wound up and all liabilities have been satisfied, a majority of any residual assets should be given to a not-for-profit institution with reasonably similar objects, rather than distributed to members.

### Other Standard Provisions

The model articles also cover standard company governance including:

- Directors' powers, delegation, and decision-making procedures
- Appointment and removal of directors, remuneration, and expenses
- Share issuance, transfers, and distributions
- General meeting procedures, voting, and proxy arrangements
- Administrative matters including record-keeping and insurance

## Working with Legal Support

When creating your mission lock, consider working with a legal adviser who understands social enterprise structures. UnLtd's Big Venture Challenge used a "social mission locking risk register" -- a structured process to help social entrepreneurs identify:

- Key risks of social mission drift specific to their venture
- The most appropriate type of mission lock given their circumstances
- Practical implications for investor relations and governance

This collaborative approach ensures your mission lock works with your commercial model rather than against it.
