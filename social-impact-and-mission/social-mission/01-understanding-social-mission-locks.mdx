## Why Mission Locks Matter

As your social venture grows, balancing the competing interests of beneficiaries, customers, employees, and investors can become increasingly challenging. Pressure to meet financial targets can become overwhelming and the primacy of your social mission can be forgotten -- this is known as **mission drift**.

Many social entrepreneurs seek to protect their venture's purpose by adopting a regulated legal structure, such as a charitable company or a Community Interest Company (CIC). Asset locks and limits on profit distribution are built into these structures. However, business models that require flexibility in investment options and the ability to scale rapidly often choose a Company Limited by Shares (CLS) structure for its greater flexibility and wider access to finance.

For CLS ventures, **mission locks** provide a mechanism to protect your social purpose while maintaining commercial flexibility.

## What Is a Mission Lock?

A mission lock is a mechanism to ensure the intended social purpose of your venture remains its primary objective in the long term. A well-designed mission lock contains two core elements:

### Social Purpose Statement

Your social purpose is inserted into the objects clause of your Articles of Association. This redefines success for your venture and creates duties on your directors to make decisions in line with the social purpose.

### Operational Commitment

A clause is included in your Articles of Association ensuring a substantial proportion of your venture's operations are used to further the social purpose. For example, a commitment to reinvest a percentage of profits into delivering social impact, or a requirement that a minimum proportion of customers are from your target beneficiary group.

## Types of Mission Locks

There are three broad categories of protection for social ventures:

### Charitable Structures

Charities (trusts, CIOs, charitable companies) have the strongest form of mission protection built into their legal structure. [Setting up a charity: structures](https://www.gov.uk/setting-up-charity/structures)

### Asset Locks (CICs)

Community Interest Companies have a statutory asset lock that limits the amount of profit that can be distributed to private shareholders and defines what happens to assets on closure. [Community Interest Companies: the asset lock](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/605418/14-1089-community-interest-companies-chapter-6-the-asset-lock.pdf)

### Social Mission Locks for Companies

For companies limited by guarantee or by shares, you can create a bespoke social mission lock. Once you have defined your social mission statement and operational commitment, you can entrench them using one of three methods:

| Method | How It Works | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Supermajority clause** | Any change to a company's articles normally requires a 75% majority. A supermajority clause raises this threshold for your mission lock provisions to 90% or even 100% of shareholders. | Ventures where shareholders are broadly aligned on social purpose |
| **Golden share** | A third party (usually a charity or social mission organisation) holds a special share. Their consent is required to change the mission lock provisions in your governing documents. | Ventures seeking external accountability and investor reassurance |
| **Redeemable golden share** | Like a golden share, but the share can be returned to the venture after a specified period or event. The specific terms are stated in your governing documents. | Ventures that want temporary external oversight with flexibility |

## The Investment Case for Mission Locks

UnLtd's Big Venture Challenge (BVC) programme has worked with 120 social ventures since 2013 and produced compelling evidence for mission locks:

| Metric | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Average increase in beneficiaries (all BVC ventures, 12 months post-support) | 698 |
| Average increase in beneficiaries (CLS ventures only) | 2,826 |
| Total external risk capital attracted by BVC ventures | £8,669,877 |
| Of which attracted by CLS ventures (70%) | £6,108,877 |
| Average raise per CLS venture | £160,000 |
| Average raise per non-CLS structure | £71,000 |

Social entrepreneurs report that mission locks reassure them their venture's legacy will remain impactful. They also make priorities clear when engaging potential investors -- many angel investors and social investors want evidence that social impact is embedded in the venture's DNA.

> "The danger is that investors or future Directors might be tempted to encourage the organisation to move away from its initial social mission. The mission lock provides a way of ensuring that doesn't happen." -- Social entrepreneur

## Five Questions to Ask When Establishing Your Mission Lock

1. **Is your social impact aligned with business growth?** Consider whether there is alignment between the impact you create and your business model. If there is a pivot risk, a mission lock can assure your primary purpose.

2. **Is there a risk of pricing out your beneficiaries?** If your beneficiaries are also your paying customers, consider committing to accessible pricing in your mission lock.

3. **Are owners, directors, or executives being paid above market rate?** A mission lock can help ensure remuneration does not result in unnecessary or excessive private gain.

4. **Do investors stand to gain excessively from exiting?** A mission lock can apply conditions related to investor exits, reducing the risk of excessive private gain.

5. **Are excessive net profits being realised?** Review how much is distributed to owners versus reinvested to further the social mission.

## Case Studies

### Oomph! (CLS)

Oomph! improves the quality of life of care home residents across the UK. They inserted a social purpose statement and an operational commitment requiring half of profits to be reinvested in the company or used to advance the social purpose. Their articles also state that assets cannot be sold at reduced cost unless it advances the social purpose. They raised £300,000 initially and a further £1.5 million to expand, now reaching over half a million older people in 1,200 care homes.

### Humanutopia (CLS)

Humanutopia runs life-changing courses for young people in schools. They specified a social purpose in their objects clause, inserted an operational commitment to reinvest a percentage of profits, and used a golden share to entrench the mission lock. They raised £175,000 and have now supported 240,000 students and 25,000 staff across the UK.

### School Space (CLS)

School Space helps schools generate funds by hiring out unused spaces. They inserted a social purpose statement and an operational commitment requiring at least 60% of properties to be state schools, entrenched by a supermajority clause requiring 90% of shareholding to agree to changes. They raised £320,000 during the BVC programme.
