## What Design Thinking Is and Why It Matters

Design thinking is a creative, human-centred approach to solving problems. Instead of jumping straight to solutions, you start by deeply understanding the people you are designing for, explore the real challenges they face, and then develop and test ideas that genuinely meet their needs.

For social entrepreneurs, design thinking is particularly powerful. The problems you tackle are often complex, involving multiple stakeholders, systemic barriers, and communities whose voices are not always heard. Design thinking gives you a structured way to navigate that complexity by putting people at the heart of every decision.

Through collaborative design techniques, you can:

- **Understand your users deeply** rather than relying on assumptions
- **Reframe challenges** to uncover the root causes of problems
- **Generate innovative ideas** by drawing on diverse perspectives and lived experience
- **Test and iterate quickly** to reduce risk and improve your solutions before investing heavily
- **Build shared understanding** across your team, stakeholders, and community

## The Double Diamond Framework

The Double Diamond, developed by the Design Council, provides a clear and visual description of the design process. It organises your work across four distinct phases, moving through two cycles of expanding your thinking (divergent thinking) and then narrowing it down (convergent thinking).

### Discover

The first half of the first diamond is about exploring your challenge collaboratively. You speak to and spend time with people to gather a broad range of perspectives. The goal is to understand rather than assume what the problem is. You uncover the root cause of a challenge and its associated problems and needs.

### Define

The second half of the first diamond helps you define the challenge in a new way. You analyse and refine your findings from the Discover phase, turning insights into early ideas and opportunity areas. Your goal is to articulate both the problem and the opportunity into a clear brief you can take forward.

### Develop

The first half of the second diamond encourages you to develop ideas and concepts that address your newly defined problem. You seek inspiration from elsewhere, co-design with different people, and test ideas at a small scale. The iterative cycle of "make, test, learn" at this stage helps improve viability and manage risk.

### Deliver

The second half of the second diamond involves further testing a preferred solution and making improvements to maximise its success as it is delivered. You refine, iterate, and prepare for implementation.

## Four Key Design Principles

The Design Council's Framework for Innovation identifies four principles that should guide your design work throughout every phase.

### 1. Be People Centred

Be inclusive and focus on people's lives, needs, and wants. This ensures your projects respond to real requirements and creates solutions that genuinely serve your community.

### 2. Communicate Visually and Inclusively

Convey concepts and ideas visually to accelerate shared understanding and build consensus among diverse stakeholder groups. Sketches, diagrams, and visual frameworks make abstract ideas tangible.

### 3. Collaborate and Co-Create

Include diverse perspectives, expertise, and lived experience in your design process. Collaboration generates unique insights and inspires truly original ideas that you would never arrive at alone.

### 4. Iterate, Iterate, Iterate

Test your insights and ideas throughout the design process. Each cycle of testing helps you mitigate risk, identify new opportunities, and move closer to solutions that meet your intended outcomes.

## Design Tools at a Glance

The following table maps each tool to the Double Diamond phase where it is most commonly used. However, most methods can be applied across multiple phases depending on your needs.

| Tool | Phase | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Challenge Statements / How Might We | Discover | Frame your challenge as an actionable question |
| Stakeholder Mapping | Discover | Identify everyone involved in or affected by your challenge |
| Understanding People / Extreme Users | Discover | Map the full range of users to design inclusively |
| Interviewing (In Context / Out of Context) | Discover | Gather insights directly from the people you are designing for |
| Be-the-User | Discover | Experience a product or service as your users do |
| Journaling / Fly on the Wall | Discover | Capture rich qualitative data over time through observation |
| Experience Diagrams | Define | Plot the highs and lows of a service experience |
| Pen Portraits / Personas | Define | Create fictional characters representing your key user types |
| Purpose Primer Design Square | Define | Align your team around a shared ambition, vision, and challenges |
| Creative Matrix | Develop | Generate focused ideas at the intersection of challenges and stakeholders |
| Alternative Worlds | Develop | Draw inspiration from how other organisations solve similar problems |
| Cover Story Mock-Up | Develop | Envision what success looks like five to ten years from now |
| Clustering and Theming | Develop | Organise ideas into meaningful groups and opportunity areas |
| Round Robin | Develop | Critique and evolve ideas collaboratively without dominant voices |
| Prototyping | Deliver | Test ideas through rough models, roleplay, storyboards, and mock-ups |
| Customer Journey Mapping | Deliver | Map step-by-step how a user interacts with your service |
| Rose, Thorn, Bud | Deliver | Quickly categorise feedback as positive, negative, or having potential |
| Importance/Difficulty Matrix | Deliver | Prioritise actions by plotting importance against difficulty |
| Dotmocracy | Deliver | Vote as a group to decide which ideas to take forward |

## External Resources

Two curated external toolkits are especially useful as you build your design practice:

### Hyper Island Toolbox

A resource kit for applying creative collaboration and unleashing potential in your team or organisation. Hyper Island organises its tools into five categories: energisers to get people moving, tools to unlock insight and innovation, tools for self-leadership and reflection, tools to drive change through action, and tools to build a strong team.

[Explore the Hyper Island Toolbox](https://toolbox.hyperisland.com/)

A step-by-step guide to putting the people you serve at the centre of your design process. IDEO organises its methods by question (for example, "How do I conduct an interview?") or by stage (Inspiration, Ideation, Implementation), making it easy to find exactly what you need.

[Explore the IDEO Design Kit](http://www.designkit.org/methods#filter)

## Tips for Using Design Tools Effectively

1. **Use Post-it notes generously.** They are easy to move around and the small size forces you to be concise. Write only one idea per Post-it to help you identify similarities and separate differences.
2. **Use Sharpie pens.** Bold markers are easy to read in photos and from a distance, which matters when you are working on wall-mounted frameworks.
3. **Collaborate.** All design methods are designed to be used in groups and with your community. Involve the people whose lives your work will affect.
4. **Be visual.** Where possible, use simple drawings to support your ideas and notes. Visuals accelerate understanding across diverse groups.
5. **Combine methods.** Design methods should not be used in isolation. Explore using a series of methods together to build momentum and deeper insight.
6. **Embrace iteration.** Your first attempt will not be perfect, and that is the point. Each cycle of testing and learning brings you closer to a solution that truly works.
