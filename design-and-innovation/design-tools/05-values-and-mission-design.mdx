## Why Values and Mission Matter

Your organisation's values and mission are more than words on a wall. They shape how your team makes decisions, how you communicate with the outside world, and how you hold yourselves accountable. When your values are clearly defined and genuinely shared, they become a compass that guides everything from hiring to strategic planning.

A well-crafted mission statement tells the world what you do and why it matters. A clear vision describes the change you want to see. And a strong set of values defines the qualities your people need to embody for the mission and vision to be achieved.

## Definitions: Mission, Vision, and Values

Before you begin designing these for your organisation, make sure everyone in the room shares a common understanding of the terms.

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Mission** | How you are going to contribute to the vision. Your mission describes your purpose and the work you do every day. |
| **Vision** | The change you want to see in the world. This is aspirational and forward-looking. |
| **Values** | The qualities that your organisation and its people need to demonstrate for the vision and mission to be achieved. |

These three elements work together. Your vision sets the direction, your mission describes the journey, and your values define how you travel.

## Planning Your Workshop

### Time and Format

Plan for a session of approximately **3 to 3.5 hours**, including a break in the middle. Arrive at 10:00 for a 10:30 start to allow for informal conversation and settling in.

### Aim

Create a set of company values and a mission statement that can be used both internally and externally.

### Materials Needed

- Large sticky notes (2 colours)
- 2 packs of 4 different-colour post-it notes
- 6 black marker pens
- Flipchart paper
- Masking tape
- A wall or board surface for clustering

## Step-by-Step Facilitation Guide

### Step 1: Introduction and Warm-Up (5 minutes)

Open the session by explaining the purpose of values and mission statements. Cover why they matter, how they will be used, and what the group will produce by the end of the day. Set expectations that the outputs from this session will be owned by the organisation and incorporated into all future work and publications.

### Step 2: Reviewing Famous Mission Statements (10 minutes)

Share examples of well-known mission statements and discuss what makes each one effective. Use these to spark thinking about what a good mission statement looks like.

| Organisation | Mission Statement |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Patagonia** | Build the best product, cause no unnecessary harm, use business to inspire and implement solutions to the environmental crisis. |
| **Amazon** | The Earth's most customer-centric company, where customers can find and discover anything they might want to buy online. |
| **Greenpeace** | To ensure the ability of the earth to nurture life in all its diversity. |
| **Facebook** | To give people the power to share and make the world more open and connected. |
| **Google** | To organise the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful. |

Ask the group: What do you notice about these statements? Which ones resonate and why? What patterns do you see?

### Step 3: Pitching Exercise (15 minutes)

This exercise helps you find the essence of what your organisation does. Each person takes a turn pitching the organisation to the rest of the group. The key is that the pitch gets progressively shorter, forcing you to distill your message down to what truly matters.

**Round 1:** Each person pitches for **3 minutes**. Listeners note down keywords and phrases that feel integral to the organisation's identity.

**Round 2:** The same pitch, but in **1.5 minutes**. Notice what gets kept and what gets cut.

**Round 3:** Just **30 seconds**. By now, only the most essential ideas survive.

After all rounds, collect the keywords and phrases that listeners noted. These recurring themes reveal what your team instinctively sees as most important.

### Step 4: Values Exploration (1 hour)

This is the core of the workshop, built around a central question and four supporting prompts.

#### Central Question: "When Were We Most [Your Organisation Name]?"

Ask each participant to write down moments, projects, or decisions where the organisation felt most like itself. Use post-it notes so each idea can be moved and grouped later.

#### Supporting Prompts

Work through these questions in sequence, each one building on the last:

1. **What makes these moments important to us?** Explore why these particular examples stand out.
2. **How much are we living these values right now?** Use dotmocracy (dot-voting) to rate how strongly each value is currently present in your work.
3. **How might we emphasise these values more?** Brainstorm practical ways to strengthen the values you have identified.
4. **What do we need for the future?** Look ahead at the challenges and opportunities coming your way, and consider which values will be essential going forward.

### Break (15 minutes)

### Step 5: Clustering Into Proto-Values (10 minutes)

Bring the group back together and begin clustering the post-it notes from the previous exercises. Group related ideas together and give each cluster a broad heading. These headings are your proto-values -- the raw material from which your final values will emerge.

### Step 6: Mission Statement Creation (25 minutes)

Use the **Hyper Island 20:20 exercise** to draft your mission statement collaboratively.

**How it works:**

1. Each person writes a draft mission statement individually (5 minutes).
2. Pair up and share your drafts. Combine the best elements of both into a single improved version (5 minutes).
3. Each pair joins another pair. Again, combine the best elements into one statement (5 minutes).
4. Continue combining until the whole group converges on a single mission statement (5 minutes).

This method ensures that every voice is heard while progressively refining the language until you reach a statement the whole team can support.

### Step 7: Values Review (20 minutes)

Re-cluster your ideas around the mission statement. Then review how other organisations have articulated their values. Study examples from social enterprises and companies whose values feel genuine and actionable rather than generic.

Consider how your organisation could map out its values in a way that is detailed, specific, and accountability-focused. For each value, ask: What does this look like in practice? How would we know if we were living it?

### Step 8: Creating a Values Tree (40 minutes)

A values tree is a visual tool that connects your mission statement to your values and makes them tangible.

**Structure:**

- **Roots:** Your mission statement forms the foundation.
- **Trunk:** Your core values grow from the mission.
- **Branches:** Each value is supported by specific characteristics and behaviours.
- **Leaves:** Examples of best practice that demonstrate each value in action.

This structure makes your values more robust, tangible, and accountable. Instead of abstract words, you end up with concrete descriptions of what each value looks like when it is lived.

### Close (20 minutes)

Use the final session to review everything created during the workshop. Test the mission statement and values against real scenarios: Does this help us make decisions? Does it feel authentic? Would we be proud to share this externally?

## Case Study: Find Your Voice Workshop Outcomes

The Find Your Voice organisation ran this workshop process and arrived at several powerful insights and emerging values.

### Key Themes from the Pitching Exercise

Through successive rounds of pitching, the following themes consistently emerged:
- "More than just a memory"
- Community-led and learner-led approaches
- Health and wellbeing as central concerns

### Insights from the Values Exploration

When the team explored moments where they felt most like themselves, several striking principles surfaced:

- **"If it's not sustainable, we move on"** -- and the confidence that the work will still be outstanding
- **Everything must have a social and/or financial value** -- no activity without purpose
- **Systemisation is important, but it must never be onerous** or hold back leaders
- **Responsible leadership and trust** -- trusting the people you work with
- **Facilitators are the channel** -- the organisation works through its facilitators
- **Clear communication and commitment** from the outset

### Proto-Values That Emerged

After clustering, the team identified six broad value areas:

1. **Learning** -- continuous growth and development at the heart of everything
2. **Community Comes First** -- the people you serve are always the priority
3. **Quality Creates Difference** -- high standards distinguish your work
4. **Sustainability** -- if it is not sustainable, move on
5. **Sharing Who We Are** -- transparency about identity and practice
6. **People and Staffing** -- investing in the right people and supporting them

These proto-values then formed the basis of the organisation's values tree, connecting the mission statement to specific behaviours and examples of best practice.
